## A. What Triangle Ehime actually is

The FY2026 primary-source reconstruction shows that the programme is not faithfully described as a scalar pilot subsidy.

Verified functions include:

- survey of local needs and implementation fields;
- national supplier recruitment (target 500+ applications in the FY2026 specification);
- pre-application consultation and matching;
- a requirement that main-route applicants secure at least two Ehime implementation sites;
- staged screening to roughly 20 new projects;
- project-specific publicly supported implementation contracts, with milestones, risk/progress management and implementation problem-solving;
- reporting of implementation data/IP/products/outcomes;
- at least three data-based study groups per project;
- accompaniment for implementation retention and horizontal deployment;
- support for constructing packaged offerings and promotional materials;
- separate prefectural subsidies enabling additional local adopters to introduce/customize previously validated solutions;
- networking, private-finance/employment matching, outside co-creation-hub links, local branch/R&D attraction and AI/digital-human-capital measures;
- multi-year follow-up of project retention.

The architecture is therefore a staged public production process:

`problem/field discovery -> supplier recruitment/matching -> scalability-oriented screening -> financed real-world implementation -> evaluation/packaging -> horizontal replication -> regional embedding/private continuation`.

## B. Functional architecture

Four primary blocks are verified:

1. **Project formation:** field discovery, supplier recruitment, pre-matching and multi-site screening.
2. **Implementation production:** real field access, finance, adaptation and project management.
3. **Translation to a replicable offering:** evaluation, data use, retention support and packaging.
4. **Market extension:** adopter-side horizontal-deployment support and broader regional embedding.

The appropriate provisional economic object is:

**`publicly orchestrated private technology-market formation`**.

That label remains provisional; it is not a contribution claim.

## C. Why bundle may matter

Fifteen bundle interactions and twenty research questions were generated.

Most candidate bundles are additive or reduce immediately to known mechanisms. The strongest non-additive candidate is A1 because its components have different and potentially opposing effects:

- a multi-site requirement can reduce entry while selecting/inducing transferability;
- implementation finance can expand entry and make costly validation feasible;
- later replication support can increase the return to reusable/scalable design.

Thus the interaction may work through **project composition**, not merely the number of supported projects.

Potential conjecture language for the next audit:

1. implementation finance can worsen project composition absent a scalability screen but improve it when tied to costly multi-site validation;
2. a stricter scalability requirement can be inefficient without cost sharing but desirable when public finance offsets the validation burden;
3. later replication support can improve welfare only when the earlier architecture generates sufficiently reusable offerings, and can otherwise amplify support for bespoke projects.

None is proved.

## D. Which interactions fail

The first kill rejects or deprioritizes:

- generic supplier search × finance: hard/soft policy complementarity;
- problem articulation × national recruitment: Q3/search-product-design prior art;
- implementation × study groups: generic learning;
- implementation × human capital: human-capital/agglomeration;
- external hub × local fields: platform/network integration;
- milestone finance × continuation: staged finance/real options/soft budgets;
- networking × local establishment: cluster/agglomeration/place-based attraction;
- pilot finance × diffusion subsidy without a scalability/composition stage: generic supply-push × demand-pull policy mix.

Q11 public-intermediary sunset and Q12 scarce implementation capacity remain constraints/backups, not promoted mechanisms.

## G. Literature frontier result

The architecture framing itself is not novel. Innovation-policy-mix research already studies combinations of instruments, and recent Japanese evidence explicitly examines local hard/soft support complementarity. PPI/lead-market and market-shaping research also studies public demand plus complementary strategies. Multi-site and implementation literatures already recognize external validity and “design for scale.”

The remaining possible white space is narrower: a **selection-mediated, sequenced architecture** where early scalability requirements endogenously reshape the supplier/project pool that later finance and replication support act upon.

The decisive next-stage reduction is:

> “A1 is just a standard screening contract plus a participation subsidy plus an additive downstream market-size benefit.”

If that sentence is accurate, A1 must be killed.

## H. Government / local-government essentiality

Not established.

A private accelerator or platform could reproduce several components. The architecture audit therefore does not manufacture publicness through a local-information parameter. The next audit must determine whether the economic result is sufficiently general IO/organizational economics or whether a minimal public-policy rationale is essential.

Local-government specificity is also not required for survival. The candidate should generalize to regional/national technology-deployment programmes, smart agriculture, health tech, climate tech, manufacturing extension and AI/digital adoption.
