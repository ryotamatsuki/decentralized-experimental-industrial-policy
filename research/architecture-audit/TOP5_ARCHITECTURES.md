## Rank 1 — A1 Scalability-by-Design Architecture

### Research question

> When can a public programme improve the composition of technology projects by combining multi-site scalability requirements, implementation finance and downstream replication support rather than subsidizing pilots or adoption in isolation?

### Minimal bundle

`multi-site requirement + implementation finance + downstream replication support`

### Sequence

`eligibility/screening -> financed multi-site implementation -> validated offering -> replication support/private adoption`

### Actors

Public programme; technology supplier; at least two initial adopters; later adopters.

### Strategic loop

`scalability requirement + finance -> supplier entry/design -> composition of validated projects -> downstream replication opportunity -> return to designing/entering for scalability`

### Composition effect

The architecture may select or induce reusable/multi-user solutions rather than bespoke one-off implementations. Finance and screening have opposing entry effects.

### Closest literature

Innovation policy mix; screening/mechanism design; product design; lead markets/PPI; multi-site external validity; scaling-pilot literature.

### Fatal reduction

> This is just a screening contract plus a subsidy plus a larger downstream market.

### Surviving difference

Potentially, no component is separable: finance changes who accepts the costly scalability requirement, while expected replication changes supplier design and therefore the project type generated by the screen.

### Welfare implication

A stricter scalability condition may reduce welfare without finance but improve welfare when paired with cost-sharing; more finance may increase low-scalability entry without the screen but improve scalable-project formation with it.

### Tractability

High enough for a later minimal architecture model if novelty survives; likely 3–4 strategic actor types and at most two endogenous supplier margins.

### Score

**84/100 — PREFERRED / POTENTIALLY DISTINCT / REQUIRES DEDICATED NOVELTY AUDIT**.

## Rank 2 — A2 Reference-Market-to-Private-Market Transition

### Research question

> When should government finance/orchestrate initial implementation at private users and then switch to supporting or allowing privately chosen follow-on transactions?

### Minimal bundle

`publicly financed private implementation + reference/packaging + private follow-on adoption`

### Sequence

`public initiation -> validated reference -> private downstream market`

### Actors

Government/programme; supplier; initial private implementer; later private adopter.

### Strategic loop

`public reference implementation -> commercial credibility/replicability -> private adoption -> supplier market viability -> lower need for public initiation`

### Composition effect

Selects suppliers/projects capable of transitioning from public support to private demand.

### Closest literature

PPI certification; lead markets; infant-industry support; reference customers.

### Fatal reduction

> This is just a lead-buyer procurement programme whose certification generates later private sales.

### Surviving difference

The prefecture need not be the final user/buyer and can finance implementation between private parties before later shifting support incidence to adopters. Whether this creates distinct economics is unresolved.

### Welfare implication

Temporary public initiation can be justified only if later private market formation exceeds displacement/rent costs; architecture may overstay after market formation.

### Score

**75/100 — BACKUP / HIGH PRIOR-ART RISK**.

## Rank 3 — A3 Multi-Site Field Access → Modular/Reusable Design

### Research question

> When does publicly assembled access to multiple heterogeneous implementation environments induce suppliers to design modular/reusable solutions rather than bespoke products, and when should public finance support that adaptation?

### Minimal bundle

`multi-site field access + funded adaptation`

### Sequence

`multiple real contexts -> adaptation/modular design -> larger transferable market`

### Actors

Programme; supplier; multiple initial adopters; later market.

### Strategic loop

`heterogeneous field access -> design choice -> breadth of fit -> downstream market value -> willingness to incur multi-site adaptation`

### Composition effect

Can attract solutions with higher fixed adaptation cost but wider subsequent applicability.

### Closest literature

Product design/search; standardization; lead users; testbeds; external validity.

### Fatal reduction

> This is just subsidized product design using multiple test environments.

### Surviving difference

Potentially the government supplies a scarce set of real environments as an input into product architecture rather than buying the product. Still highly exposed to product-design theory.

### Score

**73/100 — BACKUP / NOVELTY FRAGILE**.

## Rank 4 — A4 Implementation-to-Replicable-Offering Architecture

### Research question

> When should public implementation support be followed by codification/packaging assistance before subsidizing additional adopters?

### Minimal bundle

`real implementation + package construction + adopter diffusion support`

### Sequence

`bespoke implementation -> codification/package -> replication`

### Actors

Supplier; initial adopter; programme; later adopters.

### Strategic loop

`implementation experience -> package investment -> lower replication cost -> downstream adoption -> return to package investment`

### Composition effect

May separate service-heavy bespoke technologies from those that can become standardized offerings.

### Closest literature

Productization/product design, learning-by-doing, standardization, demand subsidies.

### Fatal reduction

> This is just a fixed productization investment followed by a demand subsidy.

### Surviving difference

Institutional sequence is unusually explicit, but economics is currently too close to known product-design/learning logic.

### Score

**70/100 — RETAINED FOR AUDIT CONTEXT, NOT PREFERRED**.

## Rank 5 — A5 Implementation-to-Regional-Embedding Architecture

### Research question

> Can a publicly orchestrated implementation pipeline and local-establishment/human-capital support jointly turn outside technology suppliers into persistent regional implementation capacity?

### Minimal bundle

`implementation pipeline + supplier local establishment support + local human-capital linkage`

### Sequence

`project opportunity -> establishment/training -> lower future implementation cost -> more local projects`

### Actors

Government; outside supplier; local adopters; workers/students.

### Strategic loop

Local transaction flow affects location and training investment; local capacity in turn affects future implementation economics.

### Composition effect

May convert footloose suppliers into locally embedded providers.

### Closest literature

Agglomeration, cluster policy, place-based FDI, human-capital externalities.

### Fatal reduction

> This is just subsidized agglomeration around an induced local market.

### Surviving difference

Weak. The architecture is institutionally important but current economic distance from mature place-based/agglomeration theory is insufficient.

### Score

**60/100 — NOT RECOMMENDED AS CORE**.
