# Policy-Mix Frontier

Date: 2026-08-28

## Audit question

Does the Triangle Ehime architecture create an economic interaction beyond the established proposition that combinations of innovation-policy instruments may be complementary?

## Frontier map

### Flanagan, Uyarra & Laranja (2011) — innovation-policy mix framing

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / FOUNDATIONAL`.

The policy-mix literature rejects analysis of instruments as isolated interventions and emphasizes interactions among objectives, instruments and policy processes. This makes any claim that “Triangle Ehime is novel because it uses many instruments” untenable.

### Borrás & Edquist (2013) — instrument choice / policy mix

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

Provides a conceptual rationale for combining instruments to address multiple innovation-system problems. It is a strong threat to generic bundle rationales but does not itself supply the candidate applicant-composition mechanism.

### Guerzoni & Raiteri (2015), Research Policy — demand- vs supply-side technology policies

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY CLOSE TO GENERIC COMPLEMENTARITY`.

Empirically shows that R&D subsidies/tax credits and innovative public procurement interact; evaluating one instrument in isolation can misstate its effect. This kills a simple “subsidy plus demand instrument works better” contribution.

### Characterising innovation policy mixes in innovation systems (Research Policy 2024)

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

Maps thousands of policy instruments and demonstrates the systemic complexity and institutional determinants of policy mixes. It strengthens the institutional legitimacy of architecture analysis but is not a formal strategic model.

### Cao et al. (2025) — procurement × R&D subsidy

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY CLOSE TO SIMPLE TWO-INSTRUMENT THEORY`.

Uses an evolutionary game to study joint procurement and R&D-subsidy support and policy balance. The paper itself notes that much prior work treats policy combinations as superposition and asks how to optimize the mix. It is a direct warning that a generic supply-push × demand-pull model is occupied.

### Okamuro & Nishimura (2026) — local hard × soft support

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE IN PLACE-BASED POLICY-MIX QUESTION / EMPIRICAL`.

Using a panel of 146 Japanese cities, the paper estimates hard support and soft instruments such as matching/mediation/consultation and reports evidence suggesting complementarity. This directly kills “local government should combine money and matching” as a research contribution.

### Guo et al. (2026), Technovation — balance in the policy mix

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / CURRENT FRONTIER`.

Studies firm-level response to the balance of technology-push and demand-pull instruments. Again, the existence of policy-mix interaction is not a white space.

## What remains potentially distinct

The strongest Triangle Ehime candidate does not ask whether instruments are complements in a reduced-form outcome equation. It asks whether an **earlier eligibility/screening architecture changes the endogenous project pool on which later finance and diffusion instruments operate**.

Candidate distinction:

`scalability requirement -> supplier entry/design and project composition -> implementation finance -> validated scalable pool -> downstream replication support`

The relevant non-additivity is therefore **selection-mediated**:

- finance can expand entry;
- a multi-site requirement can reduce entry but select or induce transferability;
- downstream replication changes the return to designing a solution that passes that screen.

The interaction could in principle imply that more generous finance worsens composition without a scalability rule but improves welfare with it, or that a stricter scalability requirement is harmful without cost-sharing but valuable with it.

These are conjectures only.

## Fatal prior-art risk for the next audit

The candidate must be killed if it can be written as:

> a standard screening mechanism where scalable type is exogenous + a transfer to satisfy participation + an additive downstream market-size benefit.

A dedicated architecture novelty audit must therefore search screening/mechanism-design, product-design, lead-market and staged-subsidy literatures using the exact composition/sequencing conjectures.
