# Market-Formation Frontier

Date: 2026-08-28

## Research boundary

Triangle Ehime may look like a government-created lead/reference market, but `public demand creates a market` and `government procurement certifies suppliers` are already occupied mechanisms. The architecture must be compared against those literatures before any novelty claim.

## Closest literature families

### Beise (2004) — lead markets

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE TO REGIONAL REFERENCE-MARKET LOGIC`.

Lead-market theory explains why innovations adopted/refined in one market can diffuse internationally or elsewhere. A claim that Ehime is valuable merely because local implementation creates a reference case is not novel.

### Public procurement / lead-market literature

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.

Public procurement can create demand, signal/accredit new technology and help form lead markets. Existing work explicitly discusses procurement as a cornerstone combined with complementary instruments. This kills a generic “public demand + complementary policies creates market” claim.

### Chiappinelli, Giuffrida & Spagnolo (2025), IJIO survey

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / BINDING PPI THREAT`.

The PPI frontier already covers demand pull, certification, supplier innovation incentives and policy interactions. Triangle Ehime cannot claim novelty from public demonstration followed by commercialization.

### Public clients creating lead markets / market shaping (2026)

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / VERY CLOSE IN ARCHITECTURE LANGUAGE`.

Recent work on Swedish public clients documents that public actors combine multiple market-shaping strategies and that lead-market advantages interact. This strengthens the relevance of architecture research while making “combinatory market shaping” itself insufficient as novelty.

### Market-formation / transition literature

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

Market-formation research identifies activities such as demand articulation, legitimation, standards, intermediary work and market-shaping. Much is conceptual/empirical rather than formal economics, so a formalization gap may exist, but that gap is not automatically a mechanism gap.

### Multi-site / external-validity design

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

Recent multi-site research formalizes how sites should be selected for generalization. This is a threat to claims that multiple implementation sites are valuable simply because they improve external validity. The Triangle candidate must instead connect multi-site implementation to supplier entry/design and downstream market formation.

### “Design for scale” pilot literature

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / IMPLEMENTATION LITERATURE`.

Implementation and public-health literature explicitly recommends designing pilots with future scale in mind. Therefore `pilots should be scalable` is not a contribution. The open economics question is whether financing, eligibility and later demand support jointly distort/discipline project composition.

## Government-as-orchestrator boundary

A potentially important institutional distinction from standard PPI is that the prefecture is not verified as the final technology purchaser in every project. The architecture can instead:

1. recruit a supplier;
2. connect it to private/local implementation sites;
3. publicly finance/govern initial implementation;
4. help convert the implementation into a replicable offering;
5. support additional private/local adopters later.

This is best described provisionally as **publicly orchestrated private technology-market formation**.

However, the distinction is not sufficient by itself. It must produce a different strategic or welfare result from a standard lead-buyer/procurement model.

## Most promising boundary

The market-formation candidate is strongest when linked to project composition rather than certification:

`multi-site/scalability eligibility -> composition/design of initial projects -> publicly financed implementation -> pool of validated offerings -> subsidized/unsupported private replication`

The next novelty audit should ask whether the architecture can generate different regimes:

- no/insufficient private market;
- publicly initiated scalable reference market;
- self-sustaining private replication;
- inefficient support of bespoke projects when the scalability screen is absent or weak.

These regimes are conjectural. No model has been constructed.

## Immediate kill rules

Kill any architecture candidate that reduces to:

- public procurement certification;
- a lead-market market-size effect;
- an infant-industry subsidy with temporary support;
- a platform network effect;
- a multi-site external-validity design with no supplier strategic response;
- a standard screening model plus separable downstream demand.
