# Stage 0 Reset — Institutional Re-Decomposition

Date: 2026-08-28

Purpose: describe the policy object before attaching theory labels. This file reuses verified institutional evidence but does not reuse killed C4/C6/C3 mechanisms.

## 1. Observable institutional sequence

A Triangle-Ehime-type programme can be described as the following sequence without presuming a welfare rationale.

1. A public sponsor announces problem domains / implementation opportunities.
2. Technology providers from inside or outside the region decide whether to approach or apply to the programme.
3. The sponsor and its intermediating team search for local firms, public facilities, users, or implementation fields that can receive the technology.
4. Candidate technologies, providers, and implementation partners are screened and paired.
5. A project-specific implementation plan is selected.
6. Public resources finance or coordinate some implementation activity.
7. Supplier and implementation partner perform adaptation, integration, training, testing, and operational use.
8. The project produces operational results, data, reports, products/services and possibly reusable implementation assets.
9. The sponsor evaluates the project.
10. A successful configuration may be introduced to additional local adopters.
11. The provider may commercialize the solution outside the original jurisdiction.
12. Public support ends, continues, changes instrument, or moves to a new project.

Items 2–12 are distinct decisions. Treating the programme only as a subsidy for one experiment hides most of the institutional production process.

## 2. Actors and what they control

| Actor | Observable or plausible control | Not established by Stage 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Local/subnational government | programme scope; project screening; implementation-field coordination; some funding; evaluation; horizontal-deployment support | inherent information superiority; persistent local rent; strategic cross-region free riding |
| Programme intermediary / implementation team | search; demand articulation; supplier/adopter contact; translation; project management | whether it is the efficient organizational form or government level |
| Technology supplier | whether to participate; technology and implementation know-how; commercialization decisions | exact private information; ownership of every data/IP asset |
| Local adopter / implementation partner | participation; operational access; complementary organizational inputs; follow-on adoption | representative status relative to the wider adopter population |
| Follower adopters | later adoption or non-adoption | exact informational dependence on the initial pilot |
| National / higher-level government | alternative finance, national programmes, diffusion infrastructure | whether it dominates local selection or search |

## 3. Institutionally verified margins from the existing ledger

The prior Stage 1 audit established the following as primary-source facts relevant to the reset:

- Ehime solicits private technology providers nationally, identifies local implementation sites, and screens projects.
- Ehime finances implementation trials through project-specific support.
- projects generate reports covering progress, data, outcomes, products/services and IP-related matters;
- the programme deliberately supports settlement / horizontal deployment inside the prefecture;
- successful projects may be connected to outward expansion;
- a later Ehime adopter can receive support to introduce a previously verified success model with the original provider;
- Tokyo and NEDO provide non-Ehime comparators for public field testing / demonstration / diffusion.

Therefore **selection, matching, implementation, evaluation and follow-on deployment are verified institutional margins**. They are not speculative theory inventions.

## 4. What remains unresolved and must not be smuggled into a new question

The existing evidence does **not** establish:

- strategic waiting/free riding by other prefectures;
- persistent regional first-mover or ecosystem rents;
- inherent local-government informational superiority over the national government;
- systematic duplicate/racing demonstrations across jurisdictions;
- universal public ownership of project IP/data;
- a particular political or bureaucratic objective function.

These may be future hypotheses only if separately motivated and audited.

## 5. Missing decisions exposed by the reset

The failed C4/C6/C3 branches focused mainly on occurrence, location, visible success and learning incidence. The institutional sequence above exposes a different set of decisions that remain candidate research objects:

1. **Search:** who discovers available technologies and implementation problems?
2. **Demand articulation:** who turns a local operational problem into a searchable/contestable challenge?
3. **Matching:** who pairs suppliers with adopters and on what information?
4. **Entry into the programme:** which suppliers and adopters choose to participate?
5. **Partner selection:** should a programme choose the most implementation-capable partner or the most representative one?
6. **Implementation production:** how are supplier effort, adopter effort and organizational adaptation combined?
7. **Intermediation capacity:** should government buy projects or build the capability to find/manage projects?
8. **Government capability accumulation:** does carrying out projects improve future public screening/implementation capacity?
9. **Supplier implementation capacity:** is scarce engineering/implementation attention reallocated rather than created by subsidies?
10. **Market engagement:** should public money be spent before procurement/adoption to discover and shape the supply market?
11. **Intermediary organizational form / level:** local, national, private or hybrid intermediary?
12. **Exit:** when should a public intermediary stop brokering once private market connections become viable?

## 6. Reset interpretation

The motivating policy may be better understood as **public innovation intermediation / implementation infrastructure** rather than necessarily as experimental industrial policy.

This is an open reframing, not a conclusion. The next question search must test whether intermediation, capability or implementation margins yield genuinely new economic feedbacks rather than simply relabeling search theory, matching, procurement, state capacity, adverse selection or organizational economics.
