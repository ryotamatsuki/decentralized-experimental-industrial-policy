# Finalist 1 — Q3 Multilevel Innovation Intermediation

## One-sentence question

> **How should technology-search and matching responsibilities be divided between local and national innovation intermediaries when the value of intermediation depends jointly on problem articulation on the adopter side and market thickness on the supplier side?**

## Economic object

Assignment of different parts of innovation-market intermediation across government levels, not assignment of a generic subsidy.

## Strategic loop

organizational assignment of search tasks → supplier/adopter participation and search reach → match set / relevance → value of local versus national intermediation → assignment.

## Missing margin

Fiscal-federalism models normally take the policy instrument and local public-good choice as given; innovation-intermediation studies describe functions but do not obviously solve the vertical assignment of *different sides of a two-sided search technology*.

## Closest threats

- fiscal federalism / local policy choice;
- Edler & Yeow demand–supply intermediation;
- regional public innovation intermediaries;
- search/matching platforms.

## Possible model/proposition distance

The candidate must not assume “local information good, central scale good.” It must derive why task assignment changes participation/search composition. A surviving contribution would likely be an organizational-form/regime comparison: fully local, fully central, or split intermediation.

## Immediate kill condition

If task-specific intermediation can be reduced to two exogenous cost/quality parameters, making the result a standard decentralization trade-off, kill.

## Minimal future route

Stage 2 must inspect fiscal-federalism models with endogenous information acquisition/search and platform/intermediary models with multiple principals/government levels. Only after survival may Stage 3 define the smallest two-sided intermediation game.

# Finalist 2 — Q19 Implementation Projects as State-Capacity Investment

## One-sentence question

> **When should a government undertake technology-implementation projects partly because conducting them builds its future capability to identify, screen, and govern supplier–adopter matches, and when does that learning rationale instead justify excessive public project activity?**

## Economic object

Endogenous productivity of government’s future innovation-policy production process.

## Strategic loop

current project allocation → intermediary/public organizational capability → future screening/matching quality → supplier/adopter participation and project composition → return to current capability-building project.

## Missing margin

Repeated-procurement theory learns seller type; dynamic-capability studies document public capability; the possible gap is *learning how to produce intermediation itself* and how that capability changes future market composition.

## Closest threats

- Gur, Macnamara & Saban sequential procurement learning;
- state-capacity accumulation;
- organizational learning/learning-by-doing;
- public-buyer dynamic capabilities;
- intermediary institutional entrepreneurship/capability building.

## Possible model/proposition distance

A valid theory must make government capability affect an economically distinct decision technology—e.g. which matches become feasible/enter—not simply reduce future cost or improve a signal precision mechanically.

## Immediate kill condition

If capability is only a stock increasing with cumulative projects or posterior precision about suppliers, kill as generic learning-by-doing/repeated procurement.

## Minimal future route

Stage 2 should search models of endogenous bureaucratic/state capacity, organizational learning in procurement, procurement intermediaries and endogenous policy implementation quality.

# Finalist 3 — Q23 Pre-Market Engagement versus Downstream Subsidy

## One-sentence question

> **When should public innovation policy spend resources on pre-market problem articulation and supplier engagement rather than subsidize the eventual technology transaction, when early engagement changes which suppliers enter and which solutions are offered?**

## Economic object

Instrument timing and market-formation margin before the project set is fixed.

## Strategic loop

public engagement → supplier information/entry/solution adaptation → set of bids/technologies → public value of eventual transaction → incentive to engage rather than subsidize.

## Missing margin

2026 Research Policy evidence shows engagement formats have heterogeneous effects and interactive learning matters. Many theory models start after bidders/projects are already present.

## Closest threats

- procurement entry and bid-preparation theory;
- costly information acquisition;
- innovation contests/challenge procurement;
- Edler–Yeow demand articulation;
- PPI market consultation.

## Possible model/proposition distance

A strong result would compare two policy instruments that act on different endogenous margins: market composition before matching versus transaction payoff after matching.

## Immediate kill condition

If market engagement is equivalent to an entry subsidy, disclosure signal, or exogenous increase in innovation quality, kill.

## Minimal future route

Stage 2 targeted search of procurement pre-market engagement, endogenous bidder entry, buyer-supplier communication, innovation contests and costly demand articulation.

# Finalist 4 — Q30 Public Innovation Intermediary Sunset

## Question

> **When should a publicly subsidized innovation intermediary withdraw because continued public brokerage deters the formation of self-sustaining private search and matching institutions?**

## Strategic loop

expected public persistence → private broker/supplier search investment → market thickness → social value of continued public brokerage → persistence.

## Missing margin

Public intermediary literature studies roles, adaptation and business models; the public/private boundary and endogenous sunset appears less central.

## Closest threats

public provision crowd-out; infant-industry protection; intermediary entry; innovation-system transition policy.

## Kill condition

If private-market maturity is exogenous or the public intermediary is just a subsidized private intermediary, kill.

## Why not TOP 3

Potentially strong feedback, but requires a dynamic market-formation structure and may drift away from the original implementation-policy question.

# Finalist 5 — Q11 Scarce Supplier Implementation Capacity

## Question

> **When do local technology-adoption subsidies merely redirect scarce supplier implementation capacity from other adopters, and when should policy instead expand the underlying implementation capacity?**

## Strategic loop

local subsidy → allocation of supplier engineering capacity → displacement/queues/prices → supplier capacity investment and other adopters’ decisions → additionality of local subsidy.

## Missing margin

Technology adoption is often modeled without an implementation-service bottleneck.

## Closest threats

capacity-constrained procurement; scarce scientist/R&D labor crowd-out; general equilibrium subsidy incidence.

## Kill condition

If implementation service is simply a fixed-supply factor, kill as standard crowd-out/incidence.

## Why fifth

Policy relevance is strong, but existing scarce-input theory is a major threat and the local-government role may be primarily redistributive.
