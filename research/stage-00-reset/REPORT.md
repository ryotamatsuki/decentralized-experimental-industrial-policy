## 3. Institution after removing theory labels

The verified policy process includes supplier recruitment, local implementation-field discovery, screening, pairing, project management, adaptation/integration, evidence production, evaluation, follow-on local deployment and possible outward commercialization.

This means that public actors are not merely writing subsidy checks. They are participating in a **technology-search, demand-articulation, matching and implementation-production process**. Stage 1 already verifies selection/matching/follow-on margins as institutional facts; claims about strategic free riding, persistent local rents or inherent local informational superiority remain unverified.

## 6. Frontier finding

The most interesting search result is a **thin-theory / thick-empirical-conceptual frontier around public innovation intermediation**.

Recent work shows:

- public innovation intermediaries are a widespread empirical phenomenon and their role in PPI remains underexplored in reviews;
- intermediaries manage technical/commercial/managerial knowledge and compensate for buyer/supplier capability shortfalls;
- early market engagement is underused and different engagement formats can have different or adverse innovation effects, with interactive learning important;
- public buyers' sensing/seizing/transforming capabilities affect innovation procurement and supply-market development.

At the same time, adjacent economic theories are dense: search/matching, platforms, fiscal federalism, state capacity, public provision, procurement learning and information acquisition. Thus a new paper would have to exploit a precise **interface feedback**, not merely introduce “an intermediary.”

## 8. Preferred TOP 3

### Preferred candidate for audit: Q3

> **How should technology-search and matching responsibilities be divided between local and national innovation intermediaries when the value of intermediation depends jointly on problem articulation on the adopter side and market thickness on the supplier side?**

Why it is worth a full audit:

- directly answers a level-of-government question without returning to generic subsidy decentralization;
- the policy object—demand articulation + supplier search + matching—is institutionally verified and international;
- recent intermediary literature is active but largely conceptual/empirical;
- the desired outcome is an organizational-form comparison rather than a simple monotone subsidy result.

Why it may still die:

- it may reduce immediately to local information versus central scale;
- it may be a standard two-sided matching/platform model;
- government may not be essential.

### Q19

> **When should a government undertake technology-implementation projects partly because conducting them builds its future capability to identify, screen, and govern supplier–adopter matches, and when does that learning rationale instead justify excessive public project activity?**

Potentially highest novelty, but the mechanism must differ from repeated procurement learning and generic organizational learning.

### Q23

> **When should public innovation policy spend resources on pre-market problem articulation and supplier engagement rather than subsidize the eventual technology transaction, when early engagement changes which suppliers enter and which solutions are offered?**

Most tractable of the TOP 3 and well aligned with a 2026 empirical frontier, but highly exposed to entry-cost and information-acquisition equivalence.

## 9. Why the reset is not yet a paper decision

The correct next step is **not Stage 4** and not a formal Stage 3 mechanism model. It is a focused frontier audit that compares the TOP 3 against the actual model structures in:

- two-sided search and intermediary theory;
- public/private innovation intermediation;
- state-capacity investment and organizational learning;
- repeated procurement learning;
- bidder entry / pre-market engagement;
- innovation contests / challenge procurement.
