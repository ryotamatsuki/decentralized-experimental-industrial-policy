## Hard-question-test notes

### Remove local government
Q7 and Q11 remain general economic problems if the government is removed; they survive only if the public programme changes allocation in a distinctive way. Q2/Q3/Q19/Q22/Q23/Q28/Q30 directly concern the organization of public intermediation and therefore retain a public actor naturally.

### Remove supplier
Every surviving question except a broad state-capacity interpretation becomes materially different; supplier-side entry/search/capacity is central.

### Remove experimentation
Most surviving questions continue to make sense. This is **not a defect**: the reset explicitly permits moving from experimentation to implementation/intermediation policy. The project should not retain “experimental” in the ultimate title unless later stages prove it is essential.

### Remove regions
Q3 loses its central assignment problem. Q30/Q4 remain public-versus-private organizational-boundary questions. Q19/Q22 can be national or local state-capacity questions and therefore need a later justification for subnational relevance.

### Mature-theory one-line mapping
Items were killed whenever a clean one-line mapping existed: adverse selection, soft budgets, sequential procurement learning, property rights, network coordination, standard real options, or moral hazard.

## First-kill conclusion

The post-reset viable territory is no longer primarily “how much subsidy for a demonstration?” It is provisionally:

> **who performs the search, matching, market-engagement and implementation-governance functions; how those functions affect supplier/adopter participation; and whether the capability to perform them should itself be a policy investment.**

This proposition is only a map of surviving questions. It is not yet a contribution claim.
