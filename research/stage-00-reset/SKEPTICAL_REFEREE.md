# Stage 0 Reset — Skeptical Referee

Date: 2026-08-28

Mandate: try to reject every finalist before another formal-model investment.

## Global attack

The reset may simply have moved from one crowded label (“experimentation”) to another (“innovation intermediation”). Search, matching, public capability, procurement and public/private organizational boundaries are mature economic fields. The existence of recent descriptive/empirical gaps does not imply theorem-level white space.

## Q3 — Multilevel Innovation Intermediation

### Attack 1: generic fiscal federalism
“Local actors know local problems; central actors access a larger market” is exactly the kind of information-versus-scale trade-off that decentralized public economics has studied for decades.

**Status:** `FATAL UNLESS STAGE 2 FINDS A DISTINCT TWO-SIDED PARTICIPATION FEEDBACK`.

### Attack 2: generic matching platform
If the local and national entities merely differ in matching cost or market thickness, this is standard intermediation/platform theory.

**Status:** `FATAL UNLESS TASK ASSIGNMENT CHANGES ENDOGENOUS ENTRY/SEARCH COMPOSITION IN A NONSTANDARD WAY`.

### Attack 3: why government?
Private technology brokers could perform the same function. Public involvement cannot be justified by saying local government has contacts.

**Status:** `OPEN`.

### Referee outcome
`SURVIVES STAGE 0 ONLY`. Worth a full frontier audit because the organizational-assignment question is crisp, but the novelty hurdle is high.

## Q19 — Projects as State-Capacity Investment

### Attack 1: repeated procurement learning
A public buyer learns supplier cost/quality by trading. Gur–Macnamara–Saban already formalize contractual and experimental learning.

**Status:** `SERIOUS`.

### Attack 2: generic learning-by-doing
If each project mechanically raises a capability stock, nothing is new.

**Status:** `FATAL IF THAT IS THE MODEL`.

### Attack 3: dynamic capabilities is not economic microfoundation
Empirical claims that public buyers need sensing/seizing/transforming capability do not themselves identify a strategic economic feedback.

**Status:** `SERIOUS`.

### Possible surviving core
The only defensible route is that the capability being accumulated is **the ability to create future matches / articulate searchable problems**, and suppliers/adopters strategically alter participation in response. This must be structurally different from learning seller type or lowering future administrative cost.

### Referee outcome
`SURVIVES STAGE 0; HIGHEST UPSIDE / HIGHEST MODELING RISK`.

## Q23 — Market Engagement versus Downstream Subsidy

### Attack 1: entry subsidy equivalence
Buyer engagement may simply reduce bid-entry cost.

**Status:** `FATAL IF EQUIVALENT`.

### Attack 2: costly information acquisition
If engagement only gives buyer/seller information, existing information-acquisition/communication theory likely contains the result.

**Status:** `SERIOUS`.

### Attack 3: empirical gap mistaken for theory gap
Berkhout et al. show engagement formats differ empirically; that does not imply formal theory is missing.

**Status:** `SERIOUS`.

### Possible surviving core
Engagement may jointly change demand articulation and supplier entry/solution choice **before the feasible project set exists**, while downstream subsidy only changes payoffs conditional on an existing project. A non-equivalence theorem between these instruments could be meaningful if not already in procurement/contest theory.

### Referee outcome
`SURVIVES STAGE 0; MOST TRACTABLE TOP-3 AUDIT`.

## Q30 — Public intermediary sunset

### Attack 1: infant industry
Temporary public brokerage until the private market matures sounds like infant-industry protection.

**Status:** `VERY SERIOUS`.

### Attack 2: public service crowd-out
Public provision deterring private provision is a mature literature.

**Status:** `VERY SERIOUS`.

### Possible surviving core
The public service would have to change a two-sided matching market’s endogenous thickness or learning technology in a way not captured by conventional public/private provision.

### Referee outcome
`RETAIN AS TOP-5, NOT PREFERRED TOP-3`.

## Q11 — Scarce implementation capacity

### Attack 1: inelastic input supply
The 2026 IMF innovation paper already shows R&D subsidies can crowd out private R&D through scarce scientists. Rename scientists as implementation engineers and the main insight survives unchanged.

**Status:** `NEAR-FATAL`.

### Attack 2: capacity-constrained procurement
Capacity restrictions and inefficient allocation are established.

**Status:** `SERIOUS`.

### Referee outcome
`TOP-5 COMPARATOR ONLY`. Do not promote unless a later institutional fact reveals a distinctive implementation-capacity contracting or investment margin.

# Attack on the TOP-3 as a set

The three preferred questions share a danger: all can be described as “government should invest in information/capability before paying for the final project.” If a future audit reveals they are only variants of one generic costly-information technology, the reset has failed and the project should be reframed more fundamentally.

They remain separate at Stage 0 because their endogenous objects differ:

- Q3: **vertical organizational assignment of intermediation tasks**;
- Q19: **endogenous public policy-production capability**;
- Q23: **instrument timing that changes market composition before contracting**.

# Referee recommendation

Do **not** model any of them yet. Conduct a full Stage 2-style frontier audit around the TOP 3 with explicit reduction tests against:

1. fiscal federalism with endogenous information acquisition;
2. two-sided search/platform intermediation;
3. public/private intermediary theory;
4. state-capacity accumulation and organizational learning;
5. repeated procurement learning;
6. bidder entry / pre-tender communication / procurement market engagement;
7. innovation contests and challenge-based procurement.

If no proposition-level gap remains, return `NO-GO — REFRAME PROJECT MORE FUNDAMENTALLY` rather than adding another feature.
