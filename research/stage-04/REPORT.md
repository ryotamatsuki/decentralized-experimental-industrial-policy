## 3. Minimal model

There are two symmetric jurisdictions, indexed `i in {1,2}`, one private technology supplier, and one launch/field trial.

Primitives:

- `C > 0`: real resource cost of conducting the launch trial.
- `V >= 0`: supplier's expected downstream commercialization value if the trial occurs.
- `F = C - V`: private financing gap. `F <= 0` means the supplier would launch without public support.
- `E >= 0`: information/adoption benefit received by each jurisdiction whenever a trial occurs, regardless of host.
- `H > 0`: additional host-only early-use/place benefit.
- `mu > 0`: marginal excess burden of one unit of public subsidy.
- `k = 1 + mu`.

Timing:

1. Governments simultaneously announce host-contingent subsidies `s_i >= 0`.
2. The supplier conducts the trial if `V + max{s_1,s_2} >= C`; otherwise it exits.
3. If a trial occurs, the supplier chooses the jurisdiction offering the highest subsidy; ties are split symmetrically.
4. Trial evidence supports downstream commercialization and later adoption.

Payoffs if jurisdiction `i` hosts:

- supplier: `V + s_i - C = s_i - F`;
- host government: `E + H - k s_i`;
- non-host government: `E`.

If no trial occurs, all payoffs are normalized to zero.

Transfers cancel in aggregate welfare except for the excess burden `mu s_i`.

## 4. Threshold notation

Define

- `T_H = H/k`,
- `T_L = (E+H)/k`,
- `T_S = (2E+H)/k`.

For `E > 0`,

`T_H < T_L < T_S`, with `T_L - T_H = T_S - T_L = E/k`.

These thresholds separate host-bidding incentives, unilateral local willingness to finance the experiment, and the social willingness to finance it.

## 5. Pure-strategy equilibrium

Away from threshold equalities:

### Region A: `F < T_H`

The unique pure-strategy equilibrium is

`s_1 = s_2 = T_H`.

The trial occurs. A government that slightly underbids loses and receives `E`; a government that slightly overbids wins but obtains less than `E`. At `T_H`, the host-only surplus is fully dissipated.

This region contains two economically distinct subregions:

- `F <= 0`: the trial is privately viable even with zero subsidy;
- `0 < F < T_H`: support is needed for the trial, but equilibrium bidding exceeds the minimum financing gap.

### Region B: `T_H < F < T_L`

There are two asymmetric pure equilibria:

- `(s_1,s_2) = (F,0)`,
- `(s_1,s_2) = (0,F)`.

Exactly one government covers the supplier's financing gap. The loser prefers free riding because `F > T_H`, but a government is still willing to volunteer if otherwise no trial occurs because `F < T_L`.

The reduced volunteer game also has a symmetric mixed equilibrium. If each government offers `F` with probability `q`,

`q = 2(E + H - kF) / (2E + H - kF)`.

The trial probability is

`P_trial = 4E(E + H - kF) / (2E + H - kF)^2`.

The pure equilibria implement the trial with probability one; the symmetric mixed equilibrium displays the standard volunteer-coordination inefficiency.

### Region C: `F > T_L`

No government is willing to cover the gap unilaterally. In undominated offers, the decentralized outcome is no trial.

Threshold equalities support additional weak/multiple equilibria and are recorded as boundary cases rather than used for strict propositions.

## 6. Welfare benchmark

If `F > 0`, a higher-level planner can induce the trial with the minimum subsidy `F`. Aggregate welfare from such a trial is

`W^N = 2E + H - kF`.

Thus a publicly financed trial is socially desirable iff

`F < T_S = (2E+H)/k`.

If `F <= 0`, the trial occurs privately and no subsidy is required. Laissez-faire welfare is

`W^LF = 2E + H - F`.

## 7. Candidate proposition results

### P1 — Non-additional local subsidies

**Status: PROVED, but not independently novel.**

If `F <= 0`, the supplier conducts the trial without public support. Nevertheless the decentralized hosting equilibrium has

`s_1 = s_2 = H/k > 0`.

Experimentation additionality is exactly zero. Relative to a zero-subsidy private launch, aggregate welfare falls by

`mu H/k > 0`.

The local host rent is competed away; the supplier receives the transfer and distortionary public finance creates the strict social loss.

If `mu = 0`, the transfer is globally welfare-neutral, so strict fiscal waste requires distortionary public finance.

### P2 — Partial over-subsidization when support is genuinely needed

**Status: PROVED.**

If `0 < F < T_H`, the trial requires support, but governments bid `H/k > F`. Supplier rent is

`R = H/k - F > 0`.

A targeted minimum subsidy `F` dominates decentralized bidding by

`W^N - W^D = mu(H/k - F) > 0`.

Because `F=C-V`, a one-unit rise in supplier commercialization value lowers the required gap one-for-one but does not lower the host-competition bid `H/k`; the rent component therefore rises one-for-one within this region.

### P3 — Productive local support / volunteer region

**Status: PROVED WITH EQUILIBRIUM MULTIPLICITY.**

If `T_H < F < T_L`, the two pure equilibria finance exactly `F` and create no supplier rent. Conditional on selecting a pure equilibrium, decentralized welfare equals the minimum-support planner welfare. The symmetric mixed equilibrium produces the trial with probability below one, so coordination can still generate underprovision.

### P4 — Under-experimentation from non-host informational benefits

**Status: PROVED, but close to standard externality logic.**

If

`T_L < F < T_S`,

then

`E + H - kF < 0`

for a unilateral local funder, while

`2E + H - kF > 0`

for the social planner. Decentralized governments do not finance the trial even though the trial is socially valuable.

The width of this strict under-experimentation interval is `E/k`. It collapses when `E=0`.

### P5 — Higher-level targeting

**Status: PROVED IN REGIONS, NOT AS A UNIVERSAL DOMINANCE RESULT.**

- `F <= 0`: no public support is required; restricting host subsidies avoids the excess burden `mu H/k`.
- `0 < F < T_H`: a minimum-gap instrument `F` dominates local bidding `H/k` by `mu(H/k-F)`.
- `T_H < F < T_L`: pure local volunteer equilibria attain the same aggregate welfare as minimum-gap support, although the mixed equilibrium creates coordination risk.
- `T_L < F < T_S`: higher-level financing can implement a welfare-positive trial that decentralized governments do not provide.
- `F >= T_S`: no trial/no subsidy is efficient.

### P6 — Mislocation

**Status: NOT GENERATED / NOT TESTED IN THE BASELINE.**

The symmetric baseline intentionally contains no host-specific information quality or cost heterogeneity. Mislocation cannot be claimed from this model.

## 8. Comparative statics and regime ordering

Since `F=C-V`, increasing supplier downstream commercialization value `V` moves the economy from high `F` to low `F`:

1. `F >= T_S`: no socially desirable trial;
2. `T_L < F < T_S`: socially valuable but decentralized under-experimentation;
3. `T_H < F < T_L`: productive local volunteer support;
4. `0 < F < T_H`: trial induced, but host competition overpays relative to the financing gap;
5. `F <= 0`: trial privately viable, yet positive local host subsidies persist with zero experimentation additionality.

Thus stronger private commercialization incentives reduce the social need for subsidy but do not necessarily reduce decentralized host bids.

## 9. Limiting / reduction tests

### `E -> 0`

`T_L -> T_H` and `T_S -> T_H`; the under-experimentation interval vanishes. Hosting competition survives. Therefore `E` is essential for the informational/free-riding result but not for the bidding-rent result.

### `H -> 0`

`T_H -> 0`; the positive hosting-bid/rent-shifting region vanishes. The model reduces toward a standard public-good/experimentation financing problem. Therefore `H` is essential for host competition.

### `V -> 0`

`F -> C > 0`; the privately viable zero-additionality regime cannot occur. Supplier downstream commercialization is essential for the cleanest non-additionality result.

### `mu -> 0`

Excess subsidy transfers cease to create aggregate welfare loss. Local rents can still be dissipated/transferred, but the strict social-waste claim disappears.

## 11. Prior-art re-check triggered by the algebra

The algebra sharpens the Stage 3 referee concern. Slattery (2025, JPE) explicitly models state/local subsidy bidding for mobile firms and finds that competition can transfer rents to firms; Mast (2020, AEJ: Applied) likewise shows local tax-break competition can raise exemptions with little effect on business location. These results make P1/P2, taken alone, unsuitable as a new theory contribution.

The baseline C4 contribution therefore cannot be claimed merely as `positive subsidy + zero additionality`.

## 12. Mechanism decomposition

The exact algebra reveals a separability problem:

- host competition sets the bidding cap `T_H = H/k`;
- information spillovers shift local/social financing thresholds through `E`;
- supplier commercialization shifts the private financing gap through `F=C-V`.

`E` does not enter the hosting bid `T_H`, and `V` enters the auction only through the participation gap `F`. The model stitches together two familiar wedges rather than yet making the information-producing nature of the trial alter the host-location competition itself.

This is the single diagnosed Stage 4 deficiency.

## 14. Success/failure assessment

The minimal model is not a mathematical failure. It yields clean threshold ordering, a private-versus-social experiment wedge, a non-additional local subsidy region, a productive-support region, and a higher-level coordination region.

However, Stage 4 `GO` requires a new strategic trade-off or welfare mechanism, not merely a combination of known channels. The baseline does not yet clear that bar because the experiment-information externality is host-invariant and drops out of the hosting auction.

The deficiency is narrow and economically interpretable, so `NO-GO` is too strong. One authorized hardening step can directly test whether C4 becomes experiment-specific.
