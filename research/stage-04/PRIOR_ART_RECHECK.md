## Slattery (2025)

Cailin Slattery, “Bidding for Firms: Subsidy Competition in the United States,” *Journal of Political Economy* 133(8), 2025, 2563–2614. DOI: `10.1086/735509`.

The paper models state and local governments competing for mobile firms through discretionary subsidies using an auction framework. A central result is that competition can transfer rents to firms, with limited welfare gains relative to banning subsidies.

**Stage 4 implication:** C4 Proposition P1/P2 — positive local support, competed-away local surplus, supplier rent, and weak location/additionality effects — cannot be claimed as novel by itself. The “launch trial” label does not distinguish C4 from this literature unless the information-producing nature of the trial changes the hosting competition.

Classification for P1/P2 alone: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1086/735509

## Mast (2020)

Evan Mast, “Race to the Bottom? Local Tax Break Competition and Business Location,” *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 12(1), 2020, 288–317. DOI: `10.1257/app.20170511`.

The paper studies competition among localities for mobile firms and finds that restricting local tax-break competition can materially reduce exemptions while changing equilibrium business locations relatively little.

**Stage 4 implication:** a result in which local governments spend fiscal resources without materially changing the real location/occurrence margin is already an established concern in local subsidy competition.

Classification relative to the C4 non-additionality result: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRONG EMPIRICAL-STRUCTURAL THREAT`.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20170511
