# C6 Targeted Novelty Re-Kill — Success-Biased Demonstration Design / Certification

Search date: 2026-08-28

## Executive verdict

**Candidate verdict: NO-GO for a new Stage 4 attempt.**

C6 asks whether a technology supplier's downstream certification/commercialization motive can bias demonstration choice toward visible success and away from socially valuable learning. The idea is economically coherent, but the targeted re-kill finds that the smallest admissible version is structurally contained by mature test-design / evidence-design / certification theory. Making the public sponsor strategically essential would require an additional success/reputation/political payoff or another instrument, which violates the minimality constraint for this re-entry.

## Candidate loop tested

`downstream certification value -> supplier chooses demonstration/test design -> evidence distribution / visible success -> downstream approval or commercialization -> private return`

Social welfare also values information quality / allocative learning.

The required novelty question is not whether this loop occurs in public demonstrations. It is whether the public-sponsor setting creates a proposition-level strategic feedback absent from existing test-design and certification models.

## Candidate proposition audit

### C6-P1
> Stronger downstream certification value shifts demonstration choice toward higher visible success probability but lower social informativeness.

**Status: KILLED AS MAIN NOVELTY.** Endogenous private test choice with private/planner informational divergence is already central to DeMarzo–Kremer–Skrzypacz and related evidence-design work. A special monotone success-vs-information technology would mostly specialize that theory.

### C6-P2
> A policy that raises demonstration success or quantity can reduce total information generated.

**Status: WEAK / MECHANICAL RISK.** Without a separate strategic sponsor margin, the result follows from imposing a technology in which visible success and informativeness move oppositely. That is not enough for a new mechanism.

### C6-P3
> The optimal public evaluation criterion differs from raw project success.

**Status: POLICY IMPLICATION, NOT MAIN THEORY NOVELTY.** The distinction between successful pilots and useful learning is institutionally important but not proposition-level distance from test-design/certification theory.

### C6-P4
> Government may optimally support experimentation but not reward success per se.

**Status: BENCHMARK/DERIVED POLICY CLAIM.** To make the sponsor's reward rule endogenous would add another instrument/mechanism beyond the minimal skeleton.

### C6-P5
> If supplier certification incentives are strong, additional public success incentives can reduce welfare.

**Status: NOT ADMISSIBLE AS BASELINE C6.** A public “success incentive” requires a sponsor payoff/instrument not present in the minimal model. Adding political credit, career concerns or an explicit success-contingent contract is a second substantive ingredient.

## Strategic-feedback test

The minimum C6 model can contain:

1. supplier chooses test/demonstration design;
2. evidence/outcome is realized;
3. downstream market/receiver updates and adopts/approves;
4. supplier earns commercialization value.

Removing the public sponsor leaves the core strategic loop intact. Therefore the sponsor is not strategically essential unless an additional sponsor-specific preference or contract is added.

That is fatal for C6 as the next model for **decentralized experimental industrial policy** under the current minimality constraint.

## Re-entry conclusion

C6 is a good institutional question and may motivate empirical/program-evaluation work, but it does not currently justify a standalone Stage 4 theory attempt. The smallest theoretically meaningful version is already inside endogenous test/certification design; the smallest version in which the subnational sponsor becomes strategic needs an additional mechanism.

**C6 = NO-GO in this Stage 3 re-entry.**
