# C3 Minimum Stage 4 Skeleton — Feasibility Only

This is a Stage 3 feasibility sketch, not a formal model.

## Minimum actors

1. **Technology supplier** — may privately finance a field trial because deployment produces reusable implementation knowledge and later commercialization value.
2. **Public sponsor/local government** — may finance the residual social value not internalized by the supplier.
3. **Follower market/jurisdiction** — represents later adopters receiving general evidence/learning spillovers.

The initial local adopter can be folded into the trial environment.

## Minimum learning primitive

Use one total deployment-learning process `L`, not two unrelated technologies.

Conceptually split its incidence:

- private/reusable share `alpha L`: retained by the supplier and capitalized in later deployments;
- public/general share `(1-alpha)L`: available to follower adopters/regions.

This is only a conceptual Stage 3 representation. No payoff function is fixed here.

## Candidate endogenous margin

The cleanest Stage 4 candidate would make **trial occurrence / supplier self-financing versus public residual support** the main margin.

Adding follower re-testing, disclosure, data sharing, bargaining or product-market competition would create separate mechanisms and is not part of the minimum skeleton.

## Strategic-feedback test

Under the minimum structure:

- a higher private share increases supplier continuation/private trial value;
- a higher public share increases the social value external to the supplier;
- the gap between the two determines the possible public-financing rationale.

But no opponent or follower strategic action changes because of the split. The supplier's response is primarily a participation/self-financing threshold response.

Therefore the mechanism fails the Stage 3 requirement that the learning structure create a distinct strategic feedback rather than a parameterized private/social-return wedge.

## Mechanical-result test

Any claimed non-monotonicity from varying both total learning and its appropriability share risks being constructed directly from two opposing components. Without an additional endogenous strategic margin, this is not a defensible new theorem mechanism.

## Feasibility judgment

- Players: 3 — `PASS`.
- Key endogenous choices: 1–2 — `PASS`.
- Mathematical tractability: high — `PASS`.
- One-process microfoundation rather than two arbitrary knowledge stocks: possible — `PASS`.
- Distinct strategic feedback: `FAIL`.
- Prior-art survival: `FAIL`.
- Why subnational government is theoretically necessary: weak; government mainly closes an appropriability gap — `FAIL`.

**Stage 4 value: LOW. Do not authorize C3 Stage 4 from this skeleton.**
