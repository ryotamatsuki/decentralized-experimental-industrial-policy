## 3. C6 result

### Core mechanism tested

Supplier downstream certification/commercialization incentives may distort demonstration/test design away from socially useful information.

### Strongest closest theory

1. DeMarzo, Kremer & Skrzypacz (2019): seller endogenously chooses costly information/certification; private and planner test designs differ.
2. Shishkin (2026): sender designs a quality test to obtain receiver acceptance and may disclose evidence strategically.
3. Weksler & Zik (2022): endogenous informative tests in signaling environments; test design changes strategic behavior and more information need not be preferred.
4. Perez-Richet & Skreta (2022): optimal test design with approval-motivated agent.
5. Gans (2021): firms endogenously choose biased versus unbiased experiments based on exploitation incentives.
6. Dai, Li & Chen (2021) and the 2025 PPI survey: public procurement already creates and studies certification effects.

### Why C6 fails

The smallest C6 game survives after deleting the public sponsor: supplier chooses evidence/test design, downstream receiver/market responds, supplier obtains commercialization value. Hence the public sponsor is not a strategically necessary player.

Adding a sponsor-specific taste for visible success, political credit, a success-contingent contract, or sponsor-designed certification standard could change the game, but each is an additional mechanism. The current re-entry explicitly forbids such a rescue.

## 4. C3 result

### Core mechanism tested

One field trial produces both supplier-reusable implementation learning and general/public learning useful to followers; this changes private self-financing versus public-support incentives.

### Strongest closest theory

1. Nemet (2012): subsidy rationale explicitly depends on whether firms can appropriate learning-by-doing benefits and whether learning spills over.
2. Irwin & Klenow (1994): own-firm learning is distinguished directly from learning obtained from other firms in an industrial-policy setting.
3. Foster & Rosenzweig (1995): own experience and neighbors' experience jointly determine technology adoption/profitability, with incomplete internalization.
4. Glachant & Ménière (2013): early adoption produces learning spillovers that lower later adoption costs; compares competitive supply, subsidy and private appropriation through patents.
5. Tong & Manea (2026 preprint): deployment-generated learning can be pooled or fragmented; learning architecture interacts with private deployment and product-market competition.
6. Demonstration literature documents simultaneous IP capture, learning curves and knowledge spillovers.

### Why C3 fails

The strongest minimal microfoundation is one learning process `L` split between a supplier-private/reusable share and a public/follower share. This is cleaner than two arbitrary knowledge stocks, but it exposes that the core result is an appropriability wedge:

- private share raises supplier expected return and self-financing;
- public share raises social value external to the supplier;
- government finances the residual gap.

No separate strategic response emerges without adding disclosure, data sharing, IP choice, bargaining, product-market competition, adaptation effort, or contracts. Those would be second mechanisms rather than a minimal C3 test.

### C3 score

`46/100`, with fatal prior-art override.

### C3 verdict

**NO-GO.**

## 5. Head-to-head conclusion

C6 is theoretically sharper because experiment/test design is genuinely endogenous, but exact prior art is closer and the government is not essential.

C3 fits demonstration industrial policy more naturally, but the mechanism is weaker: it mostly reallocates learning returns between private and external components and moves a financing threshold.

Neither satisfies the Stage 3 rule requiring a distinct strategic feedback, a minimal model, and plausible proposition-level distance from closest literature.

## 6. Mechanical-result audit

### C6

A maintained negative relation between visible-success probability and informativeness would directly build the result into the experiment technology. A deeper microfoundation would move C6 further into the existing test-design literature or require a new mechanism.

### C3

A public learning parameter plus a private learning parameter can mechanically create non-monotonicity. A single-learning-process/share formulation removes some arbitrariness but leaves the standard appropriability wedge.

Both therefore fail the anti-mechanical test.

## 7. International/general applicability

The rejections are not due to Japan-specificity. Both mechanisms are internationally general. Indeed, their broad applicability contributes to the prior-art problem: test design/certification and learning appropriability are already mature general economic questions.
