# C6 Minimum Stage 4 Skeleton — Feasibility Only

This file is a Stage 3 feasibility sketch. It is **not** a formal model and does not authorize Stage 4.

## Minimum strategic actors

1. **Technology supplier** — chooses the form of evidence-generating demonstration because successful/acceptable evidence affects downstream commercialization.
2. **Downstream receiver/market** — decides adoption/approval after observing the evidence permitted by the design.
3. **Public sponsor** — can finance or define the program environment, but under the re-entry restrictions has no political/reputational success payoff and no additional success-contingent contract.

The local adopter can be folded into the experimental environment rather than treated as an independent strategic player.

## Maximum four-step timing

1. Public sponsor announces an exogenous admissible support/program environment.
2. Supplier chooses one demonstration/test design `d`.
3. Outcome/evidence is generated under `d`.
4. Downstream market observes evidence, chooses adoption/approval, and supplier receives commercialization value.

## Central endogenous margin

`d` = demonstration/test design.

The intended economic content is that different designs change the distribution and decision value of evidence, rather than simply adding another subsidy margin.

## Why the provisional `p(d)` versus `I(d)` formulation is not enough

A reduced-form pair

- `p(d)` = probability of visible success;
- `I(d)` = social informativeness,

with an assumed opposite ranking can manufacture the desired conclusion mechanically. Stage 4 would need a primitive experiment technology from which both objects follow. But doing so does not by itself solve the novelty problem: endogenous private test choice and private/planner divergence are already central to the test-design literature.

## Sponsor-essentiality test

Remove the public sponsor while leaving the supplier and downstream market. The core loop survives:

`supplier test choice -> evidence -> downstream acceptance -> commercialization payoff`.

Therefore the sponsor is not a necessary strategic player in the admissible skeleton.

To make the sponsor essential would require at least one additional object such as:

- success-contingent public reward;
- sponsor reputation/political credit;
- public choice of test standard;
- procurement contract/test mandate;
- endogenous co-financing linked to evidence.

Each changes the strategic problem and is outside the one-margin C6 skeleton considered in this re-entry.

## Feasibility judgment

- Players: 2 essential strategic private/market actors + public benchmark — `PASS` for size.
- Key endogenous choices: 1 — `PASS`.
- Mathematical tractability: likely high — `PASS`.
- Distinctness from closest theory: `FAIL`.
- Why subnational government matters: `FAIL` without another sponsor-specific mechanism.

**Stage 4 value: LOW despite tractability. Do not authorize C6 Stage 4 from this skeleton.**
