# C3 Targeted Novelty Re-Kill — Region-Specific Fit × Reusable Supplier Learning

Search date: 2026-08-28

## Executive verdict

**Candidate verdict: NO-GO for a new Stage 4 attempt.**

C3 separates learning produced by a field trial into public/general evidence and supplier-reusable implementation know-how. That distinction is institutionally plausible and empirically important, but the minimum admissible theory turns into a standard appropriability / learning-spillover wedge: the supplier internalizes one share of learning and society receives another. Without a second strategic margin, supplier learning changes the private trial participation/support threshold rather than creating a distinct strategic feedback.

## Candidate loop tested

`trial -> public evidence + supplier-reusable know-how -> supplier downstream private value -> self-financing incentive -> residual public-support need`

The key novelty question is whether the split between public and private learning does more than change the private-versus-social return to experimentation.

## Candidate proposition audit

### C3-P1
> Greater transferability of supplier implementation learning can increase private experimentation incentives even while increasing diffusion.

**Status: KILLED AS MAIN NOVELTY.** Private reusable learning raises the supplier's continuation value; this is a standard private-learning/experience effect unless another strategic choice changes.

### C3-P2
> Public support need can fall when supplier-appropriable learning rises.

**Status: KILLED AS MAIN NOVELTY.** This is almost the direct policy logic emphasized by Nemet: when firms appropriate learning benefits, the subsidy rationale from learning weakens.

### C3-P3
> Public support may be non-monotone in total spillovers if spillovers differ in appropriability.

**Status: MECHANICAL-RISK / KILLED.** If one parameter increases social learning and another raises private appropriability, non-monotonicity can be obtained by parameter weighting rather than a new equilibrium feedback.

### C3-P4
> Intervention depends on who captures learning, not only how much learning occurs.

**Status: LITERATURE-VERIFIED BACKGROUND.** This is a foundational appropriability point rather than a new proposition.

### C3-P5
> A local government may inefficiently finance learning that is capitalized mainly into the supplier's national commercialization value.

**Status: INSTITUTIONALLY INTERESTING, NOT DISTINCT THEORY.** In a minimum model it becomes an incidence/participation calculation already threatened by the PPI/rent-transfer and appropriability literatures.

## One-process microfoundation test

The strongest admissible simplification is to let one deployment-learning process generate total learning `L`, with share `alpha` retained/reused privately by the supplier and share `1-alpha` observable/usable by followers.

This avoids adding two unrelated knowledge technologies, but it also exposes the core problem:

- increasing the private share raises supplier expected downstream value;
- increasing the public share raises non-supplier/social value;
- government support covers the difference between social and private returns.

Unless `alpha` changes another player's strategic action beyond participation/support, the conclusion is a private-versus-social-return wedge, not a new mechanism.

## What second mechanism would be required?

To make the learning split strategically richer would likely require at least one of:

- endogenous disclosure/data sharing;
- IP/secrecy choice;
- bargaining over learning ownership;
- downstream product-market competition;
- endogenous adaptation effort;
- contracts tying public support to knowledge sharing.

Each would be a second substantive mechanism and would move C3 into already mature adjacent literatures. They are not authorized as rescue in this re-entry.

## Re-entry conclusion

C3 is empirically credible and potentially useful as an institutional decomposition, but it does not pass the Stage 3 mechanism criterion. The minimum version is an appropriability/spillover model; a distinct strategic response requires another margin that the re-entry governance forbids adding merely to rescue the candidate.

**C3 = NO-GO in this Stage 3 re-entry.**
