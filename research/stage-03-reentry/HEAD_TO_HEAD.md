## Why C6 does not win despite a sharper endogenous choice

C6 has the more interesting choice variable: experiment design. But precisely that margin is occupied by high-level information/test-design theory. The public sponsor is not strategically necessary in the minimal admissible version. Making it necessary requires another sponsor-specific payoff or instrument, which violates this run's minimality discipline.

## Why C3 does not win despite stronger industrial-policy fit

C3 has the cleaner connection to technology demonstration and industrial policy. But its minimum mechanism is even more transparent: a supplier internalizes some learning while followers receive the rest. The supplier's main strategic response is a participation/self-financing threshold. The distinction changes incidence and subsidy need, but does not create a new strategic interaction without another margin.

## Fatal-prior-art override

Neither candidate satisfies all selection conditions:

- C6 fails prior-art survival and sponsor-essentiality.
- C3 fails strategic-feedback distinctness and prior-art survival.

Selecting either because it is the “less weak” survivor would violate the research-termination rule.
