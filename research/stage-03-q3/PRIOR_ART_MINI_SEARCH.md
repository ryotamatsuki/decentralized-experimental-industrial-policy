# Q3 Stage 3 — Targeted Prior-Art Mini-Search

Date: 2026-08-28

This search is mechanism-specific and supplements the Stage-2 Q3 frontier audit. Search failure is not used as novelty evidence.

## Evidence maturity labels

- `FULL / EXTENDED TEXT INSPECTED`: substantial article/manuscript text beyond an abstract was inspected.
- `PUBLISHED ABSTRACT / EXTENDED SYNOPSIS`: publisher abstract or detailed synopsis inspected.
- `WORKING-PAPER TEXT INSPECTED`: working-paper/manuscript text inspected.

## Core formal threats

### Kanoria & Saban (2021), *Management Science*, “Facilitating the Search for Partners on Matching Platforms”
Evidence: Stage-2 author-version full text previously inspected; published metadata rechecked.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.

Strategic agents on both sides incur costly match discovery. The platform controls aspects of the search environment and thereby changes equilibrium search behavior and welfare. This kills any Q3 candidate whose contribution is merely “intermediation architecture changes search behavior.”

### Teh (2022), *AEJ: Microeconomics*, “Platform Governance”
Evidence: published abstract and framework description inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`.

The platform chooses nonprice governance instruments affecting seller entry, information provision, recommendations, quality standards and search design. This is a broad containment threat to task-allocation candidates that simply split platform governance instruments across public bodies.

### Tan, Anderson & Parker (2020), *Information Systems Research*, “Platform Pricing and Investment to Drive Third-Party Value Creation in Two-Sided Networks”
Evidence: publisher synopsis inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M1/M4.

Platform investments can facilitate participation on both sides and be complementary. This makes split-side intermediation investments and hub-tipping/network-externality variants unsafe as new mechanisms.

### Brancaccio & Kang (2025), *Econometrica*, “Search Frictions and Product Design in the Municipal Bond Market”
Evidence: publisher full-text HTML/extended model discussion inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M2/M5.

Product design changes search frictions; the intermediary can exploit that channel; welfare consequences of standardization are explicitly studied. This is a direct threat to treating demand/problem design or standardization as a new search-friction mechanism.

### Zhong (2022), *Marketing Science*, “Platform Search Design: The Roles of Precision and Price”
Evidence: published abstract inspected.
Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / VERY CLOSE` for M2.

Search precision is an endogenous platform-design choice interacting with search incentives and competition.

### Cai, Gautier & Wolthoff (2017), *Journal of Economic Theory*, “Search Frictions, Competing Mechanisms and Optimal Market Segmentation”
Evidence: published/working-paper synopsis inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M5/M9.

The paper studies when heterogeneous agents should be pooled in one market or segmented into separate submarkets under search frictions. This is a major threat to architecture-induced sorting and bundling variants.

### Idem (working paper, 2023 version), “Coexistence of Centralized and Decentralized Markets”
Evidence: `WORKING-PAPER TEXT INSPECTED`; PDF text inspected. Screenshot call was attempted but the web cache returned an internal cache-miss error.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M7/M9 and relevant to M3.

Agents endogenously choose between a centralized marketplace and decentralized search, generating coexistence and endogenous segmentation. This kills venue-sorting/fallback mechanisms unless a genuinely different intermediation decision is central.

### Romanyuk & Smolin (2019), *AEJ: Microeconomics*, “Cream Skimming and Information Design in Matching Markets”
Evidence: published metadata/abstract plus working-paper text inspected; screenshot call attempted but cache miss prevented rendering.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M3.

A platform faces cream-skimming in a matching environment because sellers selectively reject requests; the platform's information design affects the composition of matches and welfare.

### Bolton, Santos & Scheinkman (2016), *Journal of Finance*, “Cream-Skimming in Financial Markets”
Evidence: publisher full-text page/extended abstract inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M3.

Informed intermediaries skim good assets into one venue, leaving a worse residual pool for another market; intermediary/information acquisition is endogenous and generically inefficient. This is the strongest generic reduction of M3's adverse-composition loop.

### Arbatskaya & Konishi (2012), *International Journal of Industrial Organization*, “Referrals in Search Markets”
Evidence: publisher extended abstract/model synopsis inspected.
Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP` for M3/M7.

Referrals arise endogenously in differentiated-product search, with firms choosing whether to refer consumers elsewhere.

### Rust & Hall (2003), *Journal of Political Economy*, “Middlemen versus Market Makers”
Evidence: publisher/NBER abstract and extended description inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for competing intermediation architectures.

Traders choose between middlemen and a market maker; intermediary entry changes search and welfare. This is a broad threat to “local intermediary versus national hub” as an organizational-form contribution.

### Garicano & Hubbard (2007), *Journal of Law and Economics*, and related knowledge-hierarchy literature
Evidence: published abstract inspected.
Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` for M10.

Hierarchies allocate problems according to knowledge/specialization, with market extent changing optimal organization. This makes routine-local / difficult-central escalation a known core mechanism.

### Baccara, Lee & Yariv (2023), *Journal of Economic Theory*, “Task Allocation and On-the-job Training”
Evidence: published abstract/working-paper synopsis inspected.
Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / CLOSE` for M10.

Centralized versus discretionary task assignment in a matching environment is studied formally; endogenous provider expertise adds another direct task-allocation benchmark.

## Innovation-intermediation evidence

### Edler & Yeow (2016), *Research Policy*
Demand–supply intermediation, adaptation, learning and procurement capability are directly documented. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

### Howells (2022), *R&D Management*
Innovation search is described as an extended iterative process in which intermediaries support multiple search stages. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

### Bangert, von Deimling & Eßig (2026), *Management Review Quarterly*
Evidence: full open-access HTML inspected.

The review/typology identifies distinct intermediary functions including market exploration, demand articulation, connecting/networking, mediation, testing and procurement support. It reinforces the institutional plausibility of splitting tasks, but does not itself establish formal-theory novelty.

## Search conclusion

The institutional literature is thinner in formal economics than the generic platform/search literature. However, each Stage-3-ready Q3 mechanism maps to an already mature formal mechanism family once stripped of innovation-policy labels. The remaining gap is therefore descriptive/organizational at this stage, not a sufficiently clean model/proposition gap for Stage 4.
