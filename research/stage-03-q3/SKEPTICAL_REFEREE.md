# Q3 Stage 3 — Skeptical Referee

Date: 2026-08-28

## Referee objective

Reject Q3 unless one mechanism survives after stripping away the institutional labels and comparing its strategic core to platform/search, intermediation, market-segmentation and organizational-economics theory.

## Attack 1 — “This is just a two-sided platform.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M1/M4/M6; MAJOR FOR ALL`.

Cross-side participation, network thickness, onboarding investment and multihoming are mature platform mechanisms. Merely splitting platform functions across local and national public bodies is not a new mechanism.

## Attack 2 — “This is just local information versus central scale.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL IF USED`.

Any model whose organizational ranking is driven by one local problem-knowledge parameter and one national supplier-pool parameter violates the Stage-2 contract.

## Attack 3 — “Problem articulation is just search/product design.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M2/M5`.

Once articulation is represented as changing how easily suppliers can assess/search a problem, the core maps to endogenous product/search design and standardization. Brancaccio–Kang and platform-search theory make the reduction too strong.

## Attack 4 — “Selective referral is just cream-skimming.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M3`.

The proposed residual-pool deterioration and entry response are economically analogous to existing cream-skimming and segmented-market models. Public gatekeeper labels do not change the central strategic logic.

## Attack 5 — “Local-first/national-fallback is ordinary sequential search.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M7`.

An outside option or fallback venue mechanically changes local search incentives. Without a separate mechanism there is no Q3-specific contribution.

## Attack 6 — “Easy local / hard national is a knowledge hierarchy.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M10`.

Problem escalation and specialization across hierarchical tiers are mature organizational-economics margins. Supplier market thickness can be appended, but that would be combining known blocks rather than discovering a new loop.

## Attack 7 — “The market-composition result is standard endogenous segmentation.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL FOR M9; MAJOR FOR M3/M5`.

Search-and-matching theory already studies pooling, segmentation, venue choice and heterogeneous market composition.

## Attack 8 — “Government is not essential.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL TO Q3 AS CURRENTLY FORMULATED`.

For every candidate, a private platform, broker, referral network or hierarchical firm can reproduce the core strategic response. Jurisdictional objectives can be added, but doing so alone creates a public-economics wrapper around an existing mechanism; adding a further public-specific friction would violate the one-mechanism discipline.

## Attack 9 — “The vertical task assignment does not itself create the economics.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL`.

Task assignment matters only by activating a known downstream mechanism: platform investment, search design, cream-skimming, segmentation, multihoming or hierarchy. No candidate was found in which the vertical assignment is itself the indispensable source of a new strategic feedback.

## Attack 10 — “This is interface novelty, not theory novelty.”

**Assessment:** `FATAL`.

The innovation-intermediary literature is institutionally rich and formal economics is comparatively sparse, but the apparent gap sits at the intersection of mature theories. An unmodeled institutional interface does not become a theory contribution merely because no paper uses the same vocabulary.

# Referee verdict

`REJECT / STAGE-3 NO-GO`.

The Q3 research question was worth the Stage-3 search because the institutional task division is real and internationally relevant. The search nevertheless fails the canonical criterion: no mechanism has both (i) a minimal implementable strategic loop and (ii) plausible model/proposition-level distance from the closest formal literature.
