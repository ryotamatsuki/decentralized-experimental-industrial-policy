# Q3 Stage 3 — TOP 3 Deep Dives

Date: 2026-08-28

The TOP 3 are the highest-scoring candidates *before* fatal prior-art override. All three fail the deep-dive gate.

## TOP 1 — M3 Selective referral / adverse national-pool composition

### One-sentence mechanism
Local intermediaries keep easy/high-fit cases and refer difficult cases to a shared national supplier hub; the degraded referral-pool composition reduces supplier participation, which makes referral less valuable and reinforces local retention.

### Why it initially looked promising
It creates the exact Q3 feedback requested by Stage 2:

`vertical routing architecture -> problem-pool composition -> supplier participation/thickness -> value of routing architecture`.

The effect is not mechanically a local-information-versus-central-scale comparison. It can also produce a sharp organizational implication: a split architecture may perform worse than full pooling because selective local routing endogenously destroys the national market it relies on.

### Strongest reduction
> **This is cream-skimming/adverse selection across intermediation venues with endogenous participation.**

### Closest threats
- Bolton, Santos & Scheinkman (2016): informed intermediation removes good assets from one market and worsens the residual pool, with endogenous information/intermediary activity and welfare effects.
- Romanyuk & Smolin (2019): cream-skimming in a matching market and platform response.
- Idem (2023 WP): agents endogenously sort between centralized and decentralized trading venues.
- Arbatskaya & Konishi (2012): endogenous referrals in search markets.
- Rust & Hall (2003): endogenous coexistence/competition between intermediation structures.

### Can the reduction be defeated with one minimal primitive?
No clean way was found. Making local public gatekeepers jurisdiction-specific changes the identity/objective of the selector but not the core adverse-composition logic. To obtain a distinct loop one would need an additional public-specific constraint, contracting friction, or nonappropriable supplier-investment channel. That would be a second mechanism.

### Verdict
`FATAL — KILL M3`.

## TOP 2 — M2 Demand legibility × supplier thickness

### One-sentence mechanism
Local articulation transforms tacit adopter problems into legible challenges; legibility changes supplier search/entry into a national pool, and supplier thickness changes adopter participation and the return to articulation.

### Why it initially looked promising
It focuses on a policy-production task—problem formulation—rather than generic subsidy or matching. The empirical PPI/intermediation literature makes this task highly credible.

### Strongest reduction
> **This is endogenous product/problem design changing search precision/frictions and platform participation, with a local producer of the design input.**

### Closest threats
- Brancaccio & Kang (2025): product design affects search frictions and intermediary rents; standardization policy is evaluated.
- Zhong (2022): platform search precision is endogenous and interacts with search incentives/competition.
- Kanoria & Saban (2021): platform search architecture changes strategic search.
- Teh (2022): platform governance covers seller entry, information, recommendations, quality standards and search design.
- demand-articulation literature: problem formulation and supplier responsiveness are already central institutional channels.

### Can the reduction be defeated with one minimal primitive?
Not without making the local intermediary's access to tacit needs a separate noncontractible-information technology and then adding a public/jurisdictional objective. At that point the contribution risks becoming exactly the prohibited local-information-plus-platform-design combination.

### Verdict
`FATAL — KILL M2`.

## TOP 3 — M10 Hierarchical escalation by problem difficulty

### One-sentence mechanism
Local intermediaries solve routine cases and escalate difficult cases to a national specialist/supplier layer; task composition affects the value and thickness of each layer and therefore the escalation threshold.

### Why it initially looked promising
It gives a natural reason for *different tasks* to sit at different government levels and could support a local/frontline–national/specialist organizational comparison.

### Strongest reduction
> **This is a knowledge/problem-solving hierarchy with endogenous task allocation, augmented by a matching market.**

### Closest threats
- Garicano/Hubbard hierarchy and knowledge-specialization literature.
- Baccara, Lee & Yariv (2023): centralized versus discretionary task allocation with endogenous provider expertise.
- Cai, Gautier & Wolthoff (2017): pooling versus segmentation under search frictions.
- Idem: coexistence of centralized and decentralized venues.

### Can the reduction be defeated with one minimal primitive?
No. Supplier-side participation can be appended, but that adds a known platform-thickness block to an already-known hierarchy block. No new interaction survived when both blocks were kept minimal.

### Verdict
`FATAL — KILL M10`.

# Deep-dive conclusion

The TOP 3 each have coherent economics and strong institutional interpretation. That is insufficient. Their minimal forms are already occupied by mature theory classes. Creating model/proposition-level distance would require a second mechanism precisely of the kind Stage 3 governance is designed to prevent from being added as a rescue.
