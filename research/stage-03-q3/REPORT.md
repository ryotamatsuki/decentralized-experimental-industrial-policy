## 2. Frozen Stage-2 question

> How should technology-search and matching responsibilities be divided between local and national innovation intermediaries when the value of intermediation depends jointly on problem articulation on the adopter side and market thickness on the supplier side?

The Stage-2 contract prohibited novelty claims based on innovation intermediation itself, demand–supply brokerage, local information, central scale, platform integration, generic search/matching frictions or generic fiscal federalism.

## 5. Why M3 fails

M3 was the strongest attempt to make vertical task assignment create endogenous market thickness rather than merely compare local knowledge with national scale.

Proposed loop:

`local routing -> national problem-pool composition -> supplier participation -> value of national routing -> local routing`.

But the minimal version is an adverse-selection/cream-skimming mechanism across intermediation venues. Bolton–Santos–Scheinkman show informed intermediation skimming good assets from one venue and degrading the residual pool; Romanyuk–Smolin place cream-skimming inside a matching market; Idem studies endogenous coexistence/segmentation between centralized and decentralized venues; Arbatskaya–Konishi study endogenous referrals.

Making the selectors local public intermediaries changes institutional interpretation but does not, by itself, produce a distinct strategic mechanism.

## 6. Why M2 fails

M2 made problem formulation the central task:

`problem articulation / legibility -> supplier search and entry -> market thickness -> adopter participation -> return to articulation`.

This is institutionally compelling, but its minimal formal core becomes endogenous product/search design. Brancaccio–Kang directly show product design changing search frictions and intermediary rents; platform-search and platform-governance theories make search precision, seller entry and information architecture endogenous. Treating local articulation as the producer of the design input does not create sufficient theory distance unless a second public/information mechanism is added.

## 7. Why M10 fails

M10 assigned routine problems to local intermediaries and difficult problems to a national specialist layer. The desired loop is coherent, but the easy-local/hard-central allocation is a standard knowledge-hierarchy/problem-solving structure. Garicano-style hierarchy theory and modern task-allocation/matching theory occupy the core mechanism. Adding supplier participation would combine it with a known platform-thickness block rather than generate a new primitive interaction.

## 8. Other candidates

- M1/M4 collapse to two-sided platform investments/network externalities.
- M5 collapses to standardization/search-friction/market-segmentation theory.
- M6 collapses to multihoming/platform competition.
- M7 collapses to sequential search with outside options.
- M8 becomes competition for scarce attention/search capacity and risks reviving C4-style competition logic.
- M9 becomes endogenous venue sorting/market segmentation.

## 9. Strongest frontier finding

The 2026 innovation-intermediation literature strengthens the institutional case that intermediaries perform distinct functions—market exploration, demand articulation, networking, mediation, testing and procurement support. The formal-economics problem is not that these tasks are unimportant; it is that, once a tractable strategic feedback is attached to them, the feedback belongs to a mature economics literature.

Thus the remaining “gap” is presently an **institutional/formalization gap**, not yet a defensible **mechanism/proposition gap**.

## 10. Government essentiality

Stage 2 left this unresolved. Stage 3 does not solve it.

Every candidate's central strategic response can be reproduced by a private platform, broker, referral network or hierarchy. Adding jurisdictional welfare objectives can make the actors public, but the result then becomes a familiar public-economics externality layered on a known market-design mechanism. A further public-specific constraint would be a second substantive mechanism and is not authorized as a rescue.

## 11. Skeptical-referee result

The final referee attack is terminal:

> Vertical allocation of intermediary tasks matters in every candidate only because it activates a known downstream mechanism—platform network effects, search/product design, cream-skimming, segmentation, multihoming, sequential search or hierarchy. No candidate makes vertical task assignment itself an indispensable source of new economics.

## 12. Stage-3 success test

Canonical success requires at least one candidate with:

1. a clear strategic loop;
2. a minimal implementable model;
3. plausible model/proposition-level distance from closest literature.

Several candidates satisfy (1) and (2). None credibly satisfies (3).

## 14. Research value of the negative result

The run rules out a broad class of tempting but derivative models before formal algebra investment. Specifically, it records that:

- multilevel innovation intermediation is a real institutional phenomenon but not a contribution by itself;
- “local articulation + national supplier thickness” is not enough;
- cross-side participation feedback is already platform economics;
- selective local-to-national referral creates a known cream-skimming/segmentation logic;
- hierarchical escalation creates a known organizational-economics logic.
