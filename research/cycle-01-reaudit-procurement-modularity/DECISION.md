# Cycle 1 Re-Audit — Final Decision

## Verdict

# **RE-AUDIT CONFIRMS OLD NO-GO**

## Why

The old Cycle 1 conclusion was correct for the bundle/unbundle question. The previous reasoning was incomplete because it collapsed all effects into procurement-stage bidder entry and did not separately test a persistent post-award architecture.

The re-audit restored that distinction:

\\[
\text{lotting} \neq \text{interoperability},
\\]

and verified that complex ICT, defense, and modular-system procurement can use open standards, APIs, technical-data rights, portability, and lifecycle support terms that persist after award.

That institutional correction did not produce a novel mechanism. The six allowed variants all reduced as follows:

- M1 and M4: platform access or vertical foreclosure;
- M2: open technology and supplier investment;
- M3: procurement bundling plus exogenous compatibility;
- M5: hold-up/switching costs/platform access;
- M6: dynamic procurement commitment plus standard compatibility/openness.

The closest recent threat is Hanazono and Sato (2026), *Compatibility and Investment Incentives in Dynamic Procurement*, alongside Chu and Wang (2015) on bundled procurement and future competition, and Hu, Hu, and Yang (2017) on open technology, supplier investment, and future competition.
