# Cycle 1 Re-Audit — Stage 0: Question and Primitive Re-Audit

## 1. Research question

Can a public buyer's procurement architecture endogenously affect a winning
prime supplier's post-award modularity/interoperability choice, thereby
changing post-procurement complementor entry and future market structure,
rather than merely changing bidder entry in the procurement auction?

The proposed chain is a hypothesis to be tested, not an assumption:

[
\text{procurement design}
\rightarrow
\text{prime's architecture choice}
\rightarrow
\text{persistent interfaces/modularity}
\rightarrow
\text{complementor entry}
\rightarrow
\text{future competition}.
]

## 2. Policy instrument

The policy object is public procurement contract architecture. The audit
distinguishes two potentially separate dimensions:

1. **Lotting:** one integrated contract versus multiple contracts for system
   components.
2. **Interoperability/interface terms:** technical specifications, open
   standards, API/interface access, portability, replacement rights, and
   lifecycle maintenance/upgrades.

The re-audit must not assume that unbundling automatically creates
interoperability. If the two dimensions are legally or economically separate,
the original bundled-versus-interoperable comparison is instrumentally
conflated and must be split or killed.

## 3. Economic primitive

A prime supplier or integrated incumbent chooses a post-award system
architecture, such as a more closed/integrated design or a more modular design
with usable interfaces. The choice affects:

- current coordination and system-performance costs;
- the ability of independent firms to supply complements, replacements,
  maintenance, applications, or upgrades;
- the incumbent's future rents and exposure to competition.

The distinctive primitive would be a **procurement-induced persistent
architecture choice**, not a lower entry cost imposed directly by assumption.

## 4. Strategic players

Minimum player types:

- one public buyer/government;
- one winning prime or integrated supplier;
- one potential independent complementor.

The procurement auction may contain additional bidders as a benchmark, but
they must not be the source of the claimed novelty. The post-procurement
market must contain an actual strategic complementor decision.

## 5. Proposed timing

A minimal timing candidate is:

1. government chooses contract architecture and any enforceable interface/
   portability terms;
2. procurement award occurs;
3. the winning prime chooses or implements architecture within the contract;
4. the potential complementor chooses entry, module supply, maintenance, or
   upgrade investment;
5. the installed system generates future replacement/upgrade competition.

This timing is provisional. Stage 1 must establish whether the architecture
choice and persistence are institutionally plausible.

## 6. Procurement-stage market

The procurement-stage outcome may change:

- number of bidders;
- lot-specific participation;
- prime/subcontractor organization;
- coordination cost;
- award price.

These are controls and benchmarks. They are not sufficient for a surviving
theme because the old Cycle 1 already failed on this margin.

## 7. Post-procurement market

The relevant downstream market is for complements or lifecycle services:
replacement modules, maintenance, applications, upgrades, or other products
that use the installed system's interfaces. The key question is whether the
initial procurement changes the future market's architecture and therefore the
entry/competition game.

## 8. Persistence test

A procurement architecture can affect future market structure only if at
least one persistence channel is real:

- an enforceable interface, portability, or open-standard clause;
- a durable installed-base/interface choice made by the winning supplier;
- lifecycle maintenance or upgrade rights governed by the initial contract;
- switching or replacement costs tied to the procured system.

A bare statement that today's lot structure remains tomorrow's architecture is
not acceptable. If persistence is only assumed, the candidate is a kill
candidate.

## 9. Endogeneity test

The audit separates three cases:

- **Government-specified:** the government directly requires interoperability;
- **Prime-chosen:** the prime chooses open or closed architecture after award;
- **Contract-induced:** lotting, evaluation, ownership, lifecycle terms, or
  interface obligations change the prime's payoff from choosing open or closed.

Only the third case, or a nontrivial interaction between the second and third,
can carry the proposed novelty. A government-mandated open interface with a
mechanically lower complementor entry cost is not enough.

## 10. Complementor entry decision

The complementor must make a strategic entry or investment decision after
observing the installed architecture. Entry must affect future market
structure, not merely be an exogenous count. A plausible decision is whether
to incur a fixed relationship-specific cost to supply a compatible module or
lifecycle service and compete with the incumbent.

## 11. Incumbent/integrated supplier incentive

The prime may prefer a closed architecture because it preserves future
replacement, maintenance, or upgrade rents. It may prefer openness if
coordination, procurement payment, quality, or demand expansion compensates for
the loss of future rents. The mechanism must make the prime's architecture
choice strategic and policy-responsive.

## 12. Government objective

The government values current procurement cost and system performance, plus
future complementor entry, innovation, contestability, switching resilience,
and lifecycle cost. The planner's architecture preference must differ from the
prime's for a primitive reason, not by assuming a welfare weight on entry.

## 13. Candidate welfare distortion

The candidate could generate a trade-off:

- integrated/closed design: lower current coordination cost but future
  foreclosure, high lifecycle costs, or weak complementor innovation;
- modular/open design: higher coordination or scope cost but more entry,
  replacement competition, and innovation.

The trade-off is useful only if the architecture choice is endogenous and
persistent, and if the same result cannot be obtained from standard
compatibility, foreclosure, platform openness, or entry-cost theory.

## 14. Closest standard models

The strongest reduction families are:

- procurement bundling, lotting, and multi-sourcing;
- standard entry-cost and fixed-cost models;
- vertical foreclosure and vertical integration;
- platform openness and ecosystem governance;
- compatibility and standards adoption;
- switching costs and installed-base competition;
- public procurement demand pull and lifecycle contracting.

## 15. Exact novelty burden

To pass Stage 2, the candidate must demonstrate all of the following:

1. lotting and interoperability are not being conflated;
2. procurement design changes the prime's architecture incentive;
3. architecture persists after award through a documented or explicitly
   contractible channel;
4. complementor entry is a strategic downstream response;
5. the effect is not just a lower complementor fixed cost;
6. removing the procurement stage breaks the mechanism;
7. removing modularity/interoperability breaks the mechanism;
8. the closest platform, standards, foreclosure, switching-cost, and
   procurement models cannot reproduce the main proposition.

## 16. Fatal null hypothesis

The entire chain is a relabeled standard model:

[
\text{open architecture}
\rightarrow
\text{lower complementor entry cost}
\rightarrow
\text{more entry}.
]

If this remains the mechanism, or if a platform owner, private buyer,
vertically integrated firm, or standards authority can reproduce it without
the procurement stage, the re-audit must terminate.

## Stage-0 decision

**GO TO STAGE 1 — CONDITIONAL ON INSTITUTIONAL PERSISTENCE.**

The question is worth an institutional audit because post-procurement
complementor entry is logically distinct from procurement-stage bidder entry.
However, the candidate carries a high prior-art and instrument-conflation
risk. A failure to verify persistent architecture or endogenous prime choice
will be fatal.
