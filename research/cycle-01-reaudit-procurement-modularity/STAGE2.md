# Stage 2 — Deep Literature Kill / Frontier Audit

## Audit question

The question under test is not merely whether a public buyer should bundle tasks. It is whether procurement architecture can change a winning prime's *persistent system architecture*, thereby changing later complementor entry and future market structure.

The audit therefore treats the following as separate claims:

1. lotting changes procurement-stage bidder/consortium entry;
2. a technical or contractual requirement changes the implemented architecture;
3. that architecture persists into maintenance, upgrade, replacement, or complement markets;
4. a later entrant responds strategically to the architecture;
5. the buyer's architecture choice has a welfare effect beyond a procurement-price effect.

## Frontier conclusion

**CONDITIONAL SURVIVOR — NO CLEAN WHITE SPACE; ADVANCE ONLY TO AN ADVERSARIAL STAGE 3 REDUCTION TOURNAMENT.**

No single source located in the search combines every label in the candidate—public procurement, post-award modular architecture, independent complementor entry, and ex-post concentration. That is a limited white-space observation, not a novelty result.

The reduction threat is unusually strong:

- [Chu and Wang (2015)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2014.0517) already makes current bundled procurement affect technology acquisition and competition in a future market.
- [Hu, Hu, and Yang (2017)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2016.0598) already studies open/closed technology, supplier investment, and intensified future competition; the publisher classifies it under procurement.
- [Hanazono and Sato (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176526003253) is a particularly close recent threat: its title and publisher metadata directly join compatibility, investment incentives, and dynamic procurement. The re-audit does not call it exact prior art without a full-text comparison, but it removes any presumption of an unoccupied mechanism.
- Compatibility, data portability, and switching-cost theory already makes strategic openness affect future competition: [Jeon, Menicucci, and Nasr (2023)](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mic.20200309).
- Platform theory already makes openness affect complementor/developer investment and the sponsor's ability to capture rents: [Parker and Van Alstyne (2018)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2757) and [Eisenmann (2008)](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/09-030.pdf).
- Modularity theory already connects architecture to vertical disintegration and entry: [Argyres and Bigelow (2010)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.1090.0493) and [Arrieta, Fontana, and Brusoni (2023)](https://academic.oup.com/icc/article/32/1/155/6881836).
- The 2026 [Frenken and Romagnoli](https://academic.oup.com/icc/advance-article/doi/10.1093/icc/dtag047/8756941) paper further closes the generic “architecture changes market structure” gap, although it is evolutionary and non-strategic rather than a procurement model.

The remaining possible distinction is narrow: a *contract-induced* architecture choice by the awarded prime, where lotting, lifecycle scope, technical-data rights, or replacement rights change the prime's payoff from openness before later complementors enter. This is not established as a survivor. It is the only version allowed into Stage 3.

## Exact prior-art search result

No paper was verified as an exact match to the complete five-link chain:

\\[
\text{public contract architecture}
\rightarrow
\text{prime's endogenous persistent architecture}
\rightarrow
\text{independent post-award complementor entry}
\rightarrow
\text{future market structure}
\rightarrow
\text{public buyer welfare choice}.
\\]

However, this absence is not sufficient for GO. The individual links and nearly complete combinations are already covered. The candidate therefore carries a high burden of proving that the *interaction among the links* is a genuinely new strategic margin rather than a relabeling.

## Six gateway tests

| Test | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Procurement architecture endogenizes future architecture | Possible only if lotting, lifecycle scope, interface/data rights, or replacement rights alter the prime's payoff; a government-specified open interface is exogenous architecture | Conditional |
| 2. Future architecture changes strategic entry | Yes in compatibility, platform, and open-technology models | Passed but not novel |
| 3. Effect is not merely an entry-cost shift | Fails for the baseline \(F_E^{open}<F_E^{closed}\) story; requires prime foreclosure, hold-up, investment, or rent-capture feedback | Not established |
| 4. Removing procurement breaks mechanism | Fails if a platform owner or large private buyer can make the same openness choice; passes only for contract-induced prime incentives | Conditional |
| 5. Removing modularity/interoperability breaks mechanism | Yes, for the post-award complementor claim | Passed but not novel |
| 6. Closest standard model cannot reproduce main result | The baseline is reproduced by a combination of dynamic procurement, compatibility, platform openness, and vertical-control models | Fails for baseline |

## Classification

The candidate is not a strong white-space result. It is a **conditional survivor for Stage 3 only**, with no permission to add a new primitive after a variant is frozen. Stage 3 must ask whether any one minimal contract-induced feedback escapes the reduction tournament. If every variant reduces to a known model, the candidate is killed and Stage 4 is not run.

## What this changes relative to old Cycle 1

The old Stage 2 was too quick in treating the entire question as procurement-stage bidder entry. The institutional distinction between lots and lifecycle/interface terms is real, and the post-award state transition cannot be deleted without argument.

But retaining that state transition does not by itself reverse the old verdict. The re-audit finds a stronger prior-art frontier than the old audit documented. The old **conclusion** remains plausible; the old **reasoning** was incomplete.
