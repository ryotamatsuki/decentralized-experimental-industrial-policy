# Q3 Frontier Audit — Multilevel Innovation Intermediation

Date: 2026-08-28

## Verdict

**`SURVIVES STAGE 2 / POTENTIALLY DISTINCT — UNRESOLVED`**

Q3 survives only in a narrowed form. No novelty claim is established. The surviving object is not “local information versus central scale.” It is the endogenous assignment of *different intermediation tasks* across levels when that assignment changes strategic search/participation on both sides of an innovation-matching market.

## Research question

> How should technology-search and matching responsibilities be divided between local and national innovation intermediaries when the value of intermediation depends jointly on problem articulation on the adopter side and market thickness on the supplier side?

## Strongest reduction

> **This is just fiscal federalism with local information plus a centralized matching platform with thicker supply.**

That reduction is fatal unless Stage 3 can show a feedback absent from both parent literatures: organizational assignment changes adopter/supplier participation or search behavior, which changes market thickness/match composition, which in turn changes the value of the assignment.

## Closest literature

### 1. Kanoria & Saban (2021), Management Science
Evidence: author working paper full text inspected; published metadata verified.
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`**.

- Dynamic two-sided search with strategic agents.
- Pair-specific match values require costly discovery.
- A platform chooses/search-restricts the search environment.
- Search design changes equilibrium behavior and welfare.
- Market imbalance and differentiated agents matter.

Distance: one intermediary/platform, not an assignment of distinct demand-articulation and supplier-search functions across government levels. This is nevertheless the strongest formal threat to any claim that “intermediation architecture changes search behavior” is itself new.

### 2. Zhou et al. (2022), Transportation Research Part B
Evidence: published abstract/model synopsis verified; accessible manuscript located.
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`**.

- Third-party integration pools services across otherwise fragmented platforms.
- Integration thickens the market and reduces matching frictions.
- The comparison includes equilibrium and social welfare.

Distance: it integrates already-defined platform services rather than allocating different innovation-intermediation tasks between local and national public bodies.

### 3. Edler & Yeow (2016), Research Policy
Evidence: publisher/university extended paper description and bibliographic metadata verified.
Classification: **`COMPONENT OVERLAP`**.

- Directly studies demand–supply intermediation in public procurement of innovation.
- Identifies intermediation needs/functions and buyer capability failures.
- Public intermediation can support adaptation and learning in concrete procurement.

Distance: conceptual/case-based rather than a formal vertical-assignment model with endogenous participation/search.

### 4. Lepore (2024), Journal of the Knowledge Economy
Evidence: published abstract/review description verified.
Classification: **`COMPONENT OVERLAP / VERY CLOSE IN INSTITUTIONAL QUESTION`**.

- Maps intermediaries in regional and national innovation systems.
- Identifies knowledge-broker, market-broker and capability-builder roles.
- Explicitly identifies weak interaction between regional and national levels.

Distance: review/mapping; does not provide the candidate formal organizational-assignment mechanism.

### 5. Public innovation intermediary / PPI review frontier (2026)
Evidence: review full HTML/extended synthesis inspected.
Classification: **`COMPONENT OVERLAP`**.

The literature documents substantial intermediation activity but does not by itself verify a model/proposition-level gap. A descriptive review gap is not novelty evidence.

### 6. Fiscal federalism benchmark
Besley–Coate, Lockwood and the local-policy literature remain binding threats.
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE IF Q3 IS REDUCED TO LOCAL KNOWLEDGE VERSUS CENTRAL SCALE`**.

## Government essentiality

Weak at Stage 2. Private platforms can choose search environments and integration architecture. The public-policy contribution therefore cannot rest on the existence of an intermediary.

For Stage 3, public/subnational structure is admissible only if a minimal institutional primitive creates a genuine assignment problem without inventing an extra distortion. Candidate primitives may be jurisdictional coverage/nonappropriation or distinct task access, but Stage 3 must choose at most one and must kill the branch if it merely relabels platform ownership.

## Strategic loop that must be earned in Stage 3

`task assignment across intermediary levels -> adopter/supplier search or participation -> endogenous market thickness/match composition -> value of task assignment`

This is a *test target*, not an established mechanism.

## Candidate proposition language for Stage 3 testing

1. A split intermediation architecture can dominate fully local and fully centralized architectures only if task assignment changes participation/search behavior, not merely task productivity.
2. Greater supplier-side market thickness need not favor full centralization if centralizing adopter-side problem articulation changes which demands enter or are translated into searchable problems.
3. The optimal boundary of an intermediary may switch discontinuously when participation feedback makes one side of the market thin.

None is proved.

## Stage-3 kill contract

Kill Q3 immediately if the smallest admissible mechanism becomes any of:

- local signal precision versus central economies of scale;
- a standard one-platform search-design problem;
- two matching technologies with different exogenous costs;
- a private-platform model with “government” labels;
- an organizational-form ranking that does not alter strategic participation/search.

## Stage-2 status

**Q3 is the only candidate recommended for human Stage-3 approval, but only as `POTENTIALLY DISTINCT / UNRESOLVED`.**
