# Q19 Frontier Audit — Implementation Projects as State-Capacity Investment

Date: 2026-08-28

## Verdict

**`NO-GO`**

Q19 does not survive the Stage 2 reduction test as a standalone theory question. The candidate distinction—current implementation activity builds the government's future ability to select, screen or govern projects—is economically meaningful, but the minimum structure collapses into established state-capacity investment, organizational learning-by-doing, or repeated-buyer/procurement learning unless a second strategic mechanism is added.

## Research question tested

> When should a government undertake technology-implementation projects partly because conducting them builds its future capability to identify, screen, and govern supplier–adopter matches, and when does that learning rationale instead justify excessive public project activity?

## Strongest reduction

> **This is just endogenous state capacity / learning-by-doing by the public buyer.**

The audit does not identify a defensible endogenous strategic response that makes this reduction false without adding another mechanism.

## Closest literature

### 1. Besley & Persson (2009), American Economic Review
Evidence: published abstract and canonical model description verified.
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`**.

- Current investment builds state capacity that constrains future policy choices.
- Capacity is deliberately accumulated because it raises future policy effectiveness.
- The investment decision is forward looking and welfare/political incentives determine accumulation.

Q19 changes the *type* of capacity from legal/fiscal capacity to innovation intermediation capability. That is not enough for theory novelty unless the capability changes a distinct strategic market response.

### 2. Gur, Macnamara & Saban (2022), Management Science
Evidence: published extended abstract/model description verified.
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / COMPONENT CONTAINMENT`**.

- A repeated buyer contracts with a strategic seller.
- Current transactions generate information about persistent seller cost/quality.
- The buyer trades off current performance and learning and chooses how to explore.

Q19 is not identical because it wants the buyer's own production technology to improve rather than merely its belief about seller type. But the distinction is not yet a strategic mechanism.

### 3. Kähkönen et al. (2025), Journal of Purchasing and Supply Management
Evidence: published open-access article metadata, highlights and abstract verified.
Classification: **`COMPONENT OVERLAP`**.

- Innovative public procurement is associated with public-buyer sensing, seizing and transforming capabilities.
- Those capabilities can affect supply-market development.

This makes “public procurement/implementation builds or requires buyer capability” an active empirical/organizational literature, not a blank theoretical space.

### 4. Organizational learning / public-sector learning literature
Classification: **`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`** if Q19 capability is cumulative experience, lower future cost, or generic better decision quality.

### 5. State-capacity reviews and public policy capability literature
Classification: **`COMPONENT OVERLAP`**.

The distinction between administrative capability types may be institutionally important, but type labels do not create new economics.

| K2: learning supplier quality only | explicitly occupied by procurement learning | `FATAL FOR THAT VERSION` |

## Government essentiality

Government is not sufficient to make the mechanism distinctive. A private repeated buyer, procurement intermediary, hospital system, platform or large firm can also learn to search, screen and govern suppliers through experience.

A public-specific wedge could be added—for example nonappropriation, turnover, political incentives or fragmented authority—but doing so would be a second substantive mechanism and is prohibited by this Stage 2 audit.

## Why the “excessive public projects” side fails

A benevolent government that rationally invests in a capability stock can choose more current activity because of its future return; that is not “excessive” relative to its own social objective. To obtain overactivity, Q19 would need a divergence such as agency incentives, soft budgets, inability to commit, or externalized project costs. Those are separate mechanisms.

## Residual research value

The idea remains useful institutionally and empirically: implementation programs may build public-buyer/intermediary capability. It can be a mechanism in later empirical work or an extension to another theory, but it is not a standalone main-theory branch under current governance.

## Stage-2 status

**`Q19 = NO-GO`**

Do not send Q19 to Stage 3 and do not rescue it with political incentives, soft-budget effects, personnel turnover, or an endogenous supplier/adopter-composition mechanism without a new Stage 0/3 authorization.
