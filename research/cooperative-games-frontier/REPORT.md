## Main literature result

Most obvious cooperative-game framings are occupied:

1. endogenous R&D coalitions, open/exclusive membership and government subsidies — mature;
2. external subsidy to stabilize a coalition/coalition structure — cost-of-stability prior art;
3. partition-function coalition formation and cross-coalition externalities — canonical;
4. collective conditional adoption subsidies — direct prior evidence;
5. policy changing stable coalitions — direct applied prior art outside innovation;
6. innovation ecosystems and technology alliances analyzed with cooperative games/Shapley/core — existing;
7. exclusive/nonexclusive licensing and strategic outside options in innovation diffusion — existing.

Therefore `government + coalition + subsidy`, `core becomes nonempty`, `participation threshold`, `innovation ecosystem cooperative game`, and `Shapley allocation` are not viable contributions.

## Institutional motivation and evidence boundary

FY2026 Ehime official materials verify a real supplier–adopter collaboration margin. In the manufacturing/maritime horizontal-deployment subsidy, a prefectural applicant must collaborate with a prior Triangle Ehime / Triangle Ehime 2.0 selected provider and establish an implementation structure before application. Separate current programmes support adoption/horizontal deployment of previously tested digital technologies, and the 2026 general Triangle route targets solutions expected to extend to multiple prefectural firms.

This supports the motivation `industrial policy can condition support on inter-firm implementation relationships`. It does **not** establish transferable utility, binding coalition agreements, a core allocation, or a general policy commitment to stabilize coalitions. Those remain theoretical constructs requiring separate justification.

## Surviving TOP 3

### 1. C1 — Nonseparable Coalition-Composition Grants

**Score:** 79/100.  
**Status:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`.  
**Subjective probability of eventual Stage-2/3 survival:** **35%**.

**Expected contribution:** establish a strict implementability difference between coalition-composition-contingent industrial policy and an economically justified class of additive firm subsidies when coalition-specific complementarity and blocking matter.

**Closest threat:** Bachrach et al. cost of stability + Yi & Shin R&D-coalition subsidies + collective conditional subsidy/adoption literature.

**Why it survives:** the scouting did not locate an exact theorem comparing these instrument classes in the targeted technology-diffusion setting once the question is narrowed to **nonseparability**, not merely coalition support.

**What must be proven next:** a non-equivalence result. If individual transfers can condition freely on the same joint event, `s(S)` may be only notation. The restriction generating non-equivalence must be economically defensible and not introduced ad hoc.

### 2. C5 — Partition-Contingent Diffusion Policy

**Score:** 77/100.  
**Status:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`.  
**Subjective probability of eventual Stage-2/3 survival:** **25%**.

**Expected contribution:** show that partition-blind industrial-policy instruments can select/destabilize the wrong technology-diffusion coalition structure when support to one coalition changes rival coalition values, while a partition-aware policy can implement the efficient structure.

**Closest threat:** Ray–Vohra partition-function coalition formation, R&D coalition externalities, Bareille et al. policy/coalition stability, and recent technology licensing coalition work.

**Why it survives:** no exact industrial-policy theorem with the proposed technology-diffusion instrument comparison was located.

**What must be proven next:** identify a technology-specific cross-coalition externality whose policy comparative static is not inherited from generic environmental or R&D coalition models.

### 3. C7 — Open-Membership / Portability Conditions for Technology Diffusion Coalitions

**Score:** 74/100.  
**Status:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`.  
**Subjective probability of eventual Stage-2/3 survival:** **20%**.

**Expected contribution:** explain when public support should be conditional on nonexclusive/open downstream participation because an early coalition privately prefers exclusion that blocks socially valuable diffusion.

**Closest threat:** Yi & Shin open/exclusive membership rules; Vergari & Filippini exclusive/nonexclusive licensing and strategic outside options; multihoming/licensing literature.

**Why it survives:** a public industrial-policy portability condition may alter the feasible set of blocking coalitions rather than merely subsidy levels.

**What must be proven next:** show that the result is not ordinary exclusivity regulation/licensing or a relabeled R&D membership rule.

The probabilities are subjective research-director assessments, not statistical estimates.

## Strongest candidate

**C1 — Nonseparable Coalition-Composition Grants.**

Its strongest formulation is not `government subsidizes cooperation`. It is:

> Under what economically justified restrictions does conditioning industrial policy on a complementary coalition's composition strictly enlarge the set of efficient stable coalitions implementable relative to additive firm-level support?

The central strategic object is subset-specific blocking under nonseparable complementarity. This is a genuinely new primitive relative to A1's scalability/finance/market-size architecture.

## Strongest prior-art threat

The strongest combined threat is **cost-of-stability / coalition-subsidy theory plus R&D-coalition and collective-conditional-adoption theory**. Bachrach et al. already analyze external payments conditional on maintaining a target coalition/structure; Yi & Shin already connect public subsidy to wider stable R&D-consortium participation; Ouvrard et al. show collective conditional adoption subsidies in practice. Any C1 formulation that only says `support is paid when a group forms` is therefore dead.

## Ultimate reduction test

The branch survives only if all of the following can be answered YES at Stage 0:

1. Is there an economically meaningful coalition-specific complementarity/outside-option/blocking primitive absent A1?
2. Is the relevant coalition policy genuinely nonseparable rather than `sum t_i`?
3. Is the restriction on firm-specific transfers justified by information, contracting, legal eligibility, observability or institutional structure rather than chosen to force a theorem?
4. Does blocking/coalition structure change the welfare or implementability result, rather than merely rename individual participation constraints?
5. Does the mechanism remain general after removing Ehime-specific labels?

A NO on items 1–4 kills C1 before model construction.
