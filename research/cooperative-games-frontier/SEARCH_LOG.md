# Search Log

Search date: 2026-08-29 JST. Scope: pre-Stage-0 prior-art and structural-containment audit. No formal model was built.

## Corpus accounting

The audit screened **32 unique named literature records** across seven mandatory frontiers, plus current Ehime primary sources. The count is a run-level audit count, not a claim of systematic-review exhaustiveness.

## F1 — Cooperative R&D / R&D coalitions

Queries: `research joint venture coalition formation subsidy`, `endogenous R&D coalitions spillovers government subsidy`, `innovation consortium subsidy cooperation 2024 2025 2026`.

Key records: d'Aspremont & Jacquemin (1988); Kamien, Muller & Zang (1992); Kamien & Zang (1993); Yi & Shin (2000); Branstetter & Sakakibara (1998); Sakakibara (2001); Yang et al. (2026).

Finding: endogenous coalition membership, open/exclusive membership, spillovers, free riding, government-sponsored consortia, consortium diversity and subsidy effects are mature. A generic `R&D coalition + subsidy` route is killed.

## F2 — Cooperative games and subsidies

Queries: `cost of stability coalitional games subsidy`, `core stabilization external subsidy`, `subsidies stability restricted cooperation coalitional games`.

Key records: Bachrach et al. (2009); Bachrach et al. (2018); Meir, Rosenschein & Malizia (2011).

Finding: an external authority paying a coalition or coalition structure to prevent deviation is direct prior art. `policy makes core nonempty` is not a contribution by itself.

## F3 — Coalition formation

Queries: `endogenous coalition formation partition function bargaining`, `coalition formation externalities binding agreements`, `open exclusive membership coalition formation`.

Key records: Ray & Vohra (1999); Ray (2007); Yi (2003 survey).

Finding: coalition structure, blocking/deviation and partition-dependent payoffs are canonical. Cooperative notation must identify a new economic restriction/instrument, not merely a new solution concept.

## F4 — Partition-function games

Queries: `partition function game coalition externalities industrial organization`, `technology licensing coalition externalities`, `core partition function recent`.

Key records: McQuillin (2009); Grabisch & Funaki (2012); Kóczy (2018); Nakada & Shirakawa (2025); recent NTU partition-function core work published online 2024 / volume 2026.

Finding: rival-coalition externalities are not new. A surviving industrial-policy candidate must deliver a policy theorem tied to a technology-diffusion externality or instrument restriction, not simply replace `v(S)` with `v(S,Pi)`.

## F5 — Industrial policy

Queries: `industrial policy coordination failure complementarity`, `modern industrial policy coalition`, `place based industrial policy coordination`.

Key records: Juhász, Lane & Rodrik (2024); Aghion et al. (2015); Buera et al. (2021); Rausser, Foster & Choi (2025).

Finding: coordination failures, complementarities and coalition/political-support language are already prominent. The cooperative-game contribution must be sharper than `industrial policy solves coordination failure`.

## F6 — Diffusion / adoption / procurement

Queries: `collective conditional subsidy technology adoption`, `coalition formation diffusion shared goods`, `innovation diffusion outside option licensing`, `public procurement innovation diffusion`.

Key records: Chiappinelli, Giuffrida & Spagnolo (2025); Dai, Li & Chen (2021); Ouvrard et al. (2023); Pasimeni & Ciarli (2026); Vergari & Filippini (2023); Kuhfuss et al. collective-bonus work.

Finding: conditional collective adoption subsidies, shared-good coalition formation, strategic outside options, exclusive/nonexclusive diffusion and demand-pull procurement are substantial direct threats. A coalition threshold alone is occupied.

## F7 — Ecosystems / complementarity / collective cooperation

Queries: `innovation ecosystem cooperative game`, `technology innovation alliance Shapley core`, `policy coalition formation complementarities`.

Key records: Granstrand (2024); Jiang et al. (2021); Mêgnigbêto (2018); Bareille et al. (2021).

Finding: innovation ecosystems have already been formalized in cooperative-game terms; technology alliances already use Shapley/core; and public policies have been studied as determinants of stable cooperation in another applied domain. Application-only novelty is therefore rejected.

## Current institutional sources

FY2026 Ehime official pages were checked separately from theory. They verify: (i) implementation and horizontal-deployment support for previously tested digital technologies; (ii) for manufacturing/maritime horizontal deployment, the applicant must collaborate with a previous Triangle Ehime / Triangle Ehime 2.0 selected provider and establish an implementation structure before application; and (iii) the 2026 general route targets solutions expected to expand to multiple prefectural firms.

These sources do **not** verify transferable utility, core bargaining, binding multilateral coalition contracts, or a universal policy commitment to stabilize coalitions.

## Search outcome

No exact paper was found that proves the specific C1 non-equivalence claim between economically restricted additive firm subsidies and nonseparable coalition-composition grants in a technology-diffusion industrial-policy environment. However, cost-of-stability, R&D-coalition, collective-conditional-subsidy, team/complementarity and coalition-policy literatures create a high structural-containment risk. Hence C1 is `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`, not novelty-certified.
