|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Cooperative R&D | Yi & Shin (2000); Branstetter & Sakakibara (1998); Sakakibara (2001); Yang et al. (2026) | endogenous consortium membership, spillovers, subsidies, diversity | kill generic subsidized innovation consortium |

## Highest-risk reductions

1. **Cost of stability:** an outside actor conditionally pays a coalition/structure so that players do not deviate.
2. **R&D coalition theory:** government subsidies can expand participation in efficient research consortia under spillovers/free riding.
3. **Collective conditional subsidy:** public payment can already be conditional on a local collective adoption threshold.
4. **Policy and coalition stability:** policy instruments can already alter stable coalitions in environmental/resource applications.
5. **Innovation ecosystem cooperative games:** complementarity and value capture among innovation actors are already modeled using cooperative games.
6. **Licensing/diffusion:** exclusive versus nonexclusive diffusion and strategic outside options already generate bargaining-based technology diffusion results.

## Potential white-space boundary

The only plausible frontier found is not `coalitions matter`. It is the narrower implementation question:

> Can a **genuinely nonseparable policy conditional on coalition composition or membership rights** implement an efficient stable technology-diffusion structure that cannot be implemented by an economically justified class of additive firm-level transfers?

This boundary is fragile. If coalition policy can be decomposed into individual transfers, or if individual transfers may condition on the same joint event with no meaningful institutional/information restriction, the distinction disappears.
