# Policy Instrument Map

## Instrument classes

| Instrument | Generic representation | Prior-art status | Frontier implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| firm-specific transfer | `t_i` | mature subsidy/tax policy | baseline comparator |
| additive coalition payment | `sum_{i in S} t_i` | not distinct from firm transfers | cannot carry novelty |
| grand-coalition stabilization payment | payment conditional on no deviation | cost-of-stability prior art | killed as standalone mechanism |
| collective threshold payment | payment if participation/adoption exceeds threshold | direct adoption/agri-environment prior art | coalition size threshold alone is occupied |
| composition-contingent payment | `s(S)` depends on identities/types/complement coverage | partial overlap | C1 survives only if nonseparability has economic content |
| membership-right condition | subsidy conditional on open/nonexclusive/portable participation rights | R&D membership/licensing overlap | C7 survives only if policy–stability interaction is distinct |
| partition-contingent policy | `s(S,Pi)` reacts to rival coalition structure | generic PFF theory occupied | C5 needs an industrial-policy-specific externality theorem |

## Critical non-equivalence test

C1 cannot rely on notation. The relevant question is whether there exists a realistic policy-information/instrument environment in which a composition-contingent grant cannot be reproduced by a vector of additive firm transfers.

A putative difference is **not** valid if the policymaker can simply define each `t_i` to be contingent on the same joint event, because that recreates `s(S)` by relabeling. A Stage-0 survivor therefore needs a substantive restriction such as noncontractible pairwise complementarities, restricted observability of individual contributions but observable consortium composition, budget/eligibility rules, or endogenous blocking that changes coalition-specific outside options. Those restrictions must themselves be institutionally and theoretically defensible; they cannot be inserted solely to manufacture non-equivalence.

## Welfare wedge needed

The surviving policy theorem must compare private stable coalition structures with a social objective that includes a clearly identified distortion: cross-coalition spillovers, business stealing, complementarity not internalized by prospective members, or strategic exclusion/nonexclusive diffusion. Merely transferring surplus among coalition members is not enough.
