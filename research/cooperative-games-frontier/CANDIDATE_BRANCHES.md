## C1 — Nonseparable Coalition-Composition Grants

1. **Research question:** When can a grant conditional on a coalition's complementary composition implement an efficient stable technology-diffusion coalition that cannot be implemented by an economically justified class of additive firm subsidies?
2. **Primitive:** coalition-specific complementarity; partner heterogeneity; nonseparable coalition-contingent policy.
3. **Strategic margin:** coalition membership/blocking and partner substitution.
4. **Why cooperative games:** value and deviation incentives depend on the subset that forms, not only on individual participation.
5. **Policy instrument:** composition-contingent eligibility/payment.
6. **Welfare distortion:** privately unstable complementary combination despite positive social spillovers or uninternalized complementarity.
7. **Closest literature:** cost of stability; R&D consortia; collective conditional subsidies; team/coalition formation.
8. **Reduction threat:** if `s(S)` is replicable by firm transfers contingent on the same joint event, the mechanism is notation only.
9. **Generality:** consortia, supplier–adopter groups, decarbonization, digital infrastructure, joint standards.
10. **Potential theorem:** under defensible information/instrument restrictions and nonseparable complementarities, additive transfers have a strictly smaller implementable stable-coalition set than composition-contingent grants.
11. **Kill condition:** no economically defensible non-equivalence between the two instrument classes.
12. **Verdict:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`.

## C2 — Diversity-Contingent Consortium Grants

1. RQ: should government subsidize only consortia covering heterogeneous capabilities?
2. Primitive: member-type diversity and complementarity.
3. Strategic margin: partner composition.
4. Cooperative necessity: weak; team formation can often reproduce it.
5. Instrument: diversity eligibility rule.
6. Distortion: missing complementary capabilities.
7. Closest: Sakakibara (2001), R&D consortium/team-composition literature.
8. Threat: application of diversity/complementarity selection.
9. Generality: high.
10. Theorem candidate: diversity threshold changes stable membership.
11. Kill: absorbed by C1 or standard team formation.
12. Verdict: `HIGH OVERLAP — KILLED AS SEPARATE BRANCH`.

## C3 — Coalition Architecture via Core Stabilization

RQ: can government reshape coalition values so a desired coalition becomes stable? Primitive: blocking/core. Instrument: external subsidy. Closest threat: Bachrach et al. cost of stability and related restricted-cooperation subsidies. Potential result is already structurally contained. **KILL.**

## C4 — Supplier-to-Adopter Stability Switch

RQ: can changing blocking incentives over an innovation lifecycle switch the least-cost side of support from supplier to adopter? Primitive: time-varying outside options/stability. Threats: standard dynamic R&D/adoption subsidies, licensing bargaining, shared-good coalition diffusion. Cooperative necessity was not established without adding a new primitive. **HIGH OVERLAP / KILL FOR CURRENT SELECTION.**

## C5 — Partition-Contingent Diffusion Policy

1. **RQ:** how should industrial policy respond when support for one diffusion coalition changes the value and stability of rival coalitions?
2. **Primitive:** coalition externality and rival coalition formation.
3. **Strategic margin:** coalition partition/blocking.
4. **Cooperative necessity:** private payoff cannot be evaluated from own coalition alone.
5. **Instrument:** partition-aware subsidy/eligibility rule.
6. **Distortion:** interoperability, exclusion, business stealing or common-supplier congestion not internalized in coalition choice.
7. **Closest:** partition-function theory; R&D coalition externalities; Bareille et al. policy/stability; technology licensing coalitions.
8. **Threat:** generic PFF application.
9. **Generality:** high.
10. **Potential theorem:** partition-blind subsidies can destabilize or select the wrong diffusion structure whereas a partition-contingent policy implements the efficient structure under a technology-specific externality.
11. **Kill:** theorem inherited directly from generic environmental/coalition policy.
12. **Verdict:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`.

## C6 — Coalition Selection versus Individual Screening

RQ: when is selecting combinations of firms different from screening firms separately? Primitive: match-specific complementarity. Threat: team formation/matching/mechanism design; without a distinct coalition-contingent instrument this is C1 stripped of its key policy question. **KILL AS STANDALONE BRANCH.**

## C7 — Open-Membership / Portability Conditions for Technology Diffusion Coalitions

1. **RQ:** can public support conditioned on nonexclusive/open membership prevent an early adopter coalition from blocking socially valuable downstream diffusion?
2. **Primitive:** exclusivity/nonexclusive membership, multi-coalition participation rights, coalition-specific outside options.
3. **Strategic margin:** supplier/adopter blocking and ability to join additional coalitions.
4. **Cooperative necessity:** membership rights change feasible blocking coalitions and the supplier's outside options across coalitions.
5. **Instrument:** subsidy/eligibility conditional on portability or nonexclusive downstream access.
6. **Distortion:** early private coalition captures surplus through exclusion and suppresses later diffusion.
7. **Closest:** Yi & Shin open/exclusive membership; nonexclusive licensing/diffusion; platform multihoming.
8. **Threat:** licensing policy or membership-rule theory may fully contain it.
9. **Generality:** digital technology, standards, shared infrastructure, licensing and public testbeds.
10. **Potential theorem:** a portability condition can dominate an equal-cost cash subsidy when early coalition exclusivity creates a diffusion-blocking outside-option externality.
11. **Kill:** reduction to ordinary licensing/exclusivity regulation or R&D membership rules.
12. **Verdict:** `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`, high risk.

## C8 — Public Coalition-Formation Insurance

Government guarantees coalition members against partner failure. This reduces to insurance/risk-sharing/contracting unless group deviations create a separate mechanism; cooperative theory is not essential. **KILL.**

## C9 — Scarce Public Implementation Capacity as Coalition Member

A local public resource can support only a limited number of coalitions. The central allocation problem reduces to matching/assignment/capacity allocation without a necessary cooperative-game margin and risks reopening previously explored scarce-capacity ideas. **KILL.**

## C10 — Coalition-Conditional Shared Learning

Government rewards coalitions for shared evidence/learning that benefits subsequent adopters. This maps to learning spillovers, cooperative R&D and diffusion policy and would risk rescuing previously killed learning-based mechanisms. **KILL.**
