# Partition-Function Industrial Policy Frontier

## Baseline

A partition-function game permits a coalition's value to depend on the entire coalition structure, written abstractly as `v(S, Pi)`. This is canonical theory, not novelty.

## Direct prior-art pressure

- Ray & Vohra (1999) make coalition payoffs depend on the full coalition structure and derive endogenous structures through bargaining.
- Yi's coalition-formation literature applies partition-function logic to R&D coalitions and other economic coalitions with externalities.
- Nakada & Shirakawa (2025) study stable core partitions with technology licensing in an oligopoly.
- Bareille et al. (2021) explicitly examine how public policy instruments alter the stability of coalitions in a spatial externality setting.

Therefore `technology diffusion + v(S,Pi) + subsidy` is not sufficient.

## Surviving narrow question — C5

**Partition-Contingent Diffusion Policy** asks whether subsidizing one technology-adoption coalition changes the outside options/stability of rival coalitions through a technology-specific externality, so that the welfare-optimal policy must condition on the coalition partition rather than only on individual adoption.

Possible economically relevant externalities to audit later include interoperability, strategic exclusion, congestion in a common supplier, or business stealing. None is adopted as a model primitive in this run.

## Why it provisionally survives

The search did not identify an exact industrial-policy theorem establishing a non-equivalence between partition-blind additive subsidies and partition-contingent diffusion policy for technology coalitions. This is narrower than generic PFF analysis.

## Why it is fragile

Environmental-coalition policy already shows that policy affects coalition stability, while IO coalition models already incorporate cross-coalition externalities. A Stage-0 formulation that merely changes the application domain from environmental cooperation to innovation diffusion would be killed.

## Verdict

**C5: `PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE`**, score 77/100, subject to a fatal Stage-0 reduction test: identify an industrial-policy-specific externality/instrument interaction whose comparative statics cannot be inherited from generic partition-function coalition policy.
