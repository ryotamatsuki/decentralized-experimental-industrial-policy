# Supplier-to-Adopter Policy Switching Audit

## Candidate claim

Can innovation lifecycle changes in coalition marginal contributions or blocking incentives endogenously switch optimal support from the technology supplier to adopters?

## Closest threats

1. Standard R&D versus adoption/demand subsidy models already generate lifecycle-dependent policy incidence.
2. Public procurement and demand-pull literatures already distinguish support for innovation creation from support for diffusion.
3. Vergari & Filippini (2023) show that strategic outside options and exclusive/nonexclusive licensing affect innovation diffusion and bargaining.
4. Pasimeni & Ciarli (2026) directly study coalition formation as a condition for diffusion of shared goods.

## Cooperative-game necessity test

A decline in the supplier's marginal contribution after fixed costs become sunk and an increase in adopter marginal contribution is not sufficient. That story can be written as ordinary dynamic surplus or adoption economics without blocking coalitions.

To survive, the switch would need to be caused by a qualitative change in the **stability constraint**: for example, the set of profitable blocking coalitions changes after technology creation, and the least-cost policy capable of deterring those deviations changes side. No current institutional fact establishes this as the Triangle Ehime mechanism, and the scouting did not locate a robust primitive that prevents non-cooperative reconstruction.

## Verdict

**C4: `HIGH OVERLAP / KILLED FOR CURRENT SELECTION`.**

The idea remains intuitively interesting, but at this gate it does not satisfy the requirement that cooperative-game structure add irreducible economics. Reopening would require a genuinely new primitive, not merely lifecycle timing.
