# Stage 3 Candidate Mechanisms

Search/assessment date: 2026-08-28

Stage 2 kills are binding. Each candidate below must create a strategic feedback, not merely add a feature. “Expected result” means a Stage 4 conjecture, not a theorem.

## C1 — Supplier co-financing and subsidy incidence

- **Mechanism:** A supplier expecting downstream sales after a successful local trial is willing to absorb part of the experimentation cost; local public support can therefore crowd out supplier self-financing rather than create additional experimentation.
- **Feedback loop:** downstream commercial rent → supplier willingness to finance → lower incremental effect of subsidy → government support changes rent incidence rather than experiment occurrence.
- **Endogenous margins:** supplier contribution; local support; trial occurrence.
- **Minimum players:** local government, supplier, local adopter, downstream market.
- **Minimum new primitive:** supplier-owned downstream rent from trial success/evidence.
- **Closest threat:** PPI risk allocation, procurement versus grants, supplier rents.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** zero/low optimal local subsidy when supplier commercialization incentives alone induce the trial; positive support only for the residual social benefit not appropriated by supplier/host.
- **Welfare content:** fiscal transfers may be pure rent transfers.
- **Fatal attack:** ordinary procurement contract/risk-sharing result.

## C2 — Local exclusivity versus evidence disclosure

- **Mechanism:** A host government values temporary local exclusivity while the supplier values broad evidence diffusion to sell elsewhere; public support may be exchanged for delayed disclosure or local exclusivity.
- **Feedback loop:** local subsidy → exclusivity/disclosure condition → slower follower diffusion → higher host rent but lower supplier/social commercialization value → changes subsidy demand and trial incentives.
- **Endogenous margins:** support, disclosure/exclusivity, downstream diffusion.
- **Minimum players:** host government, supplier, local adopter, follower jurisdictions.
- **Minimum new primitive:** disclosure affects host rent and supplier downstream market in opposite directions.
- **Closest threat:** Callander–Harstad transparency/IPR; Acemoglu et al. patents; procurement IP design.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** decentralization can intentionally suppress diffusion even when public finance was justified by information production.
- **Fatal attack:** relabeled IPR/transparency result.

## C3 — Region-specific fit uncertainty × reusable supplier learning

- **Mechanism:** A field trial reveals both common technology viability and region-specific implementation requirements; the supplier converts some local implementation learning into reusable adaptation know-how for later regions.
- **Feedback loop:** local trial → common public evidence + supplier-private reusable know-how → larger supplier downstream profit → stronger supplier willingness to finance/choose a host → changes local subsidy and diffusion incentives.
- **Endogenous margins:** supplier contribution, host support, location, later adaptation/adoption.
- **Minimum players:** local government, supplier, host adopter, follower jurisdiction/adopter.
- **Minimum new primitive:** two knowledge components with different appropriability/transferability.
- **Closest threat:** Detragiache adaptation externality; Acemoglu et al. copying; demonstration learning; absorptive-capacity literature.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** public support can be non-monotone in knowledge spillovers because more transferable knowledge raises social spillovers while more supplier-appropriable know-how raises private trial incentives.
- **Welfare content:** separates public-information externality from supplier-owned implementation learning.
- **Fatal attack:** two spillover parameters mechanically generate the ranking.

## C4 — Competition to host a supplier’s scarce launch trial

- **Mechanism:** A supplier needs at most one launch/field trial before national commercialization. Multiple local governments bid/support to host it because the host gets an early-use/place-specific benefit, while trial evidence and supplier commercialization benefit non-host regions later.
- **Feedback loop:** supplier downstream rent determines whether a trial is privately viable → local host benefits induce competing support offers → supplier selects host/max support → support may change only trial location/rent rather than the extensive margin → follower information remains external.
- **Endogenous margins:** whether the trial occurs; which region hosts; local support/bid; supplier location choice; later follower adoption.
- **Minimum players:** two local governments, one supplier, one host adopter per region/follower market; national planner only as benchmark.
- **Minimum new primitive:** a single/scarce experiment location creates host-only early-use benefit while supplier owns national downstream rent.
- **Closest threat:** local bidding for firms; Callander–Harstad; PPI supplier rents/procurement competition.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** local subsidies can be strictly wasteful when supplier downstream rents already make experimentation privately viable—support changes the host or transfers rent but creates no experiment. Conversely, decentralized support can still be too low when the trial is socially valuable only after counting non-host information/adoption benefits.
- **Welfare content:** directly partitions no-policy, useful-local-support, wasteful-hosting-race, and possible national-coordination regions.
- **Institutional interpretation:** local governments competing to become the first customer/test field for a technology supplier that plans wider commercialization.
- **Fatal attack:** ordinary firm-location bidding with “experiment” as a label. Stage 4 must make the experiment generate information/downstream commercialization that changes the extensive margin.

## C5 — Endogenous local re-validation / replication

- **Mechanism:** After observing another region’s successful trial, a follower decides whether outside evidence is sufficient or whether local-fit uncertainty justifies a second local validation trial.
- **Feedback loop:** pioneer evidence → follower uncertainty reduction → follower chooses adopt versus re-test → repeated trials generate more local fit information but duplicate common information → affects pioneer value and diffusion speed.
- **Endogenous margins:** follower adoption/retest; number/location of validations.
- **Minimum new primitive:** evidence has common and local-fit components.
- **Closest threat:** experimentation with heterogeneous districts; technology diffusion/adaptation; clinical/field replication logic.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** decentralized systems may rationally duplicate trials even when common-quality evidence is public, but duplication can be efficient when local-fit variance is high.
- **Fatal attack:** heterogeneity parameter mechanically rationalizes retesting.

## C6 — Success-biased demonstration design / certification

- **Mechanism:** A supplier values a visible success because it certifies the technology for downstream buyers, while the public sponsor may value a successful local project; both can prefer an easy/high-success but low-information demonstration to a harder experiment that is more informative socially.
- **Feedback loop:** downstream certification rent + sponsor success payoff → choice of demonstration design/site → higher success probability but lower information content → follower adoption beliefs and commercialization → feeds back into project selection/support.
- **Endogenous margins:** trial design/site/informativeness; supplier effort; support; disclosure/certification.
- **Minimum players:** public sponsor, supplier, local adopter/test field, downstream buyers/followers.
- **Minimum new primitive:** trade-off between probability of visible success and informativeness/external learning.
- **Closest threat:** PPI certification/signaling; demonstration-project governance; information design; political credit claiming.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** stronger downstream certification value can reduce social information production (“demonstration theater”), so evaluating programs by success rate may worsen experimentation quality.
- **Welfare content:** distinguishes success from learning; suggests failure-tolerant/evidence-based evaluation.
- **Fatal attack:** existing signaling/information-design model with a government label, or need for too many behavioral/political primitives.

## C7 — Selective disclosure of trial outcomes

- **Mechanism:** Sponsor and supplier may disclose successful trial evidence more aggressively than failures because both face reputational/commercial costs from bad news.
- **Feedback loop:** disclosure incentives → follower beliefs → downstream demand → supplier/sponsor reputation → ex-ante trial and disclosure choices.
- **Endogenous margins:** disclosure, trial participation, follower adoption.
- **Minimum new primitive:** outcome-dependent disclosure cost/benefit.
- **Closest threat:** voluntary disclosure, certification and publication-bias literatures.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** public demonstrations can increase apparent success while worsening follower decisions unless disclosure is mandatory.
- **Fatal attack:** standard disclosure/signaling theorem.

## C8 — Local-retention conditionality versus diffusion

- **Mechanism:** A host government conditions support on supplier branch location, local hiring or continued local activity to capture part of the supplier’s national commercialization benefit.
- **Feedback loop:** support → retention condition → supplier location/commercialization cost → host captures local benefit → changes willingness to host and supplier diffusion.
- **Endogenous margins:** support, retention commitment, supplier location/entry, diffusion.
- **Minimum new primitive:** local-retention condition tied to trial support.
- **Closest threat:** place-based subsidies, firm-location bidding, local procurement preference.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** retention conditions may internalize local fiscal cost but distort efficient supplier location or deter high-spillover trials.
- **Fatal attack:** standard place-based conditional subsidy result.

## C9 — Sequential wait-versus-lead timing

- **Mechanism:** Regions decide whether to sponsor now or wait for another region’s information.
- **Feedback loop:** waiting → information → reduced risk → reduced incentive to lead.
- **Closest threat:** direct strategic-experimentation/federal-experimentation prior art.
- **Decision:** `REJECT FROM TOP SET` unless combined with a genuinely new supplier margin; as stated it is already known.

## C10 — National co-financing × local skin in the game

- **Mechanism:** National co-financing internalizes cross-region benefits while mandatory local contribution screens local value/fit.
- **Feedback loop:** match rate → local participation/selection → national fiscal exposure → trial quantity/quality.
- **Closest threat:** fiscal federalism/matching grants/common pool plus procurement centralization.
- **Decision:** useful policy extension/benchmark, not baseline contribution.

## C11 — Portfolio duplication / fashionable technologies

- **Mechanism:** Local governments independently select demonstrations with high visible expected success or political salience, causing correlated pilot portfolios; a national planner values diversified experiments because information across technologies is a portfolio public good.
- **Feedback loop:** local success incentives → correlated technology selection → low information diversity → follower learning → future selection.
- **Endogenous margins:** project/technology choice, portfolio diversity, support.
- **Minimum new primitive:** correlated experiment alternatives / diversity value.
- **Closest threat:** Callander–Harstad diverse experimentation; portfolio experimentation; political selection.
- **Expected nontrivial result:** decentralization may create too many pilots but too little informational diversity.
- **Fatal attack:** Callander–Harstad diversity result plus another project dimension.

## Reduction-test results

- C9 fails immediately: collapses to strategic/federal experimentation.
- C10 is retained only as a later policy benchmark: collapses to fiscal-federalism matching-finance logic.
- C2 and C7 face severe IPR/disclosure prior art.
- C8 faces severe place-based/subsidy-competition prior art.
- C5 and C11 are plausible but require additional heterogeneity/portfolio primitives and face direct experimentation-diversity threats.
- C1 is tractable and useful but faces PPI risk-allocation prior art.
- **C3, C4 and C6 survive as TOP candidates for human review.**
