# C4 — Preferred minimum viable Stage 4 skeleton

## Research question

How does a technology supplier’s downstream commercialization value affect decentralized governments’ incentives to subsidize and compete for the location of an information-producing launch trial, and when does local support create experimentation rather than merely relocate the trial or transfer rents?

## Minimum players

- two symmetric or minimally heterogeneous local jurisdictions/governments;
- one private technology supplier;
- a potential local adopter/implementation field in each jurisdiction;
- follower users/regions represented minimally through the value of information/later adoption;
- a national planner/government only as a welfare/policy benchmark, not necessarily a strategic player in the baseline.

## Minimum timing

1. A technology exists but requires one launch/field trial before broad commercialization/adoption.
2. Local governments choose whether/how much support to offer for hosting the trial.
3. The supplier decides whether to conduct the trial and, if so, which jurisdiction hosts it.
4. The trial outcome generates evidence/implementation experience.
5. If successful/useful, the supplier commercializes downstream and non-host regions may adopt using the evidence.
6. Welfare is compared across laissez-faire/private launch, decentralized local support, and later policy benchmarks.

The exact simultaneity/sequencing of local offers and the supplier’s participation/location decision is a Stage 4 design choice and must be selected for analytic minimality, not desired results.

## Minimum information structure

Baseline should avoid adding local private information unless necessary. The trial resolves uncertainty that has value for downstream commercialization/adoption. Host-specific benefits may initially be common knowledge. Local-information asymmetry should be reserved for extension/robustness because Stage 2 killed it as a standalone contribution.

## Minimum qualitative primitives

- real resource cost of conducting the launch trial;
- supplier’s expected downstream commercialization value generated by a successful/informative trial;
- host-only early-use/place benefit from being the trial location;
- non-host informational/adoption benefit generated by the trial;
- fiscal/social cost of public support;
- optional location-specific trial quality/cost only if needed to test mislocation; do not put this in the first baseline unless the core extensive-margin result already survives.

Do not insert a persistent ecosystem rent unless necessary; Stage 1 did not verify it.

## Endogenous decisions

- local support/hosting bids;
- supplier participation: trial or no trial;
- supplier host-location choice;
- later adoption may be reduced to a continuation value in the minimum model and should become explicit only if required.

## Strategic feedback that must be present

Supplier downstream commercialization affects the private profitability of the **experiment itself**. Local governments care about the **host status**. Thus local support can operate on two different margins:

- **extensive experimentation margin:** whether a trial occurs at all;
- **location/rent-shifting margin:** which jurisdiction hosts a trial that would occur anyway.

If the model cannot separate these margins, reject C4.

## Welfare comparison targets

At minimum compare:

- no government support / supplier-led launch;
- decentralized local support/hosting competition;
- social-planner benchmark.

Only after the baseline mechanism survives should Stage 4/5 consider national co-financing or restrictions on local support.

## Conjectures to attempt to falsify

1. **Non-additionality:** supplier downstream commercialization can make the trial privately viable while local hosting bids remain positive, causing fiscal support with zero experiment additionality.
2. **Under-experimentation:** when non-host information/adoption value is large but supplier+host private/local value is too small, decentralized support can fail to induce a socially valuable trial.
3. **Mislocation:** if minimal heterogeneity is later authorized, hosting competition may choose the region with the greatest local bidding value rather than the socially best information/cost location.
4. **Policy targeting:** higher-level support targeted to the extensive experimentation margin may dominate unrestricted local hosting subsidies.

## Mandatory Stage 4 kill tests

Kill C4 if any of the following holds:

- after removing the information/downstream value of the trial, the headline result is unchanged and becomes ordinary firm-location bidding;
- after removing local host benefits, the result becomes standard supplier subsidy/procurement participation;
- after removing supplier downstream commercialization, the result becomes standard federal experimentation/free riding;
- positive local subsidy under private trial viability is only a welfare-neutral transfer with no fiscal distortion, resource cost or strategic consequence;
- the “under versus over” classification is tautologically imposed by separate benefit parameters rather than equilibrium behavior;
- an exact prior-art model is found during Stage 4 re-check.

## Preferred baseline discipline

Start with the smallest possible deterministic/one-shot location game around one uncertain launch trial. Do **not** initially add:

- private local information;
- endogenous supplier effort;
- political credit;
- branch-location/retention conditions;
- multiple suppliers;
- repeated trials;
- endogenous disclosure;
- dynamics beyond the minimum trial→commercialization sequence.

One strategic wedge must carry the result.

# C6 — Alternative skeleton

## Minimum players
Public sponsor, technology supplier, implementation site/adopter, downstream users/followers.

## Core choice
The trial/design/site has a trade-off between probability of visible success and informational value/diagnostic difficulty.

## Strategic loop
Supplier downstream certification value and sponsor value from local success push toward visible success; society values information for future adoption. The experiment may become less informative as commercial/political success incentives rise.

## Stage 4 conjectures

- stronger downstream certification rent can reduce information production;
- success-rate-based program evaluation can select low-information demonstrations;
- evidence/information-based evaluation can improve welfare.

## Kill risk
High: signaling, persuasion/information-design, certification and political-credit literatures may contain the mechanism. Do not choose C6 without a targeted full-text audit around endogenous test difficulty/informativeness.

# C3 — Alternative skeleton

## Minimum players
Local sponsor, supplier, local adopter, follower jurisdiction/adopter.

## Core knowledge structure
A trial produces two economically distinct outputs:

- public/common evidence about technology viability;
- supplier-owned/reusable implementation/adaptation know-how.

## Strategic loop
More reusable supplier learning raises private downstream commercialization incentives and supplier self-financing; more public evidence raises external social value. Public support responds to the split in appropriability.

## Stage 4 conjectures

- optimal support may fall as supplier appropriability rises even while total trial value rises;
- support should target the non-appropriable information component rather than supplier-owned commercial learning;
- follower benefits can coexist with substantial private supplier incentives, overturning a naive “spillovers imply subsidy” result.

## Kill risk
High: two knowledge parameters may mechanically generate the result; adaptation/diffusion prior art is close.
