# Multi-Site / Scale Frontier — A1 Novelty Kill

Date: 2026-08-28
Agent G role: Multi-Site / External-Validity Scout

## What can “multi-site” mean economically?

Three readings were audited separately:

1. **information/generalization:** several sites improve external validity;
2. **selection:** several sites act as a costly eligibility criterion selecting broadly applicable projects;
3. **design:** suppliers choose a design that works across heterogeneous users/environments.

## 1. Information/generalization reading

### Gechter et al. (2024; current arXiv version)

A Bayesian decision-theoretic framework chooses experimental sites to improve external validity of policy evidence. Site selection matters because treatment effects differ across locations.

### Egami & Lee (2024)

The parent bibliography records a multi-site external-validity design paper using synthetic purposive sampling.

**Verdict:** `FATAL AS A1 NOVELTY CLAIM`. If S matters only because more sites produce more generalizable evidence, the economic object is experimental/site design, not a new supplier mechanism.

## 2. Selection reading

A multi-site requirement raises the cost/feasibility threshold for participating projects. Drake & Xu (2025) and minimum-quality-standard/free-entry theory already provide the generic economics of costly eligibility/requirements affecting entry.

**Verdict:** `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED`.

## 3. Endogenous-design reading

Multi-market product-design literature explicitly studies commonality versus customization across separate markets; modularity theory studies products supporting multiple standards; minimum-quality-standard theory links public standards to endogenous innovation/R&D.

**Verdict:** `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED AT THE MECHANISM-FAMILY LEVEL`.

## Pilot-to-scale literature

### Brown & Hendry (2009)

Government-funded demonstration projects/field trials test technology under operational environments and aim to shorten the path to widespread availability and adoption. The study connects demonstration design, market targeting and later diffusion.

### WHO / ExpandNet (2011), Beginning with the End in Mind

Implementation guidance explicitly recommends designing pilot projects with future scaling in mind and provides a scalability checklist.

**Interpretation:** not formal economics, but it removes any novelty claim based on the managerial maxim `pilots should be designed for scale`.

## Multi-site scout conclusion

Multi-site implementation is institutionally distinctive in Triangle Ehime, but its economic roles are not unoccupied:

- external validity -> site-selection theory;
- eligibility -> screening/entry regulation;
- broad-fit design -> commonality/modularity/product-quality theory.

A1 would need an irreducible connection among these roles without adding another mechanism. The frozen definition does not supply one.
