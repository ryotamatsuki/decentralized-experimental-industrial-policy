# Reduction Tests — A1 Scalability-by-Design

Date: 2026-08-28
Agent B/C/D/E/F/G synthesis

## R1 — Screening

**Reduction:** A1 is an eligibility rule selecting scalable firms/projects.

**Finding:** Entry-regulation and MQS/free-entry theory directly contains a requirement that changes entry and project/product composition.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` for exogenous-type interpretation.

## R2 — Participation subsidy

**Reduction:** F is a transfer that makes costly multi-site participation privately acceptable.

**Finding:** No distinct A1 mechanism remains if F only lowers participation/implementation cost. Government innovation finance and conditional-support models occupy this role.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` as a standalone novelty claim.

## R3 — Demand pull / market size

**Reduction:** R is a larger expected downstream market for reusable solutions.

**Finding:** Market expansion, multi-market commonality/modularity and lead-market/demand-pull literatures already make future market scope affect design and innovation.

Triangle sources do not verify a universal binding ex-ante follow-on subsidy/contract for every initial project.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` under the frozen evidence boundary.

## R4 — Procurement

**Reduction:** qualification standard + cost support + follow-on contract/demand.

**Finding:** Qualification/contractual requirements with endogenous entry and innovation procurement/demand-pull are mature. Exact follow-on contractual commitment is not verified for A1.

**Verdict:** `WEAKENED / STRUCTURALLY CLOSE`; becomes fatal in combination with R1–R3 and decomposition failure.

## R5 — Lead market

**Reduction:** initial regional market plus downstream diffusion opportunity.

**Finding:** Lead-market theory already explains local adoption/refinement followed by broader diffusion and product-design implications.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` to a reference-market-only contribution.

## R6 — Pilot to scale

**Reduction:** public demonstration/implementation followed by commercialization/scale support.

**Finding:** demonstration/field-trial and implementation literature explicitly connects pilots to later commercial diffusion; design-for-scale guidance is longstanding.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` to the generic pilot-to-scale framing; formal composition novelty would still need independent survival, which it does not.

## R7 — Product design

**Reduction:** multi-site requirement / future market causes modular, common or general-purpose design.

**Finding:** commonality, modularity, customization and MQS-product-R&D literatures occupy this margin.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` to endogenous-design escape.

## R8 — External validity

**Reduction:** multiple sites are valuable because they improve generalizability.

**Finding:** multi-site site-selection/external-validity theory directly occupies this question.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` if information/generalization is the role of S.

## R9 — Policy mix

**Reduction:** S/F/R are complementary hard/soft/demand-side instruments.

**Finding:** policy-mix literature explicitly centers interdependencies; procurement+R&D subsidy and Japanese place-based hard/soft combinations are current examples.

**Verdict:** `FATAL` to generic complementarity.

## R10 — Market formation / infant support

**Reduction:** temporary public implementation support builds a market later served privately.

**Finding:** lead-market, PPI and demonstration/commercialization literatures already occupy temporary demand/market-formation framings. A1 adds no authorized primitive that creates a distinct transition problem.

**Verdict:** `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`; not a survivor.

# Commitment test

Two readings of R are possible:

1. **Opportunity/expected market:** verified institutionally; then R is market size/demand pull and R3 is fatal.
2. **Credible ex-ante government commitment to future support:** potentially changes ex-ante behavior, but a universal binding commitment is not verified and commitment would be an additional mechanism.

A1 cannot be rescued by silently moving from (1) to (2).

**Verdict:** `NO COMMITMENT RESCUE`.

# Government essentiality

A private accelerator, platform, systems integrator or corporate buyer could require multi-client applicability, co-finance implementation and expose successful offerings to downstream clients. The central entry/design logic remains.

**Verdict:** `GOVERNMENT NOT ESSENTIAL TO CORE STRATEGIC EFFECT`.

This is not independently fatal if a general IO theorem were novel, but the general IO design/standard mechanism is already occupied.

# Local-government essentiality

Replacing Ehime Prefecture with a national innovation agency leaves the same logic.

**Verdict:** `LOCAL GOVERNMENT NOT ESSENTIAL / NOT FATAL BY ITSELF`.

The appropriate general category would be regional/public technology-deployment architecture, but generalization does not cure the novelty failure.

# Primary fatal reduction

> A1 is a standard screening/eligibility problem with an exogenous scalable type or a standard-induced endogenous product-design choice; implementation finance lowers participation/design cost; downstream replication raises expected market size.

The audit cannot reject this reduction without adding a new primitive.

# Ultimate test

## Question 1

If Triangle Ehime is removed, is A1 still an interesting economic question?

**Answer:** As a generic policy/design question, yes; but its operative margins already sit inside mature screening, endogenous-design and policy-mix theory. Triangle Ehime supplies motivation, not novelty.

## Question 2

If “multi-site”, “implementation subsidy”, and “replication support” are replaced by generic screening, transfer, and market size, does the economics remain unchanged?

**Answer:** **YES, materially unchanged.** The only alternative—endogenous design—maps into minimum-standard/commonality/modularity theory.

Per the frozen gate, this is a kill condition.

## Question 3

Does the architecture change endogenous composition/design in a way that none of the individual instruments generates alone and that the closest formal model does not already contain?

**Answer:** **NO.** Pairwise models already generate the relevant selection, participation and design responses; S+F+R changes thresholds rather than introducing a new strategic margin.

# Reduction verdict

`PRIMARY FATAL REDUCTION SURVIVES.`

A1 cannot pass Stage 2 on the frozen primitives.
