# Selection vs Inducement — A1 Novelty Audit

Date: 2026-08-28

## Why the distinction matters

A1 can alter project composition through two conceptually different channels:

- **Selection:** different suppliers/projects enter or pass the programme.
- **Inducement:** the same supplier changes its design because of the programme architecture.

Treating them as one effect would obscure whether A1 offers any strategic margin beyond standard screening.

## Selection effect

### Frozen reading

Projects differ ex ante in scalability/multi-site feasibility. S makes the applicant/selected pool more scalable; F partially offsets participation cost; R raises the value of scalable projects.

### Prior-art comparison

- public entry/eligibility requirements with endogenous entry are formalized in procurement;
- MQS/free-entry models show a standard can reduce entry and alter market/product composition;
- conditional subsidies/finance alter participation.

### Result

`SELECTION EXISTS AS A PLAUSIBLE A1 EFFECT, BUT DOES NOT SURVIVE AS NOVELTY.`

It is structurally contained by standard entry/screening theory.

## Inducement effect

### Frozen reading

A supplier observes the architecture and chooses a more reusable, modular, broad-fit or scalable design rather than a bespoke design.

### Institutional support

Triangle Ehime's multi-site eligibility and horizontal-deployment orientation make this interpretation institutionally plausible as a research conjecture. However, the primary sources do not prove that the same supplier causally redesigns its product because of the programme. The audit therefore treats inducement as a candidate economic response, not an established institutional fact.

### Prior-art comparison

- minimum-quality standards can alter endogenous entrepreneurial innovation/product R&D;
- R&D subsidies alter endogenous quality;
- multi-market commonality/modularity theory makes broader market scope affect product architecture;
- market expansion changes customization incentives.

### Result

`INDUCEMENT EXISTS AS A MODELABLE MARGIN, BUT DOES NOT SURVIVE AS NOVELTY.`

Endogenizing design moves the problem from screening into mature product-design/quality-regulation theory.

## Can either channel alone support Stage 3?

### Selection-only variant

No. It reduces directly to eligibility/entry screening plus participation finance and market size.

### Inducement-only variant

No. It reduces to public standard + subsidized design cost + future market scope.

### Selection + inducement together

Using both channels would increase state/choice complexity while combining two already-occupied margins. The combination is not justified merely to escape prior art.

## Conclusion

| Channel | Economically plausible? | Institutionally compatible? | Novelty status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Selection | yes | yes | `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED` |
| Inducement | yes | plausible, causal effect not proven | `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED` |
| Both jointly | yes | possible | `NO INDEPENDENT NOVELTY GAIN` |

Neither channel supplies a Stage-3-worthy mechanism under the frozen A1 primitives.
