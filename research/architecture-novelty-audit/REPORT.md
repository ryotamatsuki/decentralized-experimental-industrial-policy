A1 does not pass the novelty gate. The strongest fatal reduction survives:

> A1 is a standard screening/eligibility problem with an exogenous scalable type, or a standard-induced endogenous product-design problem; implementation finance lowers participation/design cost; downstream replication raises expected market size.

The three-block bundle changes thresholds and parameter regions, but this audit identifies no strategic behavior that first appears only under S+F+R. Sequencing also does no independent work under the frozen primitives.

No formal model is authorized or produced.

## B. A1 frozen architecture

### S — Scalability / Multi-Site Eligibility

The initial programme requires/selects projects that can operate at multiple Ehime sites or plausibly deploy horizontally.

### F — Implementation Finance

Public support bears part of implementation/adaptation/validation cost for selected projects.

### R — Downstream Replication Opportunity

Validated solutions may be packaged, horizontally deployed and supported at later adopters.

Important evidence boundary: the institutional audit verifies downstream opportunity/support, but **does not verify a universal binding ex-ante commitment to future support for every initial project**. Therefore R cannot be silently converted into a commitment mechanism.

## C. Strongest prior art

At least five particularly important containing/adjacent formal literatures are:

1. **Drake & Xu (2025), Journal of Public Economic Theory** — procurer-announced entry regulations and contractual requirements alter endogenous bidder entry/bidding. Strong containment of S as costly eligibility/requirement.
2. **Constantatos & Perrakis (1998), Journal of Regulatory Economics** — minimum quality standards, free entry and the timing of quality choice; explicitly shows that entry/quality sequencing affects welfare/market structure.
3. **Zhang, Zhong & Gao (2025), Journal of the Operational Research Society** — minimum quality standards affect endogenous product R&D/quality choice.
4. **Jonnalagedda & Saranga (2017), European Journal of Operational Research** — firms choose product commonality when designing for multiple markets, directly threatening bespoke-versus-reusable A1 design logic.
5. **Jost (2024), Marketing Letters** — market expansion changes the scope of customization, containing the R -> ex-ante design margin.
6. **Toshimitsu (2003), International Journal of Industrial Organization** — R&D policy changes endogenous quality, threatening F -> design novelty.
7. **Cao et al. (2025)** and the broader innovation-policy-mix literature — procurement/demand pull and R&D subsidies are already studied jointly.
8. **Gechter et al. (2024)** / multi-site external-validity literature — if S is interpreted as generalization/evidence production, that route is already occupied.

No source is classified `EXACT PRIOR ART`. The kill is **structural containment**, not keyword or title matching.

## D. Screening reduction

### Exogenous-type interpretation

If suppliers/projects are already `scalable` or `bespoke`, S changes who enters/passes, F relaxes the participation burden, and R raises scalable projects' downstream value.

That is a standard eligibility/screening + participation-finance + market-value structure.

**Verdict: `FATAL`.**

The core project-composition result can be generated without an A1-specific mechanism.

---

## E. Product-design reduction

### Endogenous-design interpretation

The attempted escape is that the same supplier chooses bespoke versus reusable/scalable design after observing programme architecture.

But the operative arrows are separately mature:

- S -> design: minimum/performance standards can alter endogenous quality/innovation;
- F -> design: innovation/R&D subsidies can alter endogenous product quality/design;
- R -> design: wider/multiple markets alter commonality, modularity and customization.

**Verdict: `FATAL`.**

Endogenizing design moves A1 into product-quality/product-design theory rather than creating a distinct mechanism.

---

## F. Procurement / policy-mix reduction

A1 can also be read as `qualification requirement + implementation cost support + future demand opportunity`.

Public procurement already treats entry regulations/contractual requirements; public innovation policy already treats demand-pull/procurement and R&D subsidy interactions; policy-mix work makes generic complementarity unavailable as novelty.

There is no verified A1-specific follow-on procurement commitment that creates a distinct dynamic contracting problem.

**Verdict: `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED / FATAL IN COMBINATION WITH DECOMPOSITION FAILURE`.**

---

## G. Selection vs inducement

### Selection

Economically plausible and institutionally compatible, but structurally contained by eligibility/entry/MQS theory.

**Novelty status: `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED`.**

### Inducement

Economically plausible: the same supplier may design for broader reuse. But minimum-standard/product-R&D and multi-market design theory already generates this response.

**Novelty status: `STRUCTURALLY CONTAINED`.**

### Joint use

Combining both channels raises complexity while combining two already-occupied margins. It does not create an independent novelty gain.

---

## H. Sequencing

Observed institutional order is approximately:

`screen -> financed implementation -> downstream replication`.

If S/F/R rules/opportunities are anticipated before supplier entry/design, changing their administrative ordering does not add a strategic state. To make order matter would require a new primitive such as learning between stages, sunk investment before rule revelation, hidden information/effort, or government commitment.

Those additions are outside frozen A1 and several are explicitly prohibited rescue mechanisms.

**Verdict: `SEQUENCING DOES NO INDEPENDENT ECONOMIC WORK` — FATAL under the run contract.**

## Decomposition / non-additivity result

- **S+F:** costly eligibility/performance condition + participation/design-cost support.
- **S+R:** standard/broad-fit requirement + future market scope.
- **F+R:** supply-push implementation/R&D support + demand pull.
- **S+F+R:** changes thresholds/magnitudes, but no new strategic margin was identified.

**Three-way-only behavior test:** none identified within the frozen primitives.

Therefore A1 fails the required non-additivity standard.
