# Policy-Mix Frontier — A1 Novelty Kill

Date: 2026-08-28
Agent E role: Policy-Mix Killer

## Audit question

Does A1 offer more than the established result that different innovation-policy instruments interact and can be complementary or substitutable?

## Frontier

### Flanagan, Uyarra & Laranja (2011)

Foundational policy-mix framing centers interactions and interdependencies among policies. `Many instruments` or `instruments interact` is unavailable as novelty.

### Borrás & Edquist (2013)

Instrument choice is explicitly framed as combinations of policy tools matched to innovation-system problems. This contains generic bundle rationales.

### Guerzoni & Raiteri (2015)

Empirically studies demand-side and supply-side technology policies and their interaction, including R&D support and innovative public procurement.

**A1 implication:** F+R complementarity is occupied.

### Howoldt (2024), Research Policy

Maps 4,710 innovation policy instruments and characterizes policy mixes across 46 countries.

**A1 implication:** unusual breadth/architecture is an institutional fact, not a mechanism contribution.

### Cao et al. (2025)

Government procurement and R&D subsidies are jointly analyzed in an evolutionary-game framework; policy balance changes innovation behavior.

**A1 implication:** a theoretical supply-push + demand-pull mix is not white space.

### Okamuro & Nishimura (2026)

Using panel data from 146 Japanese cities, studies a place-based local R&D policy mix combining hard support with soft matching/mediation/consultation.

**A1 implication:** even local-government hard/soft complementarity in Japan is occupied empirically.

### 2026 supply-demand policy-mix work

The parent audit records current Technovation work on supply-demand instrument mixes and balance. These papers further eliminate a generic `balanced policy mix improves firm innovation` contribution.

## Non-additivity standard

Economic non-additivity is stronger than a statistical interaction or a model in which two instruments enter the same private payoff. A1 needs a distinct endogenous margin such that one block changes the *behavioral response* to another and the resulting proposition is not obtainable from the closest standard/finance/market-size model.

The audit finds:

- S changes entry/design through a standard/eligibility margin;
- F changes participation/design cost through a standard subsidy margin;
- R changes expected market scope through a standard demand/market-size margin.

The fact that these effects can have different signs or threshold interactions does not create a new mechanism.

## Policy-mix-killer verdict

`GENERIC COMPLEMENTARITY = FATAL.`

A1 can survive policy-mix prior art only through an irreducible composition mechanism. The decomposition test finds no three-way-only behavior, so the policy-mix threat is binding in the final gate.
