## F1/F2 — Coalition-contingent transfers and stability subsidies

- Bachrach et al., *The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games* (SAGT 2009; later expanded stability-cost work). Key threat: an external payment conditional on players not deviating may stabilize a target coalition/structure and can be distributed among agents.
- Yi & Shin (2000), *Endogenous Formation of Research Coalitions with Spillovers*, IJIO 18(2):229–256. Key threat: government subsidies can encourage wider participation in a stable research consortium.

## F3 — Team/joint production

- Holmström (1982), *Moral Hazard in Teams*, Bell Journal of Economics 13(2):324–340, DOI 10.2307/3003457. Aggregate output and multiagent incentive problems can create a role for an outside principal, but this is a moral-hazard mechanism and is excluded as an ad hoc C1 rescue.

## F4/F7 — Cooperative projects and public grants

- Santamaría, Barge-Gil & Modrego (2010), *Public selection and financing of R&D cooperative projects: Credit versus subsidy funding*, Research Policy 39(4):549–563, DOI 10.1016/j.respol.2010.01.011. Public agencies already select/fund cooperative projects and favour particular partner classes.
- Carnehl, Ottaviani & Preusser (2025), *Designing Scientific Grants*, Entrepreneurship and Innovation Policy and the Economy 4:139–178, DOI 10.1086/732856. Grant design is an established incentive-design field; team composition alone cannot carry novelty.

## F5 — Group deviations / coalition-proof implementation

- Boylan (1998), *Coalition-Proof Implementation*, Journal of Economic Theory 82(1):132–143, DOI 10.1006/jeth.1998.2424.
- Safronov (2018), *Coalition-proof full efficient implementation*, Journal of Economic Theory 177:659–677, DOI 10.1016/j.jet.2018.07.009.

These show that collective deviations can matter in mechanism design, but they also weaken any claim that blocking itself creates a novel industrial-policy instrument.

## F6 — Collective / group-contingent policy

- Parkhurst et al. - Bareille, Zavalloni & Viaggi (2023), *Agglomeration bonus and endogenous group formation*, American Journal of Agricultural Economics 105(1):76–98, DOI 10.1111/ajae.12305. Coalition formation and stable coalition structures are endogenous under a public bonus scheme.
- Bareille & Soubeyran (2025), *Individual versus group-level agglomeration bonuses to conserve biodiversity*, Journal of Environmental Economics and Management 133:103193, DOI 10.1016/j.jeem.2025.103193. Directly compares individual and group-level coordination incentives.
- Zavalloni & Bareille (2026), *The value of cooperation for biodiversity conservation policies*, Ecological Economics 243:108901, DOI 10.1016/j.ecolecon.2025.108901. Compares degrees of collectiveness, open-/closed-list group rules and individual schemes using group formation.

## Search conclusion

The additional search sharply strengthens the null. Policy can already make **individual payments conditional on other agents' actions or group membership**; coalition formation can be endogenized under such payment schemes; and genuine nonseparability from team production or joint liability requires a second mechanism. No exact paper with the C1 label is needed for the kill: the proposed I4 advantage fails the internal instrument-equivalence test before novelty can be claimed.
