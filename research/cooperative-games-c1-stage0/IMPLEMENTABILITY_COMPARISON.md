# Implementability Comparison — I2/I3 versus I4

## Benchmark

Hold fixed:

- the same observable coalition event `S`;
- the same target coalition structure;
- quasilinear monetary payoffs / transferable money;
- government ability to condition transfers on `S`;
- no extra private-information, moral-hazard or enforcement friction;
- no restriction on how the authority divides a fixed public payment across identified members.

## I3 to I2 replication

Suppose a coalition-labelled monetary payment `s(S)` is paid when target coalition `S` forms and is available for allocation among members. For any allocation of that payment across members, define `t_i(S)` equal to member `i`'s assigned amount. Then the total government payment and each member's post-policy monetary payoff can be reproduced by I2.

Therefore, any blocking inequality that depends only on member payoffs after receipt of the allocated subsidy is preserved under the replicated vector.

This is an implementability observation, not a formal theorem proof: Stage 0 does not parameterize a characteristic function.

## What would break replication?

Replication can fail only if at least one benchmark element changes, for example:

- the government cannot identify/contract on individual claims;
- coalition members cannot transfer or jointly procure;
- the public authority supplies an indivisible asset rather than money;
- liability is intrinsically joint;
- individual contributions are unverifiable;
- utility is materially nontransferable in the relevant dimension;
- member-specific contingent payments are legally forbidden while a coalition claim is legally feasible.

The audit asks whether any such restriction is both independently motivated and part of the frozen C1 object. None is established by current institutional evidence.

## Institutional comparison

Ehime's current manufacturing/maritime horizontal-deployment scheme points in the opposite direction: the applicant/recipient is an eligible local SME, while collaboration with a prior Triangle provider is an eligibility/implementation-structure requirement. The observed instrument is closer to `t_i(S)`—an individual/project grant conditional on a relationship—than to a coalition-owned I4 claim.

## Fatal result

**I4 non-equivalence is not established.** Under the relevant monetary benchmark, I3 is representable by I2. Creating a strict gap requires a second mechanism or a different policy instrument.
