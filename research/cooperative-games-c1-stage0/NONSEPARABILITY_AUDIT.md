# Nonseparability Audit

## A. Accounting nonseparability

A programme may send one payment to a lead applicant or label an award as a consortium/project grant. That administrative fact does not imply a nonseparable economic instrument.

If a monetary award `s(S)` can ultimately be allocated among coalition members, and the government can condition member payments on the same observed coalition event, then an allocation vector can be chosen with

`sum_{i in S} t_i(S) = s(S)`.

C1 obtains no strict implementation advantage from the payee label.

**Verdict: KILL as a novelty mechanism.**

## B. Conditionality without nonseparability

Policy may require that several actors jointly participate before any payment is made. This is economically meaningful conditionality, but it can be represented by `t_i(S)` when membership/joint action is observable.

The agglomeration-bonus literature provides a direct conceptual warning: an individual owner's payment can depend on complementary conservation choices by neighbouring owners. More recent work endogenizes group/coalition formation under these public payment rules and compares individual versus group-level schemes.

**Verdict: conditionality is not enough.**

## C. Candidate genuine nonseparability

Three classes could make the underlying *object* nonseparable:

1. indivisible common asset/public input;
2. joint production with non-attributable member contributions;
3. joint liability/common legal obligation.

But each defeats the I2 comparator only by changing a separate primitive.

- An indivisible physical input supplied directly by government changes the instrument from money to public/in-kind provision. If members can use monetary transfers to procure the same input jointly, the monetary non-equivalence disappears.
- Unverifiable individual contribution is the Holmström team-production/moral-hazard route, expressly outside frozen C1.
- Joint liability changes the liability technology and enters a mature group-lending/peer-selection literature.

## Conclusion

The audit finds no monetary `s(S)` that is genuinely nonseparable **under the same event observability, transferable-money and contracting benchmark used for `t_i(S)`**.

Nonseparability can be manufactured only by adding another friction or by changing the policy object from a monetary grant to an indivisible public input/liability structure. That is a new branch, not C1 hardening.
