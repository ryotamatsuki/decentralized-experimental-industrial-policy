## What was tested

C1 proposed that a coalition-composition-contingent industrial-policy payment `s(S)` might implement an efficient stable coalition that feasible member-level contingent transfers `t_i(S)` cannot implement.

The audit treated the strongest comparator seriously: individual payments may be conditioned on the *same observed coalition/joint-participation event*.

## Additional evidence and literature

Twelve additional named works were screened in the focused Stage-0 search, on top of the prior 32-record frontier audit, together with current FY2026 Ehime primary sources. The new search covered team production, joint liability, cooperative project funding, coalition-proof implementation, agglomeration bonuses and recent individual-versus-group incentive design.

The strongest recent direct threat is the agglomeration-bonus literature: Bareille, Zavalloni & Viaggi (2023) endogenize stable coalition structures under a public collective payment scheme; Bareille & Soubeyran (2025) explicitly compare individual and group-level bonuses; Zavalloni & Bareille (2026) compare open-list, closed-list, individual and standard schemes using group formation. This does not by itself prove C1's null, but it eliminates any claim that group-conditioned policy plus coalition formation is an unoccupied mechanism.

## Institutional evidence

FY2026 Ehime manufacturing/maritime horizontal-deployment rules require an applicant to collaborate with a prior Triangle selected provider and to establish an implementation structure before application. The eligible applicant is a prefectural SME/small business entity. The reviewed rules attach eligible expenditures, ex-post payment/evidence duties and asset restrictions to the subsidy recipient.

This verifies **relationship-contingent eligibility**, not a coalition-owned monetary claim, transferable-utility coalition, joint liability, or indivisible coalition asset.

## Fatal implementability result

Under the frozen benchmark—same observable `S`, transferable money, and authority able to condition member transfers on `S`—a monetary coalition payment allocated among coalition members can be represented by a vector `t_i(S)` with the same total public payment and the same target member payoff vector.

Accordingly, accounting nonseparability and joint-event conditionality do not create a larger implementation set.

A strict gap requires another restriction: indivisible in-kind public provision, unverifiable contributions, incomplete contracting, joint liability, nontransferable utility, or a legal inability to create member-level claims. None is established as a frozen C1 primitive. Each also redirects the paper into a distinct mature theory family.

## Blocking result

Coalition blocking can be economically meaningful when coalition surplus and outside options are subset-specific. But after reproducing the target member payoff vector with `t_i(S)`, the same blocking comparison remains. Blocking therefore does not produce the I4-over-I2 implementability difference.

## Strongest prior-art threat

The strongest combined threat is:

1. cost-of-stability theory for external payments conditional on coalition stability;
2. Yi–Shin R&D coalition/subsidy theory;
3. agglomeration-bonus/group-formation research, especially the 2023–2026 sequence that makes coalition formation, group enrollment rules and individual-versus-group incentives explicit.
