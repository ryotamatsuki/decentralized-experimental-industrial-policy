# Blocking Audit

## Question

Does coalition blocking create an implementation difference that individual participation constraints cannot capture under the C1 instrument comparison?

## Generic blocking structure

Let `B` be a subset that could leave a target coalition `S` and reorganize. Blocking is economically meaningful when the deviators' attainable joint surplus depends on membership/complementarity and cannot be replicated by unilateral exit alone.

This can make cooperative-game analysis useful. It does **not**, by itself, establish an advantage of `s(S)` over `t_i(S)`.

## Payment replication and blocking

If a coalition payment is monetary and its member allocation can be represented by `t_i(S)`, then the target members can be given the same post-policy payoff vector under I2. A blocking coalition compares its attainable deviation payoff with that same vector. Merely changing the label from coalition payment to member-contingent transfers does not alter the blocking inequality.

Thus blocking may be indispensable to describing coalition stability but is **not indispensable to C1's alleged instrument non-equivalence**.

## Primitive-specific checks

- Common asset: blocking may determine who wants access to the asset, but strict non-equivalence arises only if the asset cannot be procured/contracted via money. Then the active primitive is indivisibility/public provision.
- Team production: blocking is secondary to unverifiable contribution/moral hazard.
- Joint liability: group deviation and peer selection matter, but the active primitive is liability/information, with mature prior art.
- Complementary sunk investments: blocking can matter, but `t_i(S)` can condition rewards on joint participation unless incomplete contracting is separately imposed.
- Coalition-specific outside options: blocking is native, yet observed outside options can be offset by member-specific coalition-contingent payments; no I4 advantage follows.

## Verdict

U3 requires that blocking change implementation **relative to individual contingent transfers**, not merely that coalitions can deviate. That requirement fails.

**Blocking is economically meaningful but not indispensable to the claimed I4-over-I2 result.**
