# Reduction Tests

## R1 — Standard R&D coalition with subsidy?

For the generic claim that government support changes stable innovation coalitions: **YES**. Yi & Shin is a direct threat. C1 needed the I4-vs-I2 distinction to escape; that distinction fails.

## R2 — Cost of stability?

For a monetary external payment that stabilizes a target coalition/structure: **substantially yes**. Cost-of-stability theory already allows an external payment conditional on non-deviation and distribution of the payment among members.

## R3 — Why coalition membership rather than individual types?

Coalition-specific complementarity can make composition economically relevant. But relevance of composition does not imply a nonseparable instrument; `t_i(S)` can condition on composition.

## R4 — Why cannot firm-specific subsidies implement the same allocation?

Under the frozen benchmark, **they can** if they condition on the same `S`. This is the fatal test.

## R5 — Does cooperative game theory add economics rather than a solution concept?

Blocking can describe stability, but it does not generate the instrument non-equivalence. Therefore **not enough** for C1.

## R6 — Dynamic subsidy incidence?

Not part of C1; no supplier-to-adopter rescue allowed.

## R7 — Contractual consortium modeled noncooperatively?

Much of the conditional-payment logic can be modeled as multi-agent contracting/coordination. C1 did not identify a cooperative-game-only implementability result.

## R8 — Is government essential?

No for the instrument logic. A private platform/lead firm/funder could condition member payments on the same observed partnership. Government can be justified by welfare externalities, but that is separate from C1 non-equivalence.

## R9 — Is local government essential?

No. The abstract question would be unchanged for a national government. Ehime is a motivating institution, not a necessary theorem primitive.

## R10 — What is the blocking coalition and why meaningful?

Subset deviations can be meaningful when partner complementarities/outside options differ. Yet the same target payoff vector can be generated by `t_i(S)` under the benchmark, leaving the same blocking comparison. Blocking does not rescue I4.

## Strongest referee question

> Why can't government promise each member `i` a transfer `t_i(S)`, payable only if exactly the desired coalition forms?

**Stage-0 answer:** no defensible frozen-C1 reason prevents this. Every proposed reason either changes the instrument (direct public provision) or imports a second mature mechanism (moral hazard, incomplete contracting, joint liability, nontransferable utility).

## Final reduction

C1 is not killed because coalition formation is uninteresting. It is killed because the proposed **policy-instrument distinction is not primitive-invariant**.
