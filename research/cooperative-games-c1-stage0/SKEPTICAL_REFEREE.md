# Skeptical Referee Report

## Recommendation

**Reject C1 before formal modeling.**

## Central objection

The paper's proposed distinction between a coalition-composition grant `s(S)` and member transfers `t_i(S)` is not economically primitive under the maintained monetary/observability benchmark. If the authority observes the same coalition event and may condition each member's payment on that event, any allocation of a monetary coalition grant can be written as a vector of member payments summing to the same public expenditure.

The burden therefore shifts to explaining why member-level claims are infeasible while a coalition-level claim is feasible. The current institution does not supply that restriction: the FY2026 Ehime manufacturing/maritime horizontal-deployment scheme actually identifies a local SME applicant/recipient and conditions eligibility on having an implementation structure with a prior provider.

## Why the obvious rescues fail

1. **Indivisible asset/common infrastructure:** genuine physical nonseparability, but this changes the policy from a monetary grant to direct/in-kind public provision unless joint procurement is itself infeasible.
2. **Team production/unverifiable contribution:** the active mechanism becomes multiagent moral hazard, with canonical prior art.
3. **Joint liability:** the active mechanism becomes group liability/peer selection, also mature.
4. **Complementary sunk investments:** joint-event-contingent payments can reward exact participation unless incomplete contracting is added.
5. **Coalition-specific outside options/blocking:** useful for stability analysis, but the same target payoff vector can be reproduced by contingent member payments.
6. **Collective eligibility:** already representable by individual payments conditional on others' actions; agglomeration-bonus research is particularly close.

## Prior-art pressure

The novelty margin is additionally squeezed by three adjacent literatures:

- cost-of-stability / external coalition-stabilization payments;
- R&D coalition formation and consortium subsidies;
- collective environmental schemes that endogenize coalition formation and, by 2025–2026, explicitly compare individual versus group-level bonuses and membership rules.

## Cooperative-game necessity

Blocking is a legitimate economic object, but C1 needs more: blocking must make I4 implement something I2 cannot. The audit does not find that result. Without it, cooperative-game language changes the stability representation, not the feasible policy set.

## Referee bottom line

Do not build a characteristic-function model around C1. A theorem can be forced only by inserting a new contracting, liability, information or public-input restriction. That would be a new mechanism requiring its own frontier audit, not a refinement of C1.
