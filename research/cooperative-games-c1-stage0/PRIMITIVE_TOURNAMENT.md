## P1 — Common indivisible implementation asset

An asset can genuinely be nonseparable physically. But if `s(S)` is money and coalition members can jointly procure the asset, `t_i(S)` can finance the same purchase. If government directly supplies/owns an indivisible asset, the policy object becomes public/in-kind provision rather than a nonseparable monetary coalition grant.

**Fatal reduction:** change of instrument, not C1 non-equivalence.

## P2 — Team production / unverifiable contribution

Aggregate output may prevent attribution of individual contributions.

**Fatal reduction:** Holmström-style multiagent moral hazard supplies the non-equivalence. Moral hazard/unverifiability is an additional mature mechanism and is prohibited as an ad hoc rescue.

## P3 — Joint liability / collective guarantee

A liability claim can be intrinsically joint and can influence group formation.

**Fatal reduction:** joint-liability/group-lending theory already treats the active mechanism; this is not a property of `s(S)`.

## P4 — Complementary partner-specific sunk investments

Members may invest only when complementary partners join.

**Fatal reduction:** if the joint participation event is observable, `t_i(S)` can be made conditional on the complementary set. A gap requires incomplete contracting/commitment beyond frozen C1.

## P5 — Coalition-specific outside options / blocking

Coalition values and blocking can be genuinely subset-specific.

**Fatal reduction:** subset-specific payoffs make cooperative analysis useful but do not prevent member-specific transfers from reproducing the same target payoff vector when they are conditioned on `S`.

## P6 — Common infrastructure / public input

Infrastructure is naturally shared and sometimes indivisible.

**Fatal reduction:** direct public infrastructure provision is a different industrial-policy instrument. A monetary subsidy to its users remains decomposable if joint procurement is feasible.

## P7 — Collective eligibility / joint performance

Payment occurs only when the desired group/composition performs.

**Fatal reduction:** this is exactly Case B conditionality; I2 already captures it. Agglomeration-bonus work is direct adjacent prior art.

## P8 — Nonseparable common cost / shared procurement

A common fixed cost is incurred once for the coalition.

**Fatal reduction:** nonseparable production cost does not imply nonseparable monetary support. Individual contingent transfers can sum to the required common cost under frictionless joint procurement.

## Tournament conclusion

**No primitive survives.** P1/P6 are the closest to genuine economic nonseparability, but they survive only by changing the object to indivisible public/in-kind provision. P2/P3 survive only by importing separate moral-hazard/liability mechanisms. The remaining primitives do not defeat I2 replication.
