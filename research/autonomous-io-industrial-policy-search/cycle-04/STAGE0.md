# Cycle 4 — Stage 0: Research Question & Primitive Audit

## 1. One-sentence question

Does the timing of a public pilot's verifiable performance signal change whether a potential entrant waits or preempts an incumbent when the selected supplier also accumulates a supplier-specific learning stock, and can the release date therefore change future concentration?

## 2. Motivating institution

Government innovation-procurement guidance describes product demonstrations, public-sector lead-user activity, and forward-commitment procurement as tools for dealing with uncertainty around innovative solutions. A public pilot can therefore create a performance result that is not available before deployment.

The institution motivates a signal generated by public use. It does not establish a universal rule about when the signal must be disclosed.

## 3. General economic primitive

There is one incumbent/selected supplier and one potential entrant. A public pilot produces a verifiable high/low performance signal. The selected supplier's deployment generates a supplier-specific learning advantage for the later private market. The government chooses whether the result is disclosed before or after potential entry.

The signal is public information, not a shared data stock. There are no regions, coalitions, interoperability requirements, or open-access clauses.

## 4. Candidate welfare distortion

- Early disclosure can allow entry in high-quality states but discourage entry in low-quality states.
- Late disclosure can make entrants act on the prior, potentially causing too much or too little entry.
- Public deployment may entrench the selected supplier through learning even when the pilot improves technology quality.
- The government values consumer surplus and technology adoption, while firms value private rents and do not internalize the effect of entry timing on future competition.

## 5. Strategic actors

- Government: chooses pilot and signal release timing.
- Selected incumbent: chooses deployment/learning effort or receives the pilot-generated learning stock.
- Potential entrant: chooses entry timing/entry after observing the signal or the prior.
- Private buyers are passive demand; their surplus enters government welfare.

## 6. Government instrument

Disclosure timing of the public pilot's verifiable performance result, conditional on a public demonstration. No extra subsidy, data-sharing rule, or eligibility rule is assumed.

## 7. Nearest theory families

- Strategic entry timing and incumbent response.
- Learning-by-doing and supplier-specific experience.
- Public procurement for innovation and proof-of-concept demonstrations.
- Public information disclosure and entry thresholds.
- Dynamic procurement and future competition.

## 8. Prohibited rescue

Do not add:

- pooled deployment data or data portability;
- multiple regions, multiple pilots, partition functions, or cross-region spillovers;
- contract bundling or lotting;
- eligibility, subcontracting, vertical integration, or platform governance;
- a second subsidy, patent, exclusivity, or political-economy objective;
- stochastic structure beyond one performance signal and the existing learning stock.

## 9. Fatal null hypothesis

If the public pilot's release date only shifts an entrant's information or fixed cost, the model is a standard public-disclosure/entry-timing model. If the public buyer can be replaced by a private certifier without changing the strategic game, public procurement is decorative. If supplier-specific learning is not necessary, the claimed joint mechanism collapses.

## Stage 0 decision

**GO TO STAGE 1.** The policy lever and strategic timing are clear, and the question is sufficiently different from the killed branches to justify checking whether the public anchor adds more than a standard information disclosure.
