# Cycle 4 — Stage 2: Literature Kill / Frontier Audit

## Search conclusion

**PLAUSIBLE WHITE SPACE — GO TO STAGE 3**

No exact paper was found that combines all of the following in one model: (i) a public demonstration as the source of an observable performance signal, (ii) a chosen release date, (iii) supplier-specific learning from the public deployment, and (iv) an entrant's timing choice that affects future market structure.

This is only plausible white space. Each component is mature, and the public role may disappear under reduction. The branch is admitted to Stage 3 solely to test whether their conjunction creates a necessary strategic mechanism.

## Main prior art

### 1. Chu, Li, Wu & Wu (2025)

[Disruptive Timing](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.01734), *Management Science* 72(7), 5963–5985, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2023.01734.

The paper models an entrant's timing, an incumbent's response timing, and firm-specific learning in disruptive technology competition.

**Threat:** very close on timing plus learning. It does not use a public demonstration or government-controlled signal release, so the public anchor must do real work.

### 2. Hoppe & Schmitz (2013)

[Public-private partnerships versus traditional procurement: Innovation incentives and information gathering](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1756-2171.12010), *RAND Journal of Economics* 44(1), 56–74, DOI 10.1111/1756-2171.12010.

The paper compares procurement forms by their innovation incentives and information-gathering consequences.

**Threat:** procurement can generate information and change innovation incentives; the candidate must not reduce to a procurement-form comparison.

### 3. Chu & Wang (2015)

[Bundled Procurement for Technology Acquisition and Future Competition](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2014.0517), *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management* 17(2), 249–261, DOI 10.1287/msom.2014.0517.

The paper studies current procurement tied to technology acquisition for future competition.

**Threat:** public demand changing future competition is already known; the candidate's only possible novelty is the timing of a public performance signal.

### 4. Aghion, Blundell, Griffith, Howitt & Prantl (2009)

[The Effects of Entry on Incumbent Innovation and Productivity](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037c-8419-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download), *Review of Economics and Statistics* 91(1), 20–32, DOI 10.1162/rest.91.1.20.

Entry can alter incumbent innovation incentives and productivity through competition and reallocation.

**Threat:** an entry/innovation welfare wedge is standard; a public pilot must alter the timing game, not merely the level of entry.

### 5. Sands, Candelon & Gloor (2025)

[Proof-of-concept demonstration and aviation innovation in regional ecosystems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048733325000599), *Research Policy* 54(5), article 105104, DOI 10.1016/j.respol.2025.105104.

The paper provides current evidence connecting proof-of-concept demonstration and innovation activity.

**Threat:** the institutional motivation is credible, but the paper is not a formal theory of release timing and does not itself establish novelty.

## Structural assessment

- The exact combination is not located in the searched canonical and 2024–2026 sources.
- Chu et al. (2025) means the learning/timing core cannot be claimed as new by itself.
- Hoppe–Schmitz and Chu–Wang mean “public procurement creates information and future competition” is not enough.
- The candidate is admitted only because a government-controlled release schedule tied to a public deployment may create a distinct information set that is absent from the closest timing model.
- The next gate is a hostile Stage 3 reduction tournament. Any failure of public necessity or minimality kills the branch.
