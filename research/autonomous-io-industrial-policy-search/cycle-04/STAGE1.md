# Cycle 4 — Stage 1: Source & Institutional Audit

## Primary institutional sources

1. [Northern Ireland Department of Finance, Innovation in Public Procurement (2017)](https://www.finance-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/publications/dfp/PGN-02-17-Innovation-in-Public-Procurement.PDF) describes innovation procurement, product demonstrations, and forward-commitment procurement as ways to address uncertainty and supplier risk.
2. [OECD, Public Procurement for Public Sector Innovation (2024)](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/public-procurement-for-public-sector-innovation_9aad76b7-en.html) treats public procurement as a strategic vehicle for acquiring innovative solutions and managing implementation challenges.
3. [European Commission, public procurement of innovative solutions](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/support-policy-making/shaping-eu-research-and-innovation-policy/new-european-innovation-agenda/innovation-procurement/public-procurement-innovative-solutions_en) confirms public demand as a route for innovative-solution procurement.

## Primitive-by-primitive audit

| primitive | source-supported? | finding |
|---|---|---|
| Public authority can procure an innovative demonstration | yes in policy guidance | The guidance explicitly discusses demonstrations and innovation procurement. |
| Supplier is paid/contracted for the public pilot | yes in ordinary procurement form | Exact payment terms are contract-specific. |
| Pilot yields a verifiable performance result | partially | Demonstration is supported; verifiability and signal design are theoretical abstractions. |
| Government can choose release timing | not universal | A policy design variable to be tested, not an asserted legal entitlement. |
| Selected supplier gets supplier-specific learning | no universal rule | A firm-level learning-by-doing assumption, not an institutional fact. |
| Entrant observes public result before private entry | not universal | Must be stipulated in a program with public disclosure; not inferred from guidance. |
| Private rollout after pilot | possible, not guaranteed | A generic downstream market environment. |
| Public buyer is uniquely able to create the first deployment | not universal | Candidate assumption; if dispensable, the branch fails R11/R13. |

## Timing and roles

A coherent model timing is: government commits to a disclosure schedule; a public pilot is conducted; the selected supplier accumulates experience; the entrant chooses entry relative to the observed or unobserved result; private product-market competition follows.

The sources support the public demonstration and uncertainty context. They do not prove that a particular program uses early/late disclosure as a deliberate competition instrument.

## Stage 1 decision

**GO TO STAGE 2, conditionally.** The institution supports the public-demo primitive, but the release-date instrument is a theoretical extension. Stage 2 must therefore show a non-standard strategic interaction, not merely a public procurement application.
