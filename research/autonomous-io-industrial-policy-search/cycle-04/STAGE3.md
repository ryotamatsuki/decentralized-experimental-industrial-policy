# Cycle 4 — Stage 3: Mechanism Search and Reduction Tournament

## Stage 3 gate

The Stage 2 survivor is frozen as a public-pilot signal-timing question. No data-sharing, regional, coalition, lotting, vertical-integration, platform, or extra-subsidy primitive is admitted.

Six variants were generated. Each is described using the required mechanism fields.

## Variant M4.1 — Immediate disclosure and state-contingent entry

1. **Research question:** Does immediate public disclosure make entry contingent on the realized pilot state?
2. **Primitive:** A public pilot generates a high/low verifiable result disclosed before entry.
3. **Players:** Government, selected incumbent, potential entrant.
4. **Timing:** Policy and pilot; disclosure; entrant entry; private competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Entrant enters after a favorable signal and stays out after an unfavorable signal.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Entry can improve consumer surplus but erode the incumbent's learning rent.
7. **Instrument:** Early disclosure.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** Information filters entry by state.
9. **Welfare intuition:** Filtering may avoid bad entry or exclude socially useful entry.
10. **Closest model:** Public information and entry threshold.
11. **Reduction threat:** This is standard disclosure; public procurement is not necessary.
12. **Generality:** Any verifiable public or private signal source.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** Two states, one entrant, one entry cost.
14. **Candidate proposition:** Early disclosure produces state-contingent entry; late disclosure uses the prior.
15. **Kill condition:** The result remains when the public pilot and learning stock are removed.
16. **Stage 3 status:** Fatal reduction risk; not selected.

## Variant M4.2 — Delayed disclosure and strategic waiting

1. **Research question:** Can delayed disclosure induce an entrant to wait, allowing the incumbent to entrench?
2. **Primitive:** The entrant can enter before or after a public signal; incumbent learning grows with public deployment.
3. **Players:** Government, incumbent, entrant.
4. **Timing:** Government chooses release date; incumbent receives learning; entrant chooses entry time; competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Entrant chooses wait versus preempt; incumbent chooses learning effort.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Waiting preserves option value but may strengthen incumbent market power.
7. **Instrument:** Late versus early release.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** The entrant may wait on an unfavorable prior, but waiting changes the incumbent's response.
9. **Welfare intuition:** Delay may reduce premature entry but worsen concentration.
10. **Closest model:** Chu et al. (2025) strategic disruption timing.
11. **Reduction threat:** The public release date may be only a shift in entry cost or information timing.
12. **Generality:** Any public demonstration with later private-market entry.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** Two firms, one signal, one learning stock, two periods.
14. **Candidate proposition:** The sign of the release-date effect switches at the entrant's entry cutoff.
15. **Kill condition:** No effect remains once the signal is treated as a standard public-information event.
16. **Stage 3 status:** Survives provisionally; mathematical reduction unresolved.

## Variant M4.3 — Performance-threshold release

1. **Research question:** Should a public buyer release a pilot result immediately or only after a performance threshold?
2. **Primitive:** Release is triggered by a verifiable threshold, not a discretionary date.
3. **Players:** Government, incumbent/pilot supplier, entrant.
4. **Timing:** Pilot effort; threshold result; release if passed; entry; private competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Supplier chooses effort knowing that passing the threshold changes entry; entrant responds to release.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Effort creates quality and an information externality, while disclosure changes entry.
7. **Instrument:** Threshold rule.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** A threshold may screen weak states and amplify strong-state entry.
9. **Welfare intuition:** It can avoid wasteful entry but may create a monopoly in unreleased states.
10. **Closest model:** Signaling/test design plus innovation procurement.
11. **Reduction threat:** Threshold disclosure may be ordinary certification or screening.
12. **Generality:** Public trials with milestone-based disclosure.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** Binary effort, binary performance, one entrant, two periods.
14. **Candidate proposition:** The optimal threshold is interior only when the marginal consumer gain from entry differs by state.
15. **Kill condition:** Threshold is merely an additional screening instrument; no public-anchor-specific result.
16. **Stage 3 status:** Survives provisionally but is less minimal than M4.4.

## Variant M4.4 — Public-anchor learning and entry timing (TOP 1)

1. **Research question:** Can a public anchor deployment give the selected supplier a learning stock while the release timing of the result changes entrant timing and future concentration?
2. **Primitive:** One public pilot creates a supplier-specific learning advantage and a verifiable public signal; the government controls whether the signal is observed before entry.
3. **Players:** Government, selected incumbent, potential entrant.
4. **Timing:** Government commits to early/late release; the selected supplier deploys/learns; the entrant chooses entry relative to the signal; private competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Incumbent chooses learning effort; entrant chooses enter/wait; government chooses release timing.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Incumbent does not internalize the effect of its learning on entrant entry and consumer surplus; entrant does not internalize future competition.
7. **Instrument:** Release timing conditional on a public pilot.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** Early release can filter entry by signal, while late release can induce entry on the prior; incumbent learning shifts the entry cutoff.
9. **Welfare intuition:** The government trades faster information and adoption against excessive entrenchment or premature entry.
10. **Closest model:** Chu et al. (2025) strategic timing/learning, plus Hoppe–Schmitz (2013) procurement information.
11. **Reduction threat:** The whole effect may reduce to standard public disclosure plus entry deterrence; the public buyer may be only a signal source.
12. **Generality:** Any public innovation demonstration that creates a first deployment and a verifiable performance result.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** Three player types, one signal, one learning stock, two private periods, one policy choice.
14. **Candidate proposition:** Holding the pilot's expected quality fixed, changing release timing can reverse the direction of entry relative to the prior when the entry cost lies between state-contingent and prior expected profits.
15. **Kill condition:** The timing effect survives without supplier-specific learning or public demand, or the closest standard disclosure model reproduces it.
16. **Stage 3 status:** TOP 1; **CONDITIONAL GO TO STAGE 4**.

## Variant M4.5 — Multi-supplier pilot portfolio

1. **Research question:** Does funding several pilots rather than one preserve future competition?
2. **Primitive:** Multiple suppliers receive pilot deployments and generate signals.
3. **Players:** Government, multiple pilot suppliers, entrants/adopters.
4. **Timing:** Portfolio choice; parallel pilots; signals; private competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Supplier entry and pilot effort.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Portfolio diversity versus duplication and budget cost.
7. **Instrument:** Number of pilot winners.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** More winners can prevent lock-in.
9. **Welfare intuition:** Portfolio choice trades diversity for cost.
10. **Closest model:** Multi-sourcing, procurement portfolios, and innovation contests.
11. **Reduction threat:** Standard procurement/multi-sourcing; too many strategic types.
12. **Generality:** Innovation procurement portfolios.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** At least two pilot suppliers, already an extra primitive.
14. **Candidate proposition:** A second pilot is valuable when the first supplier's learning advantage is strong.
15. **Kill condition:** The result is a multi-sourcing/portfolio theorem.
16. **Stage 3 status:** KILL; not minimal and fatal under R5/R14/R15.

## Variant M4.6 — Public result followed by private rollout

1. **Research question:** Does public disclosure after the pilot change competition in the private rollout market?
2. **Primitive:** Public pilot result becomes a market-wide signal before private adoption.
3. **Players:** Government, pilot supplier, entrant.
4. **Timing:** Pilot; public result; private entry and competition.
5. **Strategic action:** Entry after public information.
6. **Externality/distortion:** Signal changes private market entry and prices.
7. **Instrument:** Public disclosure.
8. **Equilibrium intuition:** Good news draws entry; bad news deters it.
9. **Welfare intuition:** Disclosure can improve matching but changes concentration.
10. **Closest model:** Certification, lead-market demand, and entry.
11. **Reduction threat:** No supplier-specific learning or strategic timing is needed.
12. **Generality:** Any public certification.
13. **Minimum assumptions:** One signal and one entrant.
14. **Candidate proposition:** Disclosure can increase or decrease entry depending on the cutoff.
15. **Kill condition:** The effect is present without public deployment or incumbent learning.
16. **Stage 3 status:** KILL as a standalone theme; it is a standard information/entry model.

## S3-A–S3-H test for TOP 1

- **S3-G Theorem potential:** yes. A threshold partition separates prior-based entry from state-contingent entry; learning shifts the threshold.
