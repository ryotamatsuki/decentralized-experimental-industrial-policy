# Cycle 3 — Stage 0: Research Question & Primitive Audit

## 1. One-sentence question

When a public innovation procurement permits reliance on another entity's capacity but requires critical implementation tasks to be performed directly by the prime bidder, can the eligibility rule induce vertical integration rather than subcontracting and thereby change entry, foreclosure, and innovation?

## 2. Motivating institution

Public procurement law commonly allows groups of operators and reliance on the capacity of other entities, while also allowing conditions that critical tasks be performed directly by the tenderer or a participant in the group. This creates a concrete eligibility/organizational-boundary contrast.

The institution motivates the question; it does not imply that every contracting authority uses a direct-performance requirement.

## 3. General economic primitive

An upstream innovator can serve the public contract by:

1. vertically integrating the implementation stage;
2. contracting with an independent implementation supplier;
3. forming a temporary group or relying on another entity's capacity.

A direct-performance requirement changes which organizational forms can satisfy eligibility. The downstream market then faces a possible reduction in independent supplier entry or an integrated firm's foreclosure incentive.

## 4. Candidate welfare distortion

- Direct performance can improve accountability and coordination for critical tasks.
- It can exclude specialist entrants and force inefficient integration.
- An integrated prime may gain a strategic input advantage and foreclose independent complementors.
- Conversely, allowing subcontracting can increase entry but may create information, quality, or hold-up costs.

## 5. Strategic actors

- Government: specifies eligibility/direct-performance condition.
- Prime innovator: chooses integration, subcontracting, or a group.
- Independent implementation supplier: decides whether to enter/contract.
- Downstream adopters are passive in the minimal candidate; their surplus is used in welfare.

## 6. Government instrument

Eligibility and contract-performance rule: reliance on outside capacity allowed versus specified critical tasks performed directly.

## 7. Nearest theory families

- Vertical integration and make-or-buy.
- Public-private partnership ownership and task bundling.
- Public procurement entry and subcontracting.
- Procurement qualification and bidder participation.
- Foreclosure in competing ecosystems.
- Transaction-cost and incomplete-contract theories.

## 8. Prohibited rescue

Do not add:

- coalition-transfer or core-stability machinery;
- multi-site scalability, shared implementation finance, or C1/A1 primitives;
- data ownership, interoperability, or platform governance;
- local-content protection or place-based targeting;
- extra subsidies to one side;
- an exogenous synergy chosen only to make integration novel.

## 9. Fatal null hypothesis

An eligibility rule that changes whether a prime can use a subcontractor is a standard make-or-buy or subcontracting requirement. Any resulting entry/foreclosure trade-off is already vertical-organization theory or procurement-auction theory.

## Stage 0 decision

**GO TO STAGE 1.** The question has a concrete rule and a distinct organizational margin, so it merits an institutional check before the strong vertical-integration prior is applied.
