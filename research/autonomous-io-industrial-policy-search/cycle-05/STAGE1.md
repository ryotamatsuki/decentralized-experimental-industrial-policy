# Cycle 05 — Stage 1: Source and Institutional Audit

## Institutional facts verified

The Finance Northern Ireland procurement guidance describes Forward Commitment
Procurement as providing advance information about future needs in outcome
terms, early engagement with potential suppliers, and—most importantly—an
agreement to purchase a product or service that does not currently exist at a
specified future date if performance and cost conditions are met:

https://www.finance-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/publications/dfp/PGN-02-17-Innovation-in-Public-Procurement.PDF

The OECD's public-sector innovation procurement material treats procurement as
a demand-side innovation instrument and discusses public buyers' use of
procurement to stimulate innovative supply:

https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/public-procurement-for-public-sector-innovation_9aad76b7-en.html

The OECD's 2026 report confirms that public procurement is used as an
industrial-policy tool to develop domestic production, infant industries,
employment, regional development, and industrial resilience:

https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/06/public-procurement-trade-and-industrial-policies_e9b7ed1d.html

## Applicant, beneficiary, and payment

The guidance supports a public buyer communicating demand and potentially
agreeing to a future purchase. It does not establish a universal rule that a
particular named firm is selected, that an unconditional payment is made
upfront, or that the initial supplier is guaranteed a later monopoly. Those
features cannot be treated as institutional facts.

Accordingly, the theory must distinguish:

1. the documented FCP demand/commitment practice; and
2. the proposed theoretical consequences of irreversible investment and a
   later re-tender.

## Timing and contractual structure

Documented timing: future need is communicated before the product exists, with
early engagement and a future purchase conditional on performance and cost.

Not documented as universal: exact contract duration, renewal rights, the
identity of later bidders, a mandatory re-tender, or an exclusive award.

These are admissible theoretical choices only if kept explicit and minimal.

## Stage-1 decision

**GO TO STAGE 2.**

There is no fatal institutional contradiction, but the strongest proposed
market-structure claim—initial-supplier lock-in against later re-tender entry—
is theory-only rather than a verified universal feature of FCP.
