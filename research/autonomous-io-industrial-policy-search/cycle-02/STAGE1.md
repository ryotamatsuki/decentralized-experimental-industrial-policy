# Cycle 2 — Stage 1: Source & Institutional Audit

## Primary institutional sources

1. [OECD, Enhanced Access to Publicly Funded Data for Science, Technology and Innovation (2020)](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/enhanced-access-to-publicly-funded-data-for-science-technology-and-innovation_947717bc-en.html) treats access to publicly funded data as an innovation-policy issue and identifies the need to balance access with privacy, intellectual property, security, and public interest.
2. [Public procurement of open data-driven innovation guide](https://www.iprocurenet.eu/wp-content/uploads/guide_of_public_procurement_of_open_data-driven_innovation.pdf) describes public procurement using open-data conditions, private access, open-data licences, FAIR principles, open standards, and compatibility with installed bases.
3. [EU public procurement of innovative solutions](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/support-policy-making/shaping-eu-research-and-innovation-policy/new-european-innovation-agenda/innovation-procurement/public-procurement-innovative-solutions_en) confirms public demand as a route for acquiring innovative solutions.

## Primitive-by-primitive audit

| primitive | source-supported? | finding |
|---|---|---|
| Public support can attach data-access conditions | partially | Public-data and open-data procurement guidance supports the policy family; no universal rule is assumed. |
| Deployment generates operating data | context-dependent | Verified for data-intensive technologies as a theoretical/sectoral primitive, not a universal procurement fact. |
| Data are non-rival and reusable | theory-supported | Explicitly treated as the economic reason for access policy; data governance constraints remain separate. |
| Equal access / portability can be required | partially | Open-data licences, open standards, and access guidance support the instrument; implementation varies by program. |
| Payment recipient | yes for procurement/grant contract | The policy can condition a supplier's public contract or support. |
| Downstream rival access | not universal | A model assumption to be tied to a program's data clause, not an institutional fact about Triangle Ehime. |
| Capacity/deployment investment | no universal rule | Must be modeled as a firm response. |
| Product-market competition after deployment | no | A general IO environment, not a rule of the institution. |

## Timing

The public condition is set before deployment; firms choose deployment/capacity; data are generated through use; downstream competition follows. The sequence is economically coherent, but only the policy condition and public funding context are source-backed.

## Stage 1 decision

**GO TO STAGE 2, conditionally on separating institution from theory.** The institution supports a data-access policy family, but it does not establish the claimed competition effect. The literature audit must decide whether this distinction is substantive or merely an application label.
