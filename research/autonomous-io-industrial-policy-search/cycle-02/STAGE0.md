# Cycle 2 — Stage 0: Research Question & Primitive Audit

## 1. One-sentence question

When public support causes technology deployment to generate reusable operating data, should the government require open access or portability for qualified rivals, given that openness can intensify downstream competition but weaken firms' incentives to deploy and learn?

## 2. Motivating institution

Public R&D and innovation programs increasingly address data access, data governance, and deployment of digital or industrial technologies. Public procurement can also specify data-access, licensing, and interoperability conditions.

The motivating institution is a generic publicly supported deployment program. It is not a claim that all local programs impose data-sharing duties.

## 3. General economic primitive

A deployed technology generates a non-rival operating-data stock that improves future productivity or product quality. A government can condition support on non-discriminatory access, portability, or a shared data standard. Firms compete in a later product market and choose early deployment/capacity.

This is deliberately different from C5's partition-contingent diffusion and from C6's evidence/test design: the proposed object is the strategic interaction between data access and productive deployment capacity.

## 4. Candidate welfare distortion

- Open access may reduce data hoarding and increase downstream competition.
- The same openness can reduce the private return to deployment and future data production.
- A private equilibrium may underprovide deployment data or protect a less efficient incumbent.
- A government can face a competition-versus-learning trade-off, not merely a static subsidy incidence question.

## 5. Strategic actors

- Government: sets the data-access condition attached to support.
- Deployed technology firms: choose irreversible capacity/deployment and data-use strategy.
- Downstream competitors: decide entry/output using the data.
- Adopters are represented by deployment demand and are not a separate strategic type in the minimal candidate.

## 6. Government instrument

Eligibility or contract-performance condition: open data access, portability, or equal treatment of downstream users. No second instrument is assumed.

## 7. Nearest theory families

- Learning-by-doing and learning-by-deploying.
- Data sharing and competition.
- Data neutrality and platform data supply.
- Open versus closed technology and supplier investment.
- Network effects and data-enabled competitive advantage.
- Publicly funded data access and procurement licensing.

## 8. Prohibited rescue

Do not add:

- coalition formation or portability as a renamed C7;
- multiple regions or partition functions as a renamed C5;
- private information/test design as a renamed C6;
- local-information advantages, decentralized experimentation, or A1's multi-site scalability;
- a platform intermediary, standards body, or extra subsidy;
- privacy, political economy, or enforcement frictions unless they are a new question.

## 9. Fatal null hypothesis

The proposed result is already the standard open-versus-closed data/technology trade-off: data access raises rivals' productivity or competition but reduces the data producer's incentive to invest. Public support changes the application label but not the game.

## Stage 0 decision

**GO TO STAGE 1.** The question has a genuine policy lever and a clear welfare wedge, but the null is powerful and recent literature must be checked before any mechanism work.
