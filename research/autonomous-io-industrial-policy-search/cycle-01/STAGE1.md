# Cycle 1 — Stage 1: Source & Institutional Audit

## Primary institutional sources

1. [Directive 2014/24/EU, Article 46](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32014L0024) permits separate lots, requires reasons when a contract is not subdivided, allows authorities to state whether tenders may cover one/several/all lots, and permits limits on the number of lots awarded to one tenderer.
2. [OECD, Public Procurement, Trade and Industrial Policies (2026)](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/06/public-procurement-trade-and-industrial-policies_e9b7ed1d.html) describes public procurement as a large economic activity increasingly used for strategic industrial-policy goals.
3. [European Commission, public procurement of innovative solutions](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/support-policy-making/shaping-eu-research-and-innovation-policy/new-european-innovation-agenda/innovation-procurement/public-procurement-innovative-solutions_en) confirms the use of public demand to procure innovative solutions.

## Primitive-by-primitive audit

| primitive | source-supported? | finding |
|---|---|---|
| Public buyer chooses contract scope / lotting | yes | Article 46 directly supports it. |
| Applicant is an economic operator or group of operators | yes | Directive 2014/24 contains group-of-operators and reliance-on-capacities provisions. |
| Beneficiary / payment recipient is the awarded contractor(s) | yes in ordinary public-contract form | The rule is a procurement contract, not a general transfer to every complementor. |
| Separate specialists can bid for parts | yes as a legal possibility | The rule permits separate lots; actual supplier capability is market-specific. |
| Interoperability between lots | no | It is a modeling assumption, not established by Article 46. |
| Post-award complementor entry | no | Not an institutional requirement; it must be modeled separately. |
| Future private-market concentration | no | A possible economic consequence, not a verified rule. |
| Scope economies / coordination costs | no | Theoretical primitives, not institutional facts. |
| Government objective includes future competition | not as a universal rule | A normative industrial-policy objective to be stated, not attributed to every contracting authority. |

## Timing

The legal rule governs procurement design before bids. The post-award private-market phase is not part of the verified rule. It can motivate theory only if the model clearly labels it as an extension.

## Stage 1 decision

**GO TO STAGE 2, with a material caveat.** The policy instrument exists. The proposed post-award market-structure channel is not institutionally automatic, so the novelty test must be purely theoretical and cannot rely on “the institution requires interoperability or complementor entry.”
