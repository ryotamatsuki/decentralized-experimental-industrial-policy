# Cycle 1 — Stage 2: Literature Kill / Frontier Audit

## Search conclusion

**HIGH OVERLAP / STRUCTURAL CONTAINMENT — NO-GO AT STAGE 2**

The core question is already covered by procurement-bundling and contract-scope theory. Adding the language of complementors, innovation systems, or future concentration does not create a new strategic margin.

## Main prior art

### 1. Li, Sun, Yan & Yu (2015)

[Bundling decisions in procurement auctions with sequential tasks](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pubeco/v128y2015icp96-106.html), *Journal of Public Economics* 128, 96–106, DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.09.012.

The paper studies a principal's bundling decision for sequential tasks with task externalities and sequential information. It already connects bundling, the number of bidders for a task, and the number of consortia able to perform both tasks.

**Classification:** structural containment; the proposed complementary-task mechanism is a direct generalization at most.

### 2. Chen & Li (2018)

[Bundled procurement](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pubeco/v159y2018icp116-127.html), *Journal of Public Economics* 159, 116–127, DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2018.02.004.

The paper compares separate purchase, pure bundling, and mixed bundling when a buyer procures multiple products from competing sellers, with the optimal form depending on seller count and trade efficiency.

**Classification:** structural containment; the buyer's contract-architecture choice is already the object of analysis.

### 3. Buso (2019)

[Bundling versus unbundling: asymmetric information on information externalities](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jeczfn/v128y2019i1d10.1007_s00712-018-0642-0.html), *Journal of Economics* 128(1), 1–25, DOI 10.1007/s00712-018-0642-0.

This paper studies bundling two sequential PPP activities when a source-side externality is privately known, showing how bundling can reveal information about the externality.

**Classification:** primitive overlap and result overlap; task interdependence plus public contract form is already present.

### 4. Giosa (2018)

[Division of public contracts into lots and bid rigging](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/division-of-public-contracts-into-lots-and-bid-rigging-can-econom/), *European Procurement and Public Private Partnership Law Review* 13(1), 30–38, DOI 10.21552/epppl/2018/1/6.

The paper directly treats lot division as a competition and entry instrument and discusses the interaction with incumbents, entrants, and bid rigging.

**Classification:** exact policy-object overlap for the proposed competition effect.

### 5. Chu & Wang (2015)

[Bundled Procurement for Technology Acquisition and Future Competition](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2014.0517), *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management* 17(2), 249–261, DOI 10.1287/msom.2014.0517.

This paper explicitly studies a buyer bundling a current procurement project with technology acquisition in order to compete in a future market. It has the proposed “current public procurement changes future competition” logic already.

**Classification:** exact prior-art threat to the future-market/concentration version.

### 6. Chiappinelli, Giuffrida & Spagnolo (2025)

[Public procurement as an innovation policy: Where do we stand?](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/indorg/v100y2025ics0167718725000244.html), *International Journal of Industrial Organization* 100, article 103157, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2025.103157.

This recent survey identifies bundling versus unbundling as an established PPI design issue, making an application-only innovation claim especially vulnerable.

**Classification:** frontier map confirms the branch is not an unoccupied policy question.

## Kill tests

- **Exact prior art:** Chu & Wang is close enough on procurement changing future competition to block an application-only claim.
- **Structural containment:** Li et al., Chen & Li, and Buso cover the principal's bundling decision with complementary/sequential tasks, information, and scope effects.
- **Result overlap:** “unbundling raises entry but may sacrifice coordination” is already an established comparative-static pattern.
- **Novelty by terminology:** calling specialist bidders “complementors” does not change the game.
- **Post-award extension:** adding a downstream competition phase is a natural continuation, not a distinct mechanism unless a new state variable changes incentives; no such variable is allowed in this branch.

## Stage 2 decision

**NO-GO.** The branch is killed before Stage 3. It cannot be rescued by adding interoperability, a second procurement stage, data rights, local eligibility, or a coalition contract. Those would be new questions, not repairs.
