# Industrial Policy / Self-Discovery Scout

## Verdict

Generic private discovery followed by imitation is not a viable main contribution.

## Closest prior art

- Hausmann & Rodrik (2003): private agents discover profitable activities under uncertainty; imitation limits appropriability; ex-ante discovery is underprovided.
- Acemoglu, Bimpikis & Ozdaglar (2011): private firms strategically choose experimentation timing when success can be copied; appropriation rules change experimentation incentives.
- Juhász, Lane & Rodrik (2024): current industrial-policy framework makes information, coordination, governance and implementation standard policy rationales.

## Kill result

The project cannot claim novelty merely because a prefecture rather than a firm pays for discovery. It also cannot claim a new industrial-policy rationale from “success produces information that others use.”

## Surviving question

A supplier with a national downstream market may internalize some of the informational/commercial value of a local trial while the host government internalizes only local early-use benefits and follower jurisdictions internalize later adoption benefits. The incidence of these benefits may change whether local subsidies create an experiment or merely redistribute the location/rent of an experiment that would occur anyway.

Classification: `POTENTIALLY NOVEL / REQUIRES STAGE 3`.
