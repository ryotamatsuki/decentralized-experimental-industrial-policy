# Innovation / Technology-Adoption Scout

## Verdict

Early-adopter cost, follower learning, demonstration effects, and commercialization are established mechanisms. A viable contribution needs a new ownership/incidence feedback.

## Closest prior art

- Detragiache (1998): early adopters pay adaptation cost and create positive externalities for followers.
- Acemoglu, Bimpikis & Ozdaglar (2011): experimentation timing and copying/appropriation.
- Brown & Hendry (2009) and later demonstration literature: public field trials can generate learning and accelerate commercialization/diffusion.
- Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen (2013): technology spillovers and product-market business stealing are opposing forces.

## Kill result

The following are not new by themselves:

- pioneer bears implementation/adaptation cost;
- follower learns from pioneer;
- public demonstration reduces uncertainty;
- supplier obtains reputation/market expansion after successful demonstration;
- positive spillovers can oppose first-mover/business-stealing rents.

## Surviving mechanism families for Stage 3

1. **Supplier co-financing:** downstream commercialization makes the supplier willing to absorb trial cost, changing optimal public subsidy incidence.
2. **Local-fit × reusable supplier learning:** the trial can produce supplier-owned adaptation know-how that travels differently from public evidence.
3. **Success-biased demonstration design:** downstream certification value can make supplier and sponsor prefer a visible-success trial over a socially informative trial.
4. **Scarce launch-trial location:** one necessary launch trial can be hosted in only one jurisdiction, separating experiment occurrence from host selection.

These require targeted mechanism-level comparison rather than an additive model.
