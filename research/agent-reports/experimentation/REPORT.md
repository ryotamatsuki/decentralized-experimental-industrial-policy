# Policy Experimentation Scout

## Verdict

Cross-jurisdiction learning, waiting, free riding, diverse experimentation, and centralization/decentralization are already deeply developed. The project must not present these as new.

## Closest prior art

- Bolton & Harris (1999) and Keller, Rady & Cripps (2005): information from experiments is a public good; strategic free riding distorts experimentation.
- Callander & Harstad (2015): jurisdictions choose whether/what to experiment, learn from each other, and strategically distort experiment choice; centralization, transparency, prizes and IPR matter.
- Cai & Treisman (2009): centralization can create too much experimentation while decentralization creates too little.
- Cheng & Li (2019): decentralized policy experimentation and learning under political career concerns.

## Mandatory kill

“A pioneer pays/risks failure and followers wait for the successful result” is `KILLED AS NOVELTY CLAIM`.

## Surviving Stage 3 direction

An experiment may be conducted by a private supplier whose downstream commercialization payoff is partly generated by the evidence. This can change the **extensive margin** (whether an experiment is privately worthwhile) independently from the **location margin** (which jurisdiction hosts it). A local subsidy race can therefore exist even when the experimentation externality does not justify a subsidy at all.

This distinction must be made endogenous in Stage 4 if selected; otherwise it collapses to existing federal experimentation.
