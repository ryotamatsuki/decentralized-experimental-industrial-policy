# Regional / Place-Based Policy Scout

## Verdict

A regional label does not create novelty. Place-based policy already supplies rich theory for local externalities, business stealing, agglomeration, mobility and spatial redistribution.

## Boundary literature

- Fajgelbaum & Gaubert (2025): current theory benchmark for place-based policies.
- Agrawal, Hoyt & Wilson (2022): local policy competition and firm attraction.
- Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen (2013): technology spillovers versus product-market rivalry.
- modern regional-innovation/smart-specialisation work provides institutional context but is not itself a new strategic mechanism.

## Kill result

Do not claim a contribution from:

- a local subsidy causing local productivity gains;
- a first-mover region gaining firms/jobs/ecosystem benefits;
- another region losing activity through business stealing;
- central government internalizing interregional externalities;
- local-retention conditions attached to subsidies.

## Surviving spatial angle

The promising distinction is that the scarce object being attracted is an **experiment/launch location that produces information and supplier commercialization value**, not simply a permanent firm or fixed amount of activity. The host-only benefit may be temporary while the supplier's market and information effects are national. Thus local bidding can become socially wasteful even when the underlying experiment is socially valuable.

This spatial distinction remains `POTENTIALLY NOVEL`; Stage 3 must show an endogenous effect that disappears in a standard firm-location subsidy model.
