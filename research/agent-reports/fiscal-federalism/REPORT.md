# Fiscal Federalism / Intergovernmental Competition Scout

## Verdict

Generic local-information-versus-spillover and local-subsidy-competition mechanisms are crowded prior art.

## Closest prior art

- Besley & Coate (2003): centralization/decentralization under spillovers, heterogeneity, common financing and political allocation.
- Lockwood (2002): heterogeneous regional projects and distributive costs of centralization.
- Agrawal, Hoyt & Wilson (2022): local policy choice, fiscal competition, bidding for firms, expenditure spillovers and higher-level constraints.
- Callander & Harstad (2015), Cai & Treisman (2009), Cheng & Li (2019): experimentation under alternative government structures.

## Kill result

The following are insufficient as main contributions:

- “local governments know local needs better”;
- “national government internalizes cross-region spillovers”;
- “matching grants combine local information with national finance”;
- “prefectures may overbid to attract firms/technology.”

## Surviving question

A region may bid not for a permanently mobile firm, but for the **location of a scarce information-producing launch trial** whose supplier captures national commercialization value and whose evidence benefits non-host regions. If local support changes only where the trial occurs, not whether it occurs, local industrial policy can be pure rent/location competition; if follower information benefits are large and the supplier plus host do not cover trial cost, the same decentralized system can underprovide the trial.

Classification: `POTENTIALLY NOVEL / REQUIRES STAGE 3`.
