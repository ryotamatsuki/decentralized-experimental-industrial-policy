## 4. Phenomenon versus explanation

### Observed phenomenon, without explanation

Subnational governments operate or consider programs in which public resources and local implementation capacity are used to select and support field trials or adoption of technologies supplied by private firms, while successful technologies or evidence may later travel beyond the sponsoring jurisdiction.

### Proposed explanations, kept separate

1. local governments may select projects using information unavailable or costly to aggregate centrally;
2. trial evidence may spill across jurisdictions and invite waiting or free riding;
3. early adoption may create firm-, region-, or supplier-level rents;
4. a technology supplier may value a local trial because it changes commercialization elsewhere;
5. jurisdictional competition may induce duplication, racing, or rent capture;
6. national finance with local selection may trade off information against fiscal externalities;
7. local government may solve a matching or coordination failure rather than merely subsidize cost;
8. state capacity or capture may reverse the informational benefits of decentralization.

None is accepted as the explanation at Stage 0.

## 6. Candidate mechanisms

| ID | Candidate mechanism | Strategic feedback loop | Main kill risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Local information × project selection | local information improves selection, which changes the value and allocation of experimentation; centralization pools spillovers but may lose selection precision | assumption-driven decentralization result |
| M2 | Information-production externality | a local trial produces evidence used elsewhere, reducing the pioneer's return and changing who experiments first | standard federal-experimentation result |
| M3 | First-mover rent × free riding | diffusion creates social information value but erodes the pioneer's local rent; the balance may generate waiting or racing | rents inserted to force a sign reversal |
| M4 | Government–adopter–supplier triangle | support changes adopter participation and supplier commercialization effort; the supplier's downstream gain feeds back into the subsidy or contract needed locally | extra player with no new interaction |
| M5 | Intergovernmental experimentation race | jurisdictions compete for temporary local rents or political credit while learning from each other's trials, creating duplication or strategic delay | ordinary subsidy competition |
| M6 | National finance × decentralized selection | local selection uses local information but external finance weakens fiscal discipline or encourages duplication; national rules may alter evidence production | standard common-pool/fiscal-federalism trade-off |
| M7 | Government as matching/coordination platform | public matching affects which supplier–adopter pair forms; successful matches reveal information and reshape later matching/commercialization | descriptive platform relabeling |
| M8 | Capacity/capture reversal | decentralization improves information only when capacity is sufficient; capture changes selection and supplier targeting, potentially reversing the ranking | heterogeneity added without a strategic loop |

These are alternatives, not ingredients for one model.
